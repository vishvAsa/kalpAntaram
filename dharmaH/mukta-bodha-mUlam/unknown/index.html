    • description: RE30577 Āśaucanirṇayaḥ. The bundle, entitled Āśaucanirṇayaḥ, contains two main sections that address ritual discipline and devotional practice. The first section, which constitutes the majority of the bundle, is entitled Āśaucanirṇaya and discusses the niyama (rules) and ācāra (codes of conduct) to be observed in states of ritual impurity (āśauca). It outlines the prescribed regulations for purity, observance, and daily conduct. Composed in the Maṇipravāḷa style, this section employs a mixed use of Grantha and Tamil scripts, reflecting a traditional South Indian exegetical method. The second section comprises the complete Śrīlalitāsahasranāmāvalī, a celebrated devotional text central to Śākta worship. The codex consists of 61 folios, each measuring 2.4 cm × 30.2 cm, written in 7–8 lines per folio. The text is complete and well structured, and its combination of prescriptive ritual discourse with foundational devotional hymns makes it a valuable source for understanding both normative religious practice and Śrīvidyā-oriented devotion.

    • description: RE31378 Astottarasatanamani. This bundle, entitled Devyxadaṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalī, is a compact yet extensive collection of Aṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalīs (lists of 108 sacred names) dedicated to a wide range of deities. The contents include Śivāṣṭottaraśatam, Vināyakāṣṭottaraśatam, Ṣoḍaśagaṇapatināmāni (the sixteen forms of Gaṇapati), Vallīdevasenāsameta Śrīsubrahmaṇyāṣṭottaraśatam, Devyaṣṭottaraśatam, Durgāparameśvaryaṣṭottaraśatam, Lakṣmyaṣṭottaraśatanāmāvalī, Sarasvatyaṣṭottaraśatam, Ramāṣṭottaraśatam, Rāmāṣṭottaraśatam, Kṛṣṇāṣṭottaraśatam, Narasimhāṣṭottaraśatam, Viṣṇvaṣṭottaraśatam, Hariharāṣṭottaraśatam, Saptarṣiṛṣyxadaṣṭottaraśatam, and Tripurasundaryxadaṣṭottaraśatam. The manuscript thus reflects a broad devotional spectrum encompassing Śaiva, Śākta, Vaiṣṇava, and Saura traditions. The bundle consists of 43 folios, each measuring 11 cm × 1.5 cm, written in 7 lines per folio, arranged in 3–5 columns. Despite its small physical format, the manuscript preserves a rich liturgical collection intended for arcana and daily worship, making it an important source for understanding multi-deity devotional practices and name-recitation traditions.

    • catalog number: I00052
    • description: RE32402 Devatādhyānavacanāni. The bundle RE32402, which is titled Devatādhyānavacanāni, is a text that focuses on the art of Dhyana (meditation), which in the Vedic and Agamic traditions involves the precise mental reconstruction of a deity’s form to invite their presence into a ritual space. The text begins with the fundamental elements of ritual preparation. One of its more practical and unique sections concerns the Nālikera (coconut). The coconut is treated not merely as an offering but as a ritual instrument whose physical characteristics—shape, water content, and the pattern of lines on its shell—are analysed to determine the success or failure of a ceremony. This is followed by a profound visualisation of the Yāgaśālā (sacrificial hall). The manual instructs the practitioner to view the physical architecture of the hall as a macrocosmic body. Every pillar, gate, and altar corresponds to a specific anatomical or spiritual limb of a divine entity, transforming the physical structure into a living, breathing sacred space. It is a core point that is a vast catalogue of Dhyāna Ślokas. These verses provide the "blueprint" for a deity’s appearance. For a ritual to be effective, the practitioner must visualise the deity with exacting detail, including: (1) Attributes and Weapons - The text lists specific items held in various hands, such as the Pāśa (noose), Aṅkuśa (goad), Triśūla (trident), and Padma (lotus). (2) Vāhanas (Mounts) - It describes the vehicles of the gods, such as the seven horses of Sūrya (the Sun) or the vulture associated with Śani (Saturn). (3) The Navagrahas - A significant portion of the text is dedicated to the Nine Planets. It details not just their appearance but also their astrological "ID cards"—their Gotra (lineage), Nakshatra (birth star), and the specific direction they must face during worship to balance cosmic energies. The text is inclusive of multiple sectarian traditions, featuring deities from the Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Shakta lineages. (1) Shaiva: Includes descriptions of Sadāśiva, the five-faced form of Shiva, and Dakṣiṇāmūrti, the silent teacher. (2) Shakta: Provides vivid imagery for forms of the Goddess, such as Durgā (described in relation to the buffalo demon Mahishasura) and Bhāgyalakṣmī, who represents various forms of prosperity like Vīralakṣmī (valour) and Śauryalakṣmī (bravery). (3) Ancestral Rites: Toward the end, the text shifts to Pitṛ-Karma (ancestral rites). It contains moving passages regarding the offering of Piṇḍa (food oblations) to ancestors from both the maternal and paternal lines, as well as to "forgotten" ancestors or those who died without children to perform their rites. The document is a transcription of a physical manuscript, likely palm-leaf (Grantha or Devanagari script). The transcriber has meticulously noted the original folio numbers (e.g., P. 4b, P. 57a) and used a system of brackets and dots to indicate areas where the original material was damaged or illegible. This suggests the text is part of a preservation effort to digitise or record fragile traditional knowledge. The text is incomplete, and the size is 3.4x26.5 cm.     • description: Bundle RE31139, entitled "stotrasaṅgrahaḥ", comprises two main sections, combining ritual practice and devotional hymnology. The first section is devoted to Ātmārthapūjā and details preparatory and worship procedures, including Astrasandhi, Śivasandhi, and Śivasūryapūjā. It also provides the complete Ātmārthapūjā sequence, which reflects personal worship performed for one’s own spiritual benefit. The second section is a collection of stotras primarily dedicated to Lord Śiva. This includes Sadāśivāṣṭakam, Hālāsyanāthāṣṭakam, Liṅgāṣṭakam, Bhairavāṣṭakam, Śrīsthalīśvarāṣṭakam, Śivapañcākṣarastotram, Bhujaṅgapriyākalpakam, and Kṣetrapālavāṣṭakam, as well as several other hymns. These illustrate the rich devotional and theological dimensions of Śaiva worship. The manuscript consists of 55 folios, each measuring approximately 150 mm × 303 mm and containing 6–12 lines of text. By combining practical ritual manuals with a wide range of Śaivastotra literature, this collection is an important source for understanding the liturgical procedures and devotional expressions central to the Śaiva tradition.
