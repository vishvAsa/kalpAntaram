Most of you people will shit your pants if you have to live with Orthodox Bengali Hindu families like mine

These are the rules in my home:

- Showering every time you take a shit, we keep separate wear to go take a shit

- There are literally separate mugs for showering and taking a shit

- Bengalis keep separate utensils for "amish" and "niramish". Utensils which are used to cook even onions and masoor dal cannot be used to cook niramish

- There are older generations who consider chicken (and its eggs) as asaucha even if they consume mutton

- Also, if any non-Hindu consumes water from a glass, we have to splash Gangajal on it to purify it

- No leftover is allowed on days of Shaucha

- If you haven't washed your hands and mouth after eating, then touch any food, it becomes "aeto", meaning impure. It cannot be mixed in with other food or they become aeto as well

- You cannot enter the kitchen to cook without taking shower and changing into fresh clothes

- If you take a haircut, you have to take shower before entering the premises of the home because you become asaucha

- When a woman makes bhoga, her saree cannot touch the floor or the food cannot be offered to the gods

- Also, there is no concept of sargi among Bengali Hindus. Vrat is ideally nirjala (or water can be consumed at max)

- Once vrat ends, you can only consume sattvic food and not go down on 1kg carb/fat heavy BS like vrat pizza

- Bengalis also have a concept called "heri". If you cook fish or mutton and a drop of masala/water splashes on you, you have to take shower

- The concept of cross contamination is an extremely important thing in Bengali homes.

- Milk and salt cannot touch each other, hence Bengal has a culture of dairy based desserts but no paneer dishes

- You have separate clothes for sleeping and work, you cannot wear the same dress you went out in and sleep in the same thing

- Post travel, you have to take a shower immediately because you become asaucha

And the thing is that Bengali non-UCs (like mine) are far more likely to be orthodox when it comes to these things than even urban UCs in Kolkata

The thing is that I would actually prefer to follow these, but it's not possible in North India where people aren't that keen on rules of shaucha
