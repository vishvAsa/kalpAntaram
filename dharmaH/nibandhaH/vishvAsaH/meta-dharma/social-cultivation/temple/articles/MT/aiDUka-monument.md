Aiḍūka monument (_Viṣṇudharmottara Purāṇa_, 3.84)

## eDUka kurgan
In the mahAbharata, a related monument, the eDuka is spoken of in negative terms as part of the degenerate worship in the kali age. Priyabala Shah, who studied this structure, felt that the eDUka was a funerary monument containing bones and that it continued in later times as the bauddha elUka, which is also believed to contain funerary relics. She bases this conclusion on the explanations of medieval commentators like mallinAtha, who state that the eDUka was relictuary for bones (antargata+asthi-kuDya-nAma). In that sense, it seems to be a survival of the ancient kurgan. 

## viShNudharmottara
In contrast, the aiDuka emerged as a positive Hindu deva shrine in the viShNudharmottara. However, it was quickly superseded by standard prAsAda shrines and completely fell out of vogue. VS Agrawala and P Shah felt the massive brick li~Nga shrine at ahiChatra was an aiDUka. CD Collins pointed out that two panels at Elephanta depict an aiDUka. Below is the aiDUka section of the viShNudharmottara:

mārkaṇḍeya uvāca |
Mārkaṇḍeya said:

> aiḍūka-rūpa-nirmāṇaṃ  
śṛṇuṣva gadato mama |
aiḍūka-pūjanāt pūjā  
kṛtāsya jagato bhavet || 1 ||
> 
> Listen to me as I speak regarding the construction of the eḍūka form. By worshipping the eḍūka, worship is rendered to this entire world.

> bhadra-pīṭhaṃ budhaḥ kuryāt  
sopānaiḥ śobhanair yutam |  
caturbhir yādava-śreṣṭha  
yathā-diśaṃ arindama || 2 ||  
> A wise man should construct a bhadrapīṭha (mound/base) provided with four beautiful stairways (sopāna), O Best of Yādavas, according to the cardinal directions, O Tamer of Enemies.

> tasyopariṣṭād aparaṃ  
bhadrapīṭhaṃ tu kārayet |  
tasyopariṣṭād aparaṃ  
tādṛg-vidham arindama || 3 ||  
> 
> Above that, he should cause a second bhadrapīṭha to be made; and above that, a third of the same kind, O Tamer of Enemies.

> tasyopariṣṭāt kartavyaṃ  
liṅga-rūpaṃ vijānatā |  
na tu tatrāpi kartavyaṃ  
liṅgaṃ rekhā-virājitam || 4 ||  
> 
> Above that, a wise man should construct a liṅga shape. But even there, a liṅga adorned with lines (rekhā) should not be made.

> tasya madhye dhruvāṃ yaṣṭiṃ  
caturasrāṃ tu kārayet |  
tasyopariṣṭāt kartavyā  
bhūmikās tu trayodaśa || 5 ||  
In its center, he should cause a firm, four-sided (square) shaft (yaṣṭi) to be made. Above that, thirteen tiers (bhūmikā) are to be constructed.

> tasyopariṣṭāt kartavyaṃ  
tathaivāmala-sārakam |  
tasyopari punar yaṣṭiḥ  
kāryā rājan suvartulā || 6 ||  
samārdha-candra-madhyastha-  
candrakeṇa virājitā |   
bhūmikā yā mayā proktā  
tathaivāmala-sārakam || 7 ||
bhuvanās te tvayā jñeyāḥ  
tathā rājaṃś caturdaśa |  
liṅgaṃ maheśvaro devo  
vṛttā yaṣṭiḥ pitāmahaḥ || 8 ||  
caturasrā tu yā yaṣṭiḥ  
sā ca devo janārdanaḥ |
guṇa-rūpeṇa vijñeyaṃ  
bhadra-pīṭha-trayaṃ tathā || 9 ||  
>
> Above that, an āmala-sāraka (ridged finial/capstone) is likewise to be made. Above that again, O King, a perfectly round shaft (yaṣṭi) is to be made, adorned with a crescent moon (ardhacandra) containing a central spot/moonlet (candraka). The tiers (bhūmikā) that were described by me, along with the āmalasāraka – these are to be understood [as the] fourteen worlds (bhuvanas) by you, O King. The liṅga is the god Maheśvara (Śiva); the round shaft is Pitāmaha (Brahmā), and the shaft, which is square, is God Janārdana (Viṣṇu). The three bhadrapīṭha platforms are likewise to be understood as the forms of the three guṇas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas).

> guṇādhānam iti proktaṃ trailokyaṃ sacarācaram |
adhastād bhuvanānāṃ tu liṅgopari tathā nṛpa || 10 ||
lokapālāś ca kartavyāḥ śūlahastāś caturdiśam |
virūḍho dhṛtarāṣṭraś ca virūpākṣaś ca yādava || 11 ||
kuberaś ca mahātejāḥ sūrya-veśadharaḥ śubhaḥ |
sarve kavacinaḥ kāryāś śubhābharaṇa-bhūṣitāḥ || 12 ||
This is declared to be the repository of the guṇas (guṇādhānam) – the three worlds, moving and unmoving. Below the worlds (tiers), upon the liṅga, O King, the World-Protectors (Lokapālas) holding tridents (śūla) in their hands are to be made in the four directions: Virūḍha, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Virūpākṣa, O Yādava and Kubera, possessing great splendor, fine and wearing the solar attire ( bright as the sun). All should be armored and adorned with beautiful ornaments.

virūḍhakaṃ vijānīhi śakraṃ deva-gaṇeśvaram |
dhṛtarāṣṭraṃ vijānīhi yamaṃ bhuvana-nāyakam || 13 ||
Know Virūḍhaka to be [an emanation of] Śakra (Indra), the lord of the divine hosts; know Dhṛtarāṣṭra to be [an emanation of] Yama, the lord of the world;

virūpākṣaṃ vijānīhi varuṇaṃ yādasāṃ patim |
rājarājaṃ vijānīhi kuberaṃ dhanadaṃ prabhum || 14 ||
Know Virūpākṣa to be [an emanation of] Varuṇa, the lord of the aquatic creatures; know Rājarāja to be Kubera, the giver of wealth and powerful lord.

aiḍūka-rūpaṃ kathitam mayaitat, prajā-hitākhyaṃ yadu-vaṃśa-mukhya |
aiḍūka-pūjā-niratā labhante, sukhaṃ manuṣyā divi vāsam ante || 15 ||
Thus has this form of the aiḍūka, called "beneficial to all beings," been spoken by me, O Chief of the Yadu lineage. Men devoted to the worship of the aiḍūka attain happiness, and in the end, an abode in heaven.
