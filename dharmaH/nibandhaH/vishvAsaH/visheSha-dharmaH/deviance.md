### Hierarchy
> Anal intercourse does not appear as prominently in Sanskrit sources as fellatio; the practice is barely touched on in the Kāmasūtra and the other extant treatises on erotics, and then only in a heterosexual context. Historically, this practice is known to have aroused strong negative feelings among the Indians, perhaps because of the Hindu horror of the pollution attached to defecation,²⁰ as well as the belief in the general ritual impurity of all orifices below the navel.²¹ The preference for passive anal intercourse, unlike fellatio, is seen as an acquired behavior and not as congenital; anal intercourse is practiced, according to the Suśruta (3.2.39), by the "unchaste and others [abrahmacāryādāḥ] who treat their own anus as a man does women." According to Dalhaṇa's commentary, anal intercourse may be practiced as a preliminary to heterosexual vaginal intercourse by the lascivious or by those of weak libido (compare the fellator, above). In addition, one may bugger oneself with one's own flaccid penis.²² The possessor of a "bent penis," no. 6 in the Caraka's list above, corresponds to the latter. The Suśruta (3.2.44) explains that although the fellator and the anal receptive have the capacity for potency, they are still called ṣaṇḍha-s, because their potency derives from their pleasure in deviant (viprakṛti) practices.²³
