## Kāmākhyā
Kāmākhyā temple in Assam is the only major Hindu temple where there is evidence for occasional narabali (human sacrifice) being offered in the early modern period, though it was of war captives and "volunteers" (bhogīs, i.e. individuals who were dedicated to the temple and treated with high honors, and fed well before being sacrificed), not of children.

Moreover, narabali has no connection to puruṣamedha, which is an archaic śrauta sacrifice that is kalivarjya. 

## puruṣamedha
Even during the Vedic period, puruṣamedha did not involve sañjñapana of humans, at least in the variant codified in the Vedic texts like Śatapatha-Brāhmaṇa (paśavo babhūvur asañjñaptāḥ, atha hainam vāgabhyuvāda: "puruṣa mā santiṣṭhipo yadi saṃsthāpayiṣyasi puruṣa eva puruṣam atsyati" iti tān paryagnikṛtān evodasṛjat).

The Śunaḥśepa tale may retain serve as an etiological myth which reflects a rejection of an earlier Aryan practice of human sacrifice to propitiate the god Varuṇa and the tale of Jarāsandha keeping kṣatriyas captive a Girivraja may point to a similar practice in honor of the deity Rudra.

The Pūru king Ayutanāyin's name is traditionally claimed to be a reference to the performance of 10,000 (myriad) puruṣamedhas, but this is likely symbolic.

Rajendralal Mitra discusses the topic of human sacrifice at length.

## Construction sacrifice
"Construction sacrifice" to propritiate earth-spirits may've been practiced in South India up till the early modern period, though it was exceedingly rare (e.g. according to folklore, Bhiṣṭappayya's wife Lakṣmāmbe is said to have sacrificed her life to ensure stability of the Dvāragopura at Hampi).

Jyotiba claimed that Mangs were buried alive beneath foundations during Peshwa rule, though he didn't cite any evidence to substantiate his claim.

## Bṛhadīśvara
This claim of weekly child sacrifice in Bṛhadīśvara temple is clearly a fabrication. Bṛhadīśvara was never a major seat of Kaulas or Kāpālikas. It was an orthodox mantramārgin/Śaiva Siddhāntika temple which operated under strict Agamic injunctions.
