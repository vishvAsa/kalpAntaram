ON THE INDIRECT ACCESS TO SACRED HINDU KNOWLEDGE

On the indirect access to sacred Hindu knowledge

Satyan Sharma

Manu

Maharși Manu says ’na śūdrāya matim dadyāt’ (one should not give knowledge to a Śūdra), ’na cāsyopadiśed dharmam’ (one should also not instruct a Śūdra about dharma), ’na cāsya vratam ādiśet’ (one should also not instruct a Śūdra about an observance). All of these prohibitions appear in verse 80 of the 4th chapter in the Mānava Dharmaśāstra (MDh). Does this mean that such knowledge is truly and completely prohibited for the Śūdras?

Kullūka Bhaṭṭa

Kullūka Bhaṭṭa (somewhere between 12th and 15th centuries), the commentator of MDh, interprets it as a prohibition against direct instruction (sākṣādupadeśa), particularly regarding the instruction about dharma and vrata. He cites Maharși Aṅgiras’ statement as per which a Śūdra can be instructed about prāyaścitta (expiation) by a Brāhmaṇa, in a setting where there is a Brāhmaṇa in between himself and the Śūdra1. Kullūka then states that prāyaścitta is an indication of all dharmas, which implies that a Śūdra can learn dharma, observances and so forth through such indirect instruction2.

Raghavānanda Sarasvatī

Another commentator of MDh, Raghavānanda Sarasvatī (later than 14th century) mentions a verse from the mokṣadharma parvan of Mahābhārata (MBh) to state the same3. In his Caturviṃśatimataṅgraha (CMS), Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita (17th century) follows the same line of thought and makes way for indirect instruction of Śūdras4.

Nīlakaṇṭha

Nīlakaṇṭha Bhaṭṭa (17th century) states the same in his Saṃskāramayūkha (SM)5.

Nanda Paṇḍita

In his ‘Keśavavaijayantī’ (KV), a commentary on the Viṣṇu Smṛti, Nanda Paṇḍita (between 16th to 17th centuries) uses this technique at two levels. At the first level, he makes a room for indirect instruction for Śūdras about dharma and observances6. At the second level, he makes a room for indirect hearing of Vedas by Śūdras in a similar setting, i.e., by keeping a Brāhmaṇa in the middle of the reciter and the Śūdra7.

Vedādhyayana

Regarding the knowledge of Vedas, the primary system is that of Vedādhyayana (memorising the Veda by hearing and repeating the recitation performed by one’s Guru)8. This is governed by the Svādhyāyavidhi (svādhyāyo'dhyetavyaḥ), which is found in the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (2.15). This is about the direct instruction and memorisation of Vedas. Majority of traditional scholars take this to be applicable only to the initiated (upanīta) males of the Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya and Vaiśya varṇas.

Vedaśrāvaṇa

There is also a secondary system, which is Vedaśrāvaṇa (causing someone to hear Vedas by keeping a Brāhmaṇa in the middle). The is based on a verse from the mokṣadharma parvan of MBh. Thus is the verse spoken by Maharși Vedavyāsa:

sarvas taratu durgāņi sarvo bhadrāņi paśyatu | śrāvayec caturo varṇān kṛtvā brāhmaṇam agrataḥ || — MBh, 12.314.45 (as per the BORI edition)

“All should cross difficult regions, all should see the auspicious. (He) should cause the four varṇas to hear, by keeping a Brāhmaṇa ahead.”

This verse gives a system where people of all varṇas (all humans in general) could indirectly hear the Vedas. The condition is that between the reciter and the listener, there must be a Brāhmaṇa (at whom the recital would be directed). If you are acquainted with traditional Sanskrit texts, you may say that this verse is an injunction about hearing Itihāsas and Purāṇas, and I would ask you to read the whole chapter in which this verse is found and point out exactly where this verse or any other verse in the chapter talks about Itihāsas and Purāṇas, which could directly lead to a restricted interpretation of this verse. The concerned chapter is about the Vedas.+++(5)+++

This verse fits perfectly with the technique of indirect instruction and specifically with Nanda Paṇḍita’s statement that there is no problem if a Śūdra hears Vedas indirectly by keeping a Brāhmaṇa in the middle. Do note that Nanda does not cite this, or any other statement as an authority while saying so.

Nārāyaṇa Tīrtha

That this verse did not appear in this context only recently is clear from Nārāyaṇa Tīrtha’s (between 17th and 18th centuries) commentary on Śāndilya Bhakti Sūtra called Bhakticandrikā (BC), where his opponent says that indirect hearing of Vedas and its meanings is fine if a Brāhmaṇa is kept in the middle9. Nārāyaṇa refutes his opponent’s claim by saying that the act of understanding Vedas through Vedadhyayana is not for all10.

It is worth noting that as per Nārāyaṇa himself, the scriptural statements he has cited to refute his opponent’s claim, are about understanding Vedas through Vedadhyayana and not Vedaśravana (hearing of Vedas). A key component of Vedadhyayana is memorisation, which is missing here in Vedaśravaṇa (aimed at understanding the teachings of Vedas). In this way, all the prohibitions regarding hearing of Vedas, could be restricted to Vedadhyayana and to the direct hearing as demonstrated by Nanda11.

Raghunandana Bhaṭṭācārya

Among the widely known traditional scholars of the olden times, only Raghunandana Bhaṭṭācārya (16th century) takes the said verse to be about making a Śūdra hear the Vedas. In his Smṛtitattva, he takes it to be related to certain rituals of Śūdras where Veda-mantras are to be chanted12. It seems that just like other scholars, Raghunandana also may have felt certain problems in accepting a very straightforward meaning of the verse. The only difference is that he did indeed accept its correct context, i.e., hearing of the Vedas, and not Itihāsas and Purāṇas.

Recent practice

Even in recent times, opportunities for the indirect hearing of Vedas have been given by some traditional Hindu institutions. For example, in the year 2021, Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pītha had uploaded videos of Veda Pārāyaṇa (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda Krama Pātha) on its Youtube channel. In his speech (premiered on Youtube on December 10, 2021), Śrī Vidhuśekhara Bhāratī Svāmī stated that all were blessed to hear the Veda Pārāyaṇa13. He also explained some of the Veda-mantras14.

In another related video (premiered on Youtube on December 26, 2021), he explained some other Veda-mantras and said that although the chanting of Vedas is not for all, understanding the meaning and praying accordingly is available to all15.

Although the said verse from MBh has not been cited as an authority for this, the basic principle of indirect instruction and hearing seems to be followed here. This principle can be traced back to Maharși Vedavyāsa’s statement: “śrāvayec caturo varṇān kṛtvā brāhmaṇam agrataḥ”.
