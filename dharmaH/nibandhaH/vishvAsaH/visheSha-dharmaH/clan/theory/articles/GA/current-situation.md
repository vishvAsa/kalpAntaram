Ideally, the best situation is where the rules naturally flow from the metaphysics (as an earthly manifestation of the metaphysical principles) as much as one can discern, with the remainder being the bare minimum to merely fill the gaps. The metaphysics does not need the rules for it to make sense.

Right now, we have two problems:

1\. Those who cannot see the metaphysical background of the rules pertaining to Varṇāśrama/Ācāra: This is a deficiency that afflicts both the detractors and traditionalists.  
It is a bigger problem for the latter because they lack the tool to distinguish that which is really the core (that which is metaphysically meaningful & potent) from what is peripheral (practices/conceptions which have sprung up in historical time to merely give support to the core ideas/precepts and do not have an independent value of their own)  
or worse, what is plainly an intrusive, unwholesome presence that is not even worthy of being peripheral. 

2\. There is zero motivation for anyone on the traditionalist side to introspect any of these things because they are often too attached to the whole package as received.

> Its not resolvable by introspection though, esp. given the trade-off is violation of shastra or tradition. Our perception of the metaphysical is always limited and so is any distinction we attempt to make w.r.t. core, peripheral & "intrusive".

1\. The metaphysical points are imparted to us through scripture, not perception. 

2\. The underlying assumption is that there is no downside or trade-off at the other end: adhere to and practise the whole gamut of traditions as received without viveka. 

3\. Śāstra cannot be violated, agreed. But if one is closed-off to all enquiry and even scripture-based yukti, he will end up practising that which is not really the Śāstra at that point in time or, worse, never was. I agree that great caution and impartiality need to be exercised and one should not let modern agendas or sentiments cloud his objectivity but to deny the necessity of the exercise is not a viable answer.
