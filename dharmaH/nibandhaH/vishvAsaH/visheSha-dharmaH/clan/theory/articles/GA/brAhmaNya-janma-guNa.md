## jaiminIya brAhmaNa
Source: [TW](https://x.com/GhorAngirasa/status/2052587727913435175)

The janma-guṇa debate specifically with respect to Brāhmaṇyam lies resolved in Śruti itself, albeit through ritual ontology—the classical explanatory framework of the Brāhmaṇas. 

The  Brāhmaṇas embody a unique approach where moral/ethical and other philosophical insights are born in a meshwork of complex ritual/metaphysical correlations.

While this may sound off to many unacquainted with this mode of thinking, it actually gives a basis for understanding why certain precepts or injunctions/vidhis are ontologically “prior” to social/historical processes and not merely products of “class conflicts” or “historical developments”, to which many in Western academia are often fond of reducing these vidhis.


The ritual metaphor for the relationship between a Brāhmaņa’s birth & conduct, from the Jaiminīya-Brāhmaņa (see image)

The exact portion from the screenshot that we are concerned with:

> ekānnasaptatiḥ prātaḥsavanasya stotriyāḥ |  
tāṣ ṣaṭ-catvāriṃśad bṛhatyaḥ saṃpadyante |  
yā hi tisro gāyatrī yaste dve bṛhatyau |  
na tāṣṣaṭcatvāriṃśadbṛhatyaḥ saṃpadyante |  
tadetadāyadeva prātaḥsavanaṃ bṛhatīmabhisaṃpadyate |  
yasmād āyadeva prātaḥsavanaṃ bṛhatīm abhisaṃpadyate  
> tasmād brāhmaṇo jāyamāna eva lokī jāyate |  
taṃ vā tvai caraṇena bhūyāṃsaṃ kurute  
> taṃ vā kanīyāṃsam |  

A few terms to understand:

ekānnasaptatiḥ - one short of 70 = 69  
prātaḥsavana - the morning-pressing of the soma plant for the juice to be offered to the Devas (savana—pressing)  
stotriyāḥ - sāmagāna-s combined together into a larger set are called stotras  
ṣaṭcatvāriṃśat - six and forty - 46   
bṛhatyaḥ - Bṛhati is a chandas where a single verse has 36 syllables (Bṛhat means 'great')   
saṃpadyante - to attain  
caraņa - conduct   

The Prātaḥsavana uses Gāyatrī.  
The Mādhyandina-savana (midday pressing) will be in the form of the Trișțubh chhandas.  
The Tṛtiya-savana (the third and final pressing of the soma for the day) will be in the form of the jagati chhandas. 

As one may already know, Gāyatrī is associated with the Brāhmaņa, 
Trișțubh with the Kșatriya  
and Jagati with the Vaiśya.

In the morning-pressing (Prātaḥsavana), only Gāyatrī verses are used in the stotra. How many Gāyatrī verses are used? 69.  
How many syllables does each Gāyatrī verse contain? 24.  

The Bṛhati verse has 36 syllables.  
Given the Gāyatrī’s 24 and Bṛhati’s 36, one would need 3 Gāyatrī-s to make two Bṛhati-s — yā hi tisro gāyatrī yaste dve bṛhatyau |

A small number of Gāyatrī-s by itself is not good enough to equal the 46 Bṛhati-s (na tāṣṣaṭcatvāriṃśadbṛhatyaḥ saṃpadyante - note the 'na...sampadyante' - do not attain/equal). The Gāyatrī-s in the morning pressing need to attain the form of 46 Bṛhati-s. How will that happen? 

By reciting 69 Gāyatrī-s—(69x24)/36= 46 Bṛhati-s  

So, although the Gāyatrī-s are there at the start of the morning rites, the chant of 69 Gāyatrī-s in the Prātaḥsavana is needed to match 46 Bṛhati-s. 

Now, recall the classic vaidika relationship between Gāyatrī chhandas/metre & the Brāhmaņa. 

Likewise, though Brāhmaņyam (Gāyatrī) is right there at birth (start of morning rites), a Brāhmaņa becomes great or lowly depending on how he builds upon the virtue of his birth. 

Due to how the morning pressing becomes equal to the  46 Bṛhati-s (through 69 Gāyatrīs), a Brāhmaņa is born with an innate spiritual lordship due to his birth but becomes high or low, depending on how he builds on the Brāhmaņyam of his birth with his conduct — 

> yasmād āyadeva prātaḥsavanaṃ bṛhatīm abhisaṃpadyate  
> tasmād brāhmaṇo jāyamāna eva lokī jāyate |  
taṃ vā tvai caraṇena bhūyāṃsaṃ kurute  
> taṃ vā kanīyāṃsam |


And the Tirukkural of Tiruvalluvar teaches in line with the Śruti:

மறப்பினும் ஓத்துக் கொளலாகும் பார்ப்பான்   
பிறப்பொழுக்கங் குன்றக் கெடும்

"Even if he forgets, he can study again  
but a pArppAn's (brAhmaNa's) birth will be ruined if his conduct falls"

Birth & Conduct are both there.

### Not mere justification
> Most human societies appeal to cosmic precursors to justify their social order. Is this fundamentally different to St Paul saying: Eve was taken from Adam's body, & marriage recreates the divinely ordered union w/man as the head of woman? Cf Purusha's body in the Rg Veda

It’s fair to say that all religions invoke some cosmic (or in the case of what you have cited, historical as they believe it) precedents for their moral teaching. I believe you are referring to 1 Corinthians 11.8-9:

For man was not made from woman, but woman from man. Neither was man created for woman, but woman for man.

What makes the Brāhmaṇas unique is the fact that moral precepts are encoded in rituals; this is not a feature you expect to find in other religious texts—i.e. the idea that a ritual structure or pre-cosmic ritual act serves as the basis for a moral injunction today. 

It’s easy to see it as a mere justification for a social order as I have pointed out. However, there are the experiences of those who see out this ritual/mythic lore play out in their lives. There is a difference between saying that Levites were rewarded with the priesthood because Levi killed certain offenders and pointing to a complex ritual structure. One must not be always cynical of the metaphysical basis of beliefs. Justifications are usually found in far more straightforward passages or narratives or myths, not complex ritual prose. 

Particularly, in the example I gave, these esoteric explanations are in Vedic texts for ritual users; an insider group and were not intended to convince those outside this group, unlike the pro-Levite passages of the Torah which were for everyone of Israel to hear and obey.
