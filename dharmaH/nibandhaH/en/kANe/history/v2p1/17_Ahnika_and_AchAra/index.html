CHAPTER XVII

ĀHNIKA AND ĀCĀRA

(Daily and periodical duties and ceremonies)

[[P640]] This forms a very important topic of [[dharmasastra|dharmaśāstra]]. The daily duties of the [[brahmacdrin|brahmacārin]] have been already described above and those of the [[v@naprastha|vānaprastha]] and yati will be spoken of later on. Under this section we are concerned principally with the duties of the [[sndfaka|snātaka]] (the would-be householder) and the [[grhastha|gṛhastha]] (the householder).

[[Béfore|Before]] the [[Ghnika|āhnika]] duties are described a few remarks about the importance of the stage of householder would not be out of place. It has already been shown (pp. 424-425) how one school of [[dharmasistra|dharmaśāstra]] writers represented by Gautama and [[Baudhiyana|Baudhāyana]] looked upon the stage of householder as the only [[&srama|āśrama]]. The eulogies of the householder are sung in numerous passages of the [[Dharmasastra|Dharmaśāstra]] works. [[Gaut,|Gaut.]] (III. 3) declares that the householder is the source (support) of all the other [[&8rama|āśrama]]s, because the other three do not produce offspring.1 Manu ([[IIL|III.]] 77-78) states that as all creatures live by receiving support from the air, so other [[ASramas|Āśramas]] subsist by relying for support on the householder and that as men belonging to all the three other orders ([[A5ramas|āśramas]]) are supported from day to day by the householder alone with (gifts of) food and sacred knowledge, the householder’s is therefore the most excellent [[&$rama|āśrama]]. Manu (VI. 89-90) reiterates the same sentiments under a different figure ‘ just as all big and small rivers find a resting place in the ocean, so men of all [[Géramas|āśramas]] find support in the householder and the householder is declared to be the most excellent of all the [[d4ramas|āśramas]] by the precepts of the Veda and [[smrtis|smṛtis]], since he supports the other three’. [[Visnu Dh. 8.|Viṣṇu Dh. S.]] (59. 27-29) contains almost the same remarks as Manu. Vide Vas. VIII. 14-16 (15 being identical with [[Manu VI,|Manu VI.]] ¢0). [[Vas. VII,|Vas. VII.]] 17 (and X. 31), [[Baud. Dh. 8.|Baud. Dh. S.]] [[IL|II.]] 2. 1, Udyogaparva 40. 25 say that a householder observing the rules laid down for him does not fall away from the world of Brahma.2 [[P641]] In the [[Mahabharata|Mahābhārata]] also the eulogy of the order of householder is frequent. [[Santiparva|Śāntiparva]] 296. 39 is the same as Manu VI. 90. [[Santi|Śānti]] 270. 6-7 states ‘as all beings live on the support given by their mothers, so other [[asramas|āśramas]] subsist on the support of the order of householders’.3 In the same chapter (verses 10-11) Kapila condemns those who hold that [[moksa|mokṣa]] (final release from [[sarsara|saṃsāra]]) is not possible for him who remains a householder. [[Santi|Śānti]] 12. 12 holds that, if weighed in the balance, the order of householders is equal to all the other three put together. Vide also [[Santi|Śānti]] 11. 15, 23. 2-5, Vanaparva chap. 2. The Ayodhyākāṇḍa 106. 22 also says that the stage of householder is the most excellent of all [[asramas|āśramas]].

The [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]] householder is again divided into several varieties from different points of view. Baud. Dh. S. III, 1. 1, Devala (quoted in the Mit. on [[Y&j|Yāj.]] I. 128) and other works divide a householder into two varieties viz. [[$alina|śālīna]] and [[yayavara|yāyāvara]]4 the latter being superior to the former.’5 The [[$ilina|śālīna]] [[P642]] is one who dwells in a house, is possessed of servants and cattle, has a fixed place and a fixed village and has grain and wealth and follows the life of worldly people; the [[yay&avara|yāyāvara]] is one who subsists by the best of livelihood, viz. picking up grains that fall down when the corn that is reaped is taken to the house or threshing floor by the owner and who does not accumulate wealth or who does not earn his livelihood by officiating as priest, or by teaching or by accepting gifts. Manu (IV. 7) appears to divide [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]] householders into four varieties, viz. one who possesses enough to fill a granary or a store filling a corn jar, one who collects as much as will satisfy his needs for.three days, or one who makes no provision for the morrow, Vide [[Santiparva|Śāntiparva]] 244.1-4 and [[Laghu-Visnu|Laghu-Viṣṇu]] II.17 for similar statements, The Mit. on [[Y&j|Yāj.]] I. 128 says that ‘ [[4alina|śālīna]] is of four varieties viz. (1) one who maintains himself by officiating as a priest, teaching Veda, accepting gifts, agriculture, trade and breeding cattle, (2) one who subsists by the first three out of the above six, (3) one who subsists by officiating as a priest and by teaching, (4) one who subsists by teaching alone. Manu IV. 9 (as interpreted by the Mit.) refers to these four varieties. The [[Ap. Sr.|Āp. Śr.]] (V. 3. 22) distinguishes between [[$4lina|śālīna]] and [[yayavara|yāyāvara]].6 The Baud. gr. III. 5. 4 refers to [[yayavara|yāyāvara]]. The word ‘ [[yadyavara|yāyāvara]]’ occurs in the Tai S. V. 2, 1. 77 * therefore the active man lords it over one who is easygoing’; but here the word has not probably any technical sense.

In the Vaik. VIII. 58 (=Vaik, Dh. S. I. 5) householders are divided into four classes. The first class (called vartavrtti) [[P643]] maintains itself by agriculture, cattle-rearing and trade; the second (83lina) observes various niyamas (vide [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] [[IIL|III.]] 313), offers [[pakayajñas|pākayajñas]] (sacrifices of cooked food), kindles the [[Srauta|Śrauta]] fires, offers the [[dar$a|darśa]] and [[pirnamisa|pūrṇamāsa]] sacrifices each half month, offers [[citurmasyas|cāturmāsyas]], in each half year offers an animal sacrifice and each year the soma sacrifice; the third ([[yayavara|yāyāvara]]) is engaged in the six actions viz. offering sacrifices of havis and soma, officiating as priest at such sacrifices, studying the Veda and teaching it, making gifts and receiving them, constantly attends his fires (8rauta and smarta), and gives food to guests that come to him; the fourth (called [[ghoracarika|ghorācārika]], one whose rules are awfully difficult to observe) is observant of niyamas, offers sacrifices but does not officiate at others’ sacrifices, studies the Veda but does not teach it, makes gifts but does not receive them, maintains himself on corn fallen in the fields &ec., is absorbed in [[Narayana|Nārāyaṇa]], performs agnihotra in the morning and evening, in [[Margasirsa|Mārgaśīrṣa]] and [[Jyegtha|Jyeṣṭha]] performs observances that are like the edge of a sword and attends upon his fires with herbs from a forest. These four names occur also in [[Brhat-Parasara|Bṛhat-Parāśara]] p. 290 ([[Jivinanda|Jīvānanda]]’’s ed.).

Some of the usual, outstanding or important matters alone can be dealt with here. Some of the works contain moral exhortations to the householder. For example, the [[Anusisanaparva|Anuśāsanaparva]]9 (141. 25-26) [[P644]] says ‘‘shirhs3, truthfulness, compassion towards all beings, quiescence, charity according to one’s ability—this is the best dharma for a [[grhastha|gṛhastha]] (householder). From very ancient times there were several ways of dividing the day. Sometimes the word ‘ahah’ is distinguished from night and sometimes it stands for the period from sunrise to sunrise (and includes day and night). For example, in Rg. VI. 9. 1 we have10 the dark day (i. e. night) and the bright day (i. e. the period when there is light). This part (viz. the period of sunlight) is divided some times into two parts viz, purvahna (period before noon) and aparahna (the time after noon) Vide Rg. X. 34. 11, Manu III. 278. Day time is also divided into three parts, morning, midday and evening, which correspond to the three libations of Soma juice in pratahsavana, madhyandina-savana and trtlya-savana, Vide Rg. III. 53. 8 where these three parts of the day are mentioned when Indra comes to drink Soma for a [[muhiirta|muhūrta]] each time and [[IIL|III.]] 28. 1, 4 and 5 (where all three savanas are named) and III. 32. 1, III, 52. 5-6. The day (of 12 hours) was often divided into five parts,11 viz. pratah or udaya (sunrise), sarngava, madhyandina or madhyahna (mid-day), apardhna (afternoon) and s@yahna or astagamana or sGyam (evening). Bach of these five parts of day time will be equal to three [[muhiirtas|muhūrtas]], In some [[smrtis|smṛtis]] and purinas these five parts are mentioned and defined; e. g. in the Prajapati[[smrti|smṛti]], [[P645]] vv. 156-157, [[Matsyapurana|Matsyapurāṇa]] 22. 82-84, 124. 88-90, Vayu 50. 170-174. Vide Apararka p. 465 (on [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] I. 226) where a éruti passage and verses from [[Vyasa|Vyāsa]] are quoted about these five parts.12 The whole day of twenty-four hours was divided into thirty [[muhtrtas|muhūrtas]]; vide Sat. Br. XII, 3. 2. 5, 8S. B. E. vol. 44, p. 169 where the year is said to have 10800 [[muhiirtas|muhūrtas]] (360 x 30), The Tai. Br. III 10. 1 gives the names of the fifteen [[muhiirtas|muhūrtas]] of day-time, such as Citra, Ketu &o.

The Madanaparijata p. 496 quotes [[Vyasa|Vyāsa]] for the fifteen parts of the day.

The [[smrtis|smṛtis]] however generally divide day-time into eight parts. [[Daksa|Dakṣa]] II. 4-5 divides the day into eight parts and then treats at length about the duties to be performed during those eight parts. Kātyāyana divides day-time into eight parts and asks the king to assign three parts after the first to the investigation of judicial proceedings.13 That this was a very anoient division follows from several considerations. [[Kautilya|Kauṭilya]]14 divides the day and night each into eight parts and prescribes what the king is to do in the eight parts of the day and also in the eight parts of the night. Vas, XI. 36, Laghu. [[Harlta|Hārīta]] 99, Laghu-Satatapa v. 108 say ‘the sun becomes mild-rayed in the 8th part of the day and that time is called kutapa’.15 The Kadambari of Bana (para 24) speaks of the light of the sun becoming clear and bright in the first eighth part of the day.16 In the [[Mahabharata|Mahābhārata]] frequent reference is made to the sixth part of the day as a somewhat late hour for taking one’s meal (and as making one hungry). Vide Vanaparva 179. 16, 180. 16, 293. 9, A§vamedhika parva 90. 26-27.17

[[P646]] The principal matters to be discussed under @hnika are: getting up from bed, &auca (bodily purity), [[dantadhAvana|dantadhāvana]] (brushing the teeth), [[snana|snāna]] (bath), sazndhyd, tarpaṇa, the five [[mahāyajfias|mahāyajñas]] (including brahma[[yajfia|yajña]] and honouring guests), attending on fire, bhojana (mid-day meal), obtaining wealth, studying and teaching, evening sardhya, gifts, going to bed, performing sacrifices at certain stated times. The [[Parasara|Parāśara]] smrbi I. 39 summarises the most important daily duties as bathing, performing the sarhdhya prayers, japa, homa, worship of gods and honouring of guests, and vaiśvadeva are the six (principal) daily action.18 Manu (IV. 152 =Anuéisana parva 104, 23) also enumerates in one place the principal daily actions that must be got through in the morning viz. answering calls of nature, toilet, bathing, brushing the teeth, applying collyrium to the eyes and worship of the gods.19

Although as stated in the [[Suryasiddhanta|Sūryasiddhānta]]20 the day was calculated from sunrise, daytime for practical purposes was extended for a short time before sunrise and after sunrise. According to the [[Brahmavaivarta-purana|Brahmavaivarta-purāṇa]] the four nāḍīs (or ghaṭikās) before sunrise and the four nāḍīs after sunset are included in daytime (dina) i. e. when a man takes his bath before sunrise on a particular day that bath is one for the day that is to begin after he takes his bath.21 Several smṛtis like Manu IV. 92, [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] I. 115 enjoin that a man should get up from bed at the brāhma muhūrta,22 should reflect over dharma and artha that he would seek to attain that day and over the bodily efforts that he would have to undergo for securing his object and think out the real meaning of Vedic injunctions. Kullūka and others say that the word muhūrta in Manu IV. 92 means [[P647]] only time generally and not a period of two ghatikis and that it is called brahma because that is a time when one’s intelli gence and one’s power to compose a literary work are at their best. The Par. M. ([[IL|II.]] 1. p. 220)23 says that there are two [[muhiirtas|muhūrtas]] in the half watch before sunrise, the first of the two is called brahma and the second raudra. PitAmaha quoted in the Sm. C. (I. p. 88) says that the last watch of the night is called ‘brahma [[muhiirta|muhūrta]]’. From very early times getting up before sunrise was prescribed specially for a student and generally for everyone, Gaut. 23. 21 says that if the sun rose while a [[brahmacarin|brahmacārin]] was asleep he should stand up the whole day without food and mutter the [[Gayatrl|Gāyatrī]] throughout the day (as a penance) and if the sun set while he was asleep he should sit up the whole night engaged in muttering the [[Gayatri|Gāyatrī]]. [[Ap. Dh.|Āp. Dh.]] S. [[IL|II.]] 5.12. 13-14 and Manu II. 220-221 contain similar rules and they employ the word ‘abhinirmukta’24 (or abhinimrukta) to denote one who is asleep when the sun sets. The Gobhila[[smrti|smṛti]] (in verse) I. 139 says that on getting up one should wash one’s eyes. In the Rgvidhana it is ordained that on getting up one should wipe one’s eyes with water after reciting Rg. X. 73. 11 the latter half of which says ‘ remove away from us darkness, fulfil our eyes and releases us who are as if bound with snares’.

The Sm. C. (I p. 88) quotes the [[Kirmapurana|Kūrmapurāṇa]] to the effect that on getting up from sleep some time before sunrise one should contemplate on God. The [[Ahnika-prakasa|Āhnika-prakāśa]] (p. 16) quotes five verses from the [[Vamanapuranpa|Vāmanapurāṇa]] (14. 23-27) which are to be recited on getting up as a morning hymn, one of which is quoted below.25 These verses are repeated even now by some [[P648]] old people. Some works say that he who repeats the four verses called [[Bharatasavitrl|Bhāratasāvitrī]] in the morning obtains the reward of hearing the whole [[Mahabharata|Mahābhārata]] and attains the highest Brahma.26 The [[Ahnikatattva|Āhnikatattva]] (p. 327) quotes a verse to be repeated on getting up from bed in which the serpent [[Karkotaka|Kārkoṭaka]], [[Damayantl|Damayantī]], kings Nala and [[Rtuparna|Ṛtuparṇa]] are remembered for removing the effects of Kali,27 and the [[Smrtimuktiphala|Smṛtimuktāphala]] quotes a verse about Nala, [[Yudhisthira|Yudhiṣṭhira]], [[Sita|Sītā]] and [[Krsna|Kṛṣṇa]] as [[punyasioka|puṇyaśloka]] (singing of whose glory is holy).28 The [[Acararatna|Ācāraratna]] (p.10 a) states that one should repeat the names of certain famous and long-lived ([[cirajlvins|cirañjīvins]]) personages viz. [[A§vatthiman|Aśvatthāman]], Bali, [[Vyasa|Vyāsa]], Hanuman, [[Bibhisana|Vibhīṣaṇa]], [[Krpa|Kṛpa]], [[Paradurama|Paraśurāma]] and [[Markandeya|Mārkaṇḍeya]] and also the names of five boly women, [[Ahalya|Ahalyā]], [[Draupadi|Draupadī]], [[Sita|Sītā]], Tara and Mandodarl. Even now old men brought up in the orthodox ways repeat these names on getting up.

In some works it is said that if on getting up a man sees a [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]] learned in the Veda, a lady whose husband is living, a cow, an altar where fire is kindled, he becomes free from adversities and that if a man sees on getting up in the morning a very sinful man, a widow, an untouchable, one naked, one whose nose is cut off, that is an indication of kali (misfortune or strife).29 [[Parasara|Parāśara]] XII. 47 says that one who has built the fire altar (for Vedic sacrifices), a dark-brown cow, one who is engaged in a satira (or performed it), the king, an ascetic, the ocean—these purify a man the moment they are seen, so one should see them always.

Then the next act is to answer the calls of nature. Very detailed rules are laid down about these even in the most ancient sūtras and [[smrtis|smṛtis]]. If a man has no time for this lengthy tarpaṇa the Dharma. sindhu and other digests prescribe an extremely brief one, viz. he repeats two verses and offers water thrice. The verses are ‘ may the gods, sages, pitrs,’human beings, from Brahma up to a blade of grass, be satiated and also all the pitrs, the mother and the maternal grand-father and the rest, may this water mixed with sesame be for the crores of families of bygone ages residing in the seven dvipas from the world of Brahma downwards’.

The [[Snana|Snāna]]sitra (3rd kandika) of Katyadyana attached to the Par. gr. contains a description of farpana. Like the Baud. Db. 8S. it lays down that ‘om’ is to be uttered before the name of every deity (devatd) and ‘trpyatam’ (or trpyantim if the word denoting the deity is in the plural) is the verbal form employed. The deities are only 28 and slightly differ from those of Adv. The group of sages is made up of only Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatana, Kapila, Asuri, Vodhu, and Paficasikha (Kapila, Asuri and Paficasikha are according to the Sahkhyakarikd the names of the founders of the S8inkhya philosophy and stand in the relation of teacher and pupil). Then (after the rsitarpaṇa), the householder is to mix sesame in water and wear the sacred thread under the left arm and suspend it from the right shoulder’ and offer the water to Kavyavad Anala [[P694]] (fire), Soma, Yama, Aryaman, Agnigvattas, Somapas, Barhisads.161 Offerings of water mixed with sesame are to be made thrice to each of the above with joined hands (jalaiijali) and this is to be done by all householders even if the father be living. The remaining part of tarpaṇa (viz. pitrtarpaṇa) is to be done only by him whose father is dead. Gobhila-[[smrti|smṛti]] IT. 18-20, [[Matsyapurana|Matsyapurāṇa]] 102. 14-21 are very similar to the [[Snana|Snāna]]sitra. According to Advalayana and others water is offered with the right hand only while according to Kātyāyana and others water is offered with both hands.162 The Sm. C. I. p. 191 says that the householder should follow his grhyasūtra and if there is nothing in it on this point there is an option, Karsnajini prescribes that in $r&ddha and marriage only the right hand is employed in making an offering or gift, but in tarpaṇa both hands (made into an afijali) are employed. One163 afijali of water is offered to each of the gods, two to Sanaka and other sages and three to each of the pitrs. When tarpaṇa is performed while the householder is still in the water with his wet clothes on, he offers the water in the stream itself; but when he wears dry clothes and performs tarpapa, then he is to let fall the handfuls of water in a pure vessel of gold, silver, copper, bronze, but not in an earthen one; or he may let the water fall on the ground covered with kusas (Sm. C. I, p. 192). There were several views on this point (vide Gr. R. pp. 263-264). In modern times daily tarpaṇa has become very rare. Only a few even among the orthodox or priestly [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]]s and among those who have studied the several sastras (such as grammar [[P695]] &ec.) do it daily, but generally most [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]]s perform tarpaṇa as part of brahmayajña on one day in tha year in the month of Sravana.

A special tarpaṇa was offered to Yama on the 14th of the dark half of a month if it was a Tuesday or on the 14th of the dark half. Vide Sm. C. I. pp. 197-198, Madanaparijata p. 296, Par. M. Gobhila-[[smrti|smṛti]]164 (II. 22-23) emphasizes the importance of tarpaṇa by remarking that, as all beings, animate or inanimate, desire water from the [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]] who brings prosperity to all, tarpaṇa should always be done by him and that if he does not do it he would incur great sin and that if he does it he would support this world, The idea underlying tarpaṇa seems to be indicated even by the Tai.
