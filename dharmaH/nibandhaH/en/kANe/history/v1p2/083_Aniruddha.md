83. Aniruddha

[[P727]]

[[Aniruddhabhattha|Aniruddhabhaṭṭha]] is one of the early and eminent Bengal writers on Dharmaśāstra. His *Hāralatā* was published in the *Bibliotheca Indica Series* (1909) and his *[[Pitļdayitā|Pitṛdayitā]]* alias *[[Karmo. padesinipaddhati|Karmopadeśinīpaddhati]]* was recently published by the Sanskrit Sahitya Parishad at Calcutta (No. 6).

The *Hāralatā* deals with impurity on birth and death, with the acts allowed to be done or forbidden during impurity, with periods of impurity on death in a distant land or on the deaths of infants and women, with rules when two periods of impurity overlap each other, with impurity on the death of *sapiṇḍas*, the meaning of *sapiṇḍa*, persons who have not to observe periods of impurity, cremation and burial, offering of water to the deceased, observances during mourning, persons entitled to perform rites after death, observances after the period of mourning, persons to whom no water should be offered.

The *[[Pitrdayitā|Pitṛdayitā]]* is a work intended for the followers of the [[Samaveda|Sāmaveda]]. Its contents are :— the duties on rising from bed, brushing the teeth, bath, *[[samdhyā|sandhyā]]*, *tarpana*, *[[vaisvadeva|vaiśvadeva]]* *pārvana* *śrāddha*, eulogy of gifts, *[[sapiṇḍikaraṇa|sapiṇḍīkaraṇa]]* and other *[[srāddhas|śrāddhas]]*, *[[antyesti|antyeṣṭi]]* and rites during the days of mourning after death, the letting loose of a bull. The printed work appears to be the same that is described in the I. O. Cat. at p. 474 as *[[Karmopadesini|Karmopadeśinī]]*. But the India [[Oflice|Office]] Ms. appears to have certain various [[read ings|readings]] e. g. a reference to *[[Kāmadbenu|Kāmadhenu]]* and *Kalpataru* at the end of the section on *[[antyești|antyeṣṭi]]* does not occur in the printed text.[^1044]

[^1044]: कल्पतरुकामधेन्वादिसंग्रहाकृष्टे महोपाध्यायेन विरचिते शुद्धिप्रकरणेन्त्येष्टिविधिः । folio 114b.

[[P728]]The first verse of the *Hāralatā* states that the author consulted the commentaries on Manu and other Smṛtis.[^1045] Besides the well-known [[smrtikāras|smṛtikāras]] such as Manu, [[Yājnavalkya|Yājñavalkya]], [[Narada|Nārada]] etc. the authors and works named in the *Hāralatā* are :— Asahāya as the [[Bhāsyakāra|Bhāṣyakāra]] of Gautama, *[[Kamadhenu|Kāmadhenu]]*, [[Govindaraja|Govindarāja]], [[Bho jadeva|Bhojadeva]], [[Viśvarupa|Viśvarūpa]] and [[Saikhadhara|Śaṅkhadhara]]. The *Pitṛdayitā*[^1046] says that it was composed after consulting the *[[Gobhilagshyasūtra|Gobhilagṛhyasūtra]]*, the *[[Chāndogyapariṭiṣta|Chāndogyapariśiṣṭa]]*, smṛtis, the [[sāstras|śāstras]] of Gautama and [[Vasistha|Vasiṣṭha]] and various [[sangraha|saṅgraha]] works. In the work itself, besides the above, [[Katyayana|Kātyāyana]], *[[Varāhapurana|Varāhapurāṇa]]* and smaller (*svalpa*) *[[Matsya purāṇa|Matsyapurāṇa]]* are quoted.

[^1045]: प्रणम्य पुण्डरीकाक्षं पूर्वाचार्यप्रवर्तिताः । व्याख्या मन्वादिशास्त्राणां समालोच्य विविच्यते ॥

[^1046]: गोभिलगृह्यसूत्रं दृष्ट्वा छन्दोगपरिशिष्टम् । दृष्ट्वा स्मृतीश्च बहुशः परिसंख्यानं पुराणवाक्यानि ॥ गौतमवसिष्ठशास्त्रे [[नानासंग्रहह्वचांसि|नानासंग्रहवचांसि]] चालोक्य । युक्त्या स्वयं निरूप्य ज्ञात्वा सर्व मतं च वृद्धानाम् । रचिता [[पद्धतिरेपा|पद्धतिरेषा]] सम्यक् [[कमोपदेशिनी|कर्मोपदेशिनी]] रुचिरा ।

From the last [[verse2047|verse]][^1047] of the *Hāralatā* we know that Aniruddha was a resident of [[Viharapātaka|Vihārapāṭaka]] on the bank of the Ganges and was a student of the doctrines of [[Bhatta|Bhaṭṭa]] (Kumārila). From the [[colophons1048|colophons]][^1048] at the end of the *Hāralatā* and the *Pitṛdayitā* it appears that he was a [[Dharmadhyaksa|Dharmādhyakṣa]] and a [[Campihattiya|Cāmpāhaṭṭīya]] (a section among [[Varendras|Vārendras]]) [[Brahmana|Brāhmaṇa]] of Bengal. The ms. in the India Office has a colophon in which Aniruddha is styled [[Dharma dhikaraṇika|Dharmādhikaraṇika]] (Judge). From the *[[Danasāgara|Dānasāgara]]* of [[Ballalasena|Ballālasena]] we learn (verses 6 and 7) that Aniruddha was a *guru* of the king of Bengal and rendered assistance in the composition of that work. The *[[Dānasāgara1049|Dānasāgara]]*[^1049] was composed in *[[sake|śake]]* 1091 (i. e. 1169 A. D.). From this it appears that Aniruddha was at the height of his fame in 1168, i. e. his literary activity may be placed in the third quarter of the 12th century A. D. This [[P729]]conclusion is corroborated by the fact that *Hāralatā* names Bhojadeva, Govindarāja, and the *[[Kāmadbenu|Kāmadhenu]]* as authorities and is therefore later than 1100 A. D. The *Hāralatā* is [[pamed|named]] as an authority in the *[[Suddhiviveka|Śuddhiviveka]]* of Rudradhara.[^1050] The *[[Sraddha kriyakaumudi|Śrāddhakriyākaumudī]]* of [[Govindananda|Govindānanda]] (about 1510–1545 A. D.) frequently quotes Aniruddha and includes him among ancient [[authors.1051|authors]].[^1051] In the *[[Buddhitattva|Śuddhitattva]]* of Raghunandana, Aniruddha is referred to in the plural as a mark of respect and his view is explained. In several other places the *[[Hāralata10516|Hāralatā]]*[^1051a] is quoted and rarely criticized in the *Smṛtitattva* :— vide (vol. I) Śrāddha p. 321 (cri.); (Vol. II.) [[Udvaha|Udvāha]] p. 108, [[Suddhi|Śuddhi]] pp. 238, 250 (cri.), 259, 262, 275, 291, 314. The *Pitṛdayitā* is frequently quoted by him, (vol. I). On Tithi (p. 13 cri.), p. 176; [[Śrāddba|Śrāddha]] pp. 200, 202, 231; [[Ahnika|Āhnika]] p. 358 (stating that the *Pitṛdayitā* quoted Yogiyājñavalkya VII. 162–165, VI. 27–28), 289, 393, p. 421 (cri), 424; and (vol. II. Śuddhi p. 314, [[Yajurvedi-sraddha|Yajurvedi-śrāddha]] p. 497). Aniruddhabhaṭṭa is frequently mentioned without naming any one of his works in (vol. I) [[Sraddha tattva|Śrāddhatattva]] pp. 209 (cri.), 220, 221, 222 (cri. as 'heyam'), 241 (cri. as 'pramāṇaśūnya'), 227 (cri.), Āhnika (p. 395), [[Malao|Mala°]] p. 801; Ekādaśī vol. II. p. 44. There is a commentary on the *Hāralatā* called *Sandarbhasūcikā* composed by Acyuta Cakravartin, son of [[Haridasa|Haridāsa]] Tarkācārya (vide I. O. Cat. p. 567, No. 1753). In JASB (New Sries) vol. XI. p. 362 the com. of Acyuta is described as *Sandarbhasūtikā* which seems to be a misprint for [[sūcika'|'sūcikā']].

Raghunandana in his *Ekādaśītattva*[^1052] (vol. II. p. 44) makes a casual but very striking remark. The question is about what [[P730]]day as Ekādaśī should be observed when the latter is mixed up with [[Daśami|Daśamī]] (tithi) or 12th. Vācaspati-Miśra relying on a verse in *Viṣṇurahasya* held that Ekādaśī mixed up with tenth should be observed as fast by one who does so for securing some object, while Ekādaśī mixed with 12th should be observed as fast by one who is *[[niskāma|niṣkāma]]* (who has no object in view). Raghunandana replies that *Viṣṇurahasya* has been declared as apocryphal by the *[[Danasāgara|Dānasāgara]]* claimed to have been composed by Aniruddha Bhaṭṭa (as stated in the Dānasāgrara itself). Raghunandana probably held and wants to suggest that the *Dānasāgara* was really composed by Aniruddha and allowed to be put forward in the name of his patron, king [[Ballklasena|Ballālasena]]. This will be discussed under the heading 'Ballālasena and [[Laksmanasena|Lakṣmaṇasena]]'.
