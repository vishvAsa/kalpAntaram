[[P981]]
Brief Note on [[Dharmasastra|Dharmaśāstra]] Works and Writers from Kāmarūpa ([[i, e.|i. e.]] Assam)

Raghunandana in his [[Smstitattva|Smṛtitattva]] several times mentions [[* Kamarūpiya-nibandha'|‘Kāmarūpīya-nibandha’]]. For example, in (vol. I) [[Tithitattva 587|Tithitattva]][^1537] p. 86, [[Prayaścitta|Prāyaścitta]] p. 555, Malamāsa p. 820; (vol. II) [[Ekadasi tattva|Ekādaśītattva]] p. 102. The Tithitattva (p. 76) says that the [[Smstisagara|Smṛtisāgara]] quotes the [[Matsyasūkta'|‘Matsyasūkta’]] and the Prāyaścittatattva (p. 535) says that the [[Smstisagara|Smṛtisāgara]] is [[Kāmarūpiya-nibandha|Kāmarūpīya-nibandha]]. It may be noted that on pp. 530 and 532 of the [[Prayascittatattva|Prāyaścittatattva]] (vol. I) a work entitled [[Smstisagarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]] is cited and verses of [[Bṛhad-Angiras|Bṛhad-Aṅgiras]] are quoted from it. The [[Nirnayasindhu|Nirṇayasindhu]] under the topic of [[mahānavami'|‘mahānavamī’]] (p. 186 of [[Nir,|Nir.]] edition of 1935) mentions [[Kama rūpa-nibandha|Kāmarūpa-nibandha]] and quotes the same verse as is quoted by Raghunandana (in vol. I. p. 86) as from [[Smrtisāgara|Smṛtisāgara]], a [[Kama rūpiya nibandha|Kāmarūpīya nibandha]].

On the occasion of the 22nd session of the All-India [[Ori ental|Oriental]] Conference held at Gauhati (Assam) in January 1965 a [[Prāgjyotisa souvenir'|‘Prāgjyotiṣa souvenir’]] was published by Dr. Maheswara Neog (Local Secretary of that Conference). Among the several papers contained in that souvenir, Pandit Manaranjan Sastri, Principal of the Sanskrit College at Nalbari (Assam), contributes (on pp. 91–114) a very interesting and informative paper on the Kāmarūpa School of Dharmaśāstra. In the brief account that I present here I can refer only to a few salient points.

[[P982]]

One of the early and famous writers from Kāmarūpa is [[Nilāmbarācārya|Nīlāmbarācārya]]. He is credited with the authorship of four works viz. (i) [[Amśaprakāśikā|Aṁśaprakāśikā]], a Com. on the Viṣṇupurāṇa; (ii) [[Sraddhaprakāśa|Śrāddhaprakāśa]] or [[Śrāddhabhāsya|Śrāddhabhāṣya]]; (iii) [[Kalakaumudi|Kālakaumudī]] (on the appropriate times for religious acts and rites) and (iv) Candraprabhā – a digest of [[Smsti|Smṛti]] material dealing with sins and prāyaścittas to be performed for the removal of the effects of sins. He tells us little about himself. At the end of the [[Sraddha prakāsa|Śrāddhaprakāśa]] he tells us that his father was a learned man named [[Sarkarsana|Saṅkarṣaṇa]] and gave himself up at the confluence of the Ganges with the ocean. He learnt the *śāstras* from his father. He holds the view that the word *māsa* means by itself the lunar (*cāndra*) month, while other [[Kamar ūpa|Kāmarūpa]] writers held and hold till these days the view that *māsa* means *Saura* (solar) month. As [[Sula pāṇi|Śūlapāṇi]] in his Durgotsavaviveka, and [[Govindananda|Govindānanda]] in the [[Suddhi kaumudi|Śuddhikaumudī]] (p. 275) mention him, he must be earlier than about 1375 A. D. and as he mentions the Kalpataru, [[Jimūtavāhana|Jīmūtavāhana]], Govindarāja, [[Bhavadevabhatta|Bhavadevabhaṭṭa]], he must be later than about 1225 A. D. In his Śrāddhaprakāśa (when treating of [[adhimāsas'|‘adhimāsas’]] intercalary months) he says that he himself has observed about intercalary months, certain irregularities and mentions *śake* 1199 ([[i, e.|i. e.]] 1277 A. D.) as the year in which the irregularity occurred. So [[Nilāmbara|Nīlāmbara]] must have flourished between 1240 and 1300 A. D.

The [[Kālakaumudi|Kālakaumudī]] is mentioned several times by [[Raghu nandana|Raghunandana]] e. g. vol. I, Tithi pp. 73, 129, 141 and (vol. II) in [[Ekādasitattva|Ekādaśītattva]] p. 51. A work called [[Smstisagara|Smṛtisāgara]] is quoted several times by Raghunandana e. g. (in Vol. I) Tithi p. 76 (three verses are quoted as from [[Matsyasūktā|Matsyasūkta]] cited by [[Smstisāgara|Smṛtisāgara]]), Tithi p. 86 ([[Smstisagara|Smṛtisāgara]] is a [[Kamar ipiyanibandha|Kāmarūpīyanibandha]]), Prāyaścitta-tattva p. 474 (quoting Devala), [[Prayascitta-tattva|Prāyaścitta-tattva]] p. 554 cites [[Smsti sāgara|Smṛtisāgara]] quoting Yama and Matsyatantra, (vol. I. p. 555); [[Ragu nandana|Raghunandana]] also quotes [[Smstisagara|Smṛtisāgara]] (vol. I. [[Prayascittatattva|Prāyaścittatattva]] pp. 530, 532 one verse on each page quoted from [[Bṛhad-Angiras|Bṛhad-Aṅgiras]]). It is clear from the above passages that the word ‘[[Kamarūpiya|Kāmarūpīya]]’ [[• Kamarūpanibandha'|‘Kāmarūpanibandha’]] is [[pot|not]] the name of one work, but that it is a general name for works composed by scholars from [[Kamarupa|Kāmarūpa]].

The Matsyasūkta is frequently quoted by Raghunandana, as in (vol. I) Tithi. p. 86, Malamāsa. p. 814, Saṁskāra p. 886; (in vol. II.) pp. 61, 69, 83 (several verses), 141 (when [[Stridhvani|Strīdhvani]] is *śubha* Ekādaśī pp. 61, 69, 83; [[Udvaha|Udvāha]] p. 141.

[[P983]]

Six verses from Svalpamatsyapurāṇa are cited by [[Raghu nandana|Raghunandana]] in (vol. II) [[Chandogavssotsargatattva|Chandogavṛṣotsargatattva]] p. 537 (bearing on the [[Sraddba|Śrāddha]] of a man's father.). For some details about this work, vide Journal of Ganganath Jha Research Institute, vol. IX parts 2–4, November 1953. The first five chapters are only a summary of the first six chapters of the Matsya-purāṇa. The interlocators are God incarnated as Matsya and the hearer is sage Manu.

Recently (in December 1964) Dr. P. C. Choudhury ([[Direc tor|Director]] of the Department of Historical and Antiquarian Studies, Assam) published a work edited by Pandit Manoranjan Shastri and himself entitled ‘[[Smstijyotisasāra-saigraha|Smṛtijyotiṣasāra-saṅgraha]]’ containing three Dharmaśāstra works from [[Kamarūpa|Kāmarūpa]] viz. I. [[Smstisagarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]] by [[Damodaramiśra|Dāmodaramiśra]] (composed in *śake* 1308 i. e. [[1386 A, D.|1386 A. D.]]) 76 pages; II [[Grahaṇakaumudi|Grahaṇakaumudī]] (pp. 79–121) by [[Mahāmahopadhyāya|Mahāmahopādhyāya]] [[Pitām bara-Siddhāntavāgisabhattācārya|Pītāmbara-Siddhāntavāgīśabhaṭṭācārya]], the most famous among [[Kamarūpa|Kāmarūpa]] writers on Dharmaśāstra (composed in [[sake|*śake*]] 1530 i. e. 1608 A. D.); III. Jyotirmālā (pp. 125–163) on several [[astro logical|astrological]] matters by [[Lakṣmipati|Lakṣmīpati]] in *śake* 1613 (1691 A. D.).

It appears from the 2nd Introductory verse of the [[Smrti. sāgarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]] that it was epitome of a vast work and was condensed as [[Smstisagarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]] for the benefit of the sons and pupils of [[Damodaramisra1638|Dāmodaramiśra]][^1538] and it was compiled in *śake* 1308 (i. e. 1386 A. D.). That it is a mere epitome is made clear in various places by the words [[vidhistu mūle'|‘vidhistu mūle’]] p. 43 or Vistarastu mūle (p. 73). It criticizes [[Nilambara1639|Nīlāmbara]][^1539] and mentions many works that preceded him, such as Kalpataru (p. 36), [[Jimūta vāhana|Jīmūtavāhana]], [[Rajamārtaṇḍa|Rājamārtaṇḍa]] (p. 69), and [[Bṛhad-Rājamārtaṇda|Bṛhad-Rājamārtaṇḍa]] (pp. 63, 65), several Purāṇas etc.

The colophon at the end of [[Grahana-kaumudi|Grahaṇa-kaumudī]] declares that [[Pitambara|Pītāmbara]] was patronized by the king [[Laksminārāyaṇa|Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa]] of [[Kamarūpa|Kāmarūpa]]. He quotes numerous authorities and in the [[Grahana kaumudi|Grahaṇakaumudī]] he quotes the [[Kștyacintāmaṇi|Kṛtyacintāmaṇi]] several times e. g. pp. [[P984]] 101, 102, 103 and 118). In his ‘[[Prāgjyotisa|Prāgjyotiṣa]] Souvenir’ Pandit Manoranjana Sastri points out (on p. 107) that one Pandit Taranath Goswami of Gauripur mentions 18 works called [[Kau mudis|Kaumudīs]] as composed by [[Pitambara-Vāgisa|Pītāmbara-vāgīśa]], while another Pandit adds ten more [[Kaumudis|Kaumudīs]], as composed by [[Pitambara|Pītāmbara]] (in all 28) and Pandit [[·Manoranjana|Manoranjana]] Sastri gives his own list of 31 [[Kau mudis|Kaumudīs]] of [[Pitambara|Pītāmbara]] (pp. 108–116 of Prāgjyotiṣa Souvenir). Pandit Manoranjana Sastri postulates that [[Pitambara|Pītāmbara]] lived for about 90 years from about 1525 to 1615 A. D. ([[Prāgjyotisa Sou venir|Prāgjyotiṣa Souvenir]], p. 111).

The printed Smṛtitattva apparently mentions three works called Smṛtisāgara, [[Smstisāgarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]] and [[Smṛtisārasagara|Smṛtisārasāgara]]. This last, is quoted only once by Raghunandana (vol. I Tithi p. 181, three verses) and it appears to the present author a copyist's mistake (for [[Smstisagarasāra|Smṛtisāgarasāra]]).

Besides these three, there are other [[Kamarūpa|Kāmarūpa]] writers on Dharmaśāstra, such as Vedācārya author of a Dharmaśāstra digest called [[Smsti-ratnākara|Smṛti-ratnākara]] (vide Journal of the Assam Research Society, Hemachandra Goswami Commemoration vol. XIV, 1960 pp. 63–77).

There is a large field for work by Kāmarūpa scholars on the Dharmaśāstra works composed in Assam during the last seven or eight centuries.
