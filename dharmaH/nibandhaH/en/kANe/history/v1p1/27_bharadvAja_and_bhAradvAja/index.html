[[’|]]In the Smṛticandrikā, in Haradatta and in several other works verses of Bhāradvāja are quoted, which appear to be taken from a smṛti in verse.

That Bhāradvāja was an ancient writer [[op|on]] [[arthaśāstre|arthaśāstra]] follows from the Kauṭilīya, [[where in|wherein]] the views of [[Bbāradvāja|Bhāradvāja]] are cited seven times and of Kaṇika Bharadvāja once. Some of the views of [[Bhāra ivāja|Bhāradvāja]] as described in the Kauṭilīya are that a [[kinj|king]] should choose his ministers from amongst [[bis|his]] fellow-students, that the king should consider his line of policy alone in secret, that the princes should be punished secretly when they manifest [[po|no]] love for the king their father, that the minister should set one [[priuce|prince]] against another when the [[kinu|king]] is on his [[dentli-bed|death-bed]], that when calamities befall the king and the minister, the former is the lesser of two evils, that one should bow down before the strong. This last view occurs in the [[Mahabhirata|Mahābhārata]] in the same words[[.201|.5]] The Śānti-parva (chap. 140) contains a dialogue between Bhāradvāja and king [[Sutrunjaya|Śatruñjaya]] of Sauvīra in which daṇḍa is said to be the most [[pre-emieut|pre-eminent]] among the expedients. The same parva (chap. 58. 3) mentions [[Bharadviju|Bharadvāja]] in a list of writers on

[[P292]]

rājaśāstra. The Yaśastilaka[[299|6]] (4th Āśvāsa p. 100, [[Nirṇaya. sāgara|Nirṇaya-sāgara]] ed.) quotes two verses of Bhāradvāja from his treatise on the topic of the six [[guras|guṇas]]. This shows that Bhāradvāja’s work on politics was available in the 10th century and [[contai ned|contained]] verses (probably intermixed with prose).

[[’|]]Bharadvāja (Bārhaspatya) is the sage of the whole of the sixth Maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda. The form Bharadvāja occurs (in the singular) in several verses (e. g. Ṛg. VI. 16. 5 and 33, VI. 31. 4) and in the plural also (in Ṛg. VI. 25. 9 and VI. 50. 15). The form Bhāradvāja occurs in Ṛg. VI. 51. 12 and in VII. 2. 63. Pāṇini mentions Bhāradvāja as a predecessor. The Mahābhāṣya notes at least in nine cases that the [[Bhārad vājīyas|Bhāradvājīyas]] read the Vārtikas differently ([[e. y.|e. g.]] Vārtika 9 on [[Pay.|Pāṇ.]] I. 1. 56, Vārtika 3 on I. 2. 22, Vārtika 4 on IV. 1. 79). [[Tbat|That]] is, the Bhāradvājīyas were a school of grammarians. Bhāradvāja is said to be the eldest son of Bṛhaspati in [[Anuśāsana parva|Anuśāsanaparva]] 30. 24 ff.. In [[Santiparvasan|Śāntiparva7]] chap. 140 there are two verses that state: the king should have the rod of punishment ready (or raised up), that he should always exhibit his prowess, he should have no weak points, but should find out the weak points (of others) and should pursue the holes (weaknesses) of others; that people are afraid of him whose Rod is raised and that out of the four [[upāyur|upāyas]] Daṇḍa is the supreme one. These occur in Bharadvāja’s dialogue in Śānti 140. [[Mapu|Manu]] (VII. 102-103) has almost the same verses. Kauṭilya differs

[[P293]]

from these views. He states “The ācāryas say “a king desiring the orderly maintenance of people’s life should always have the Rod of punishment lifted up (for striking when necessary); for, there is no such means for keeping beings under one’s control as Daṇḍa’; ‘No’ says Kauṭilya; for, the king that wields the Rod of punishment with severity becomes an object of disgust to beings; a king who is mild in punishing is treated with contempt, while a king that metes out the proper punishment is honoured; for the Rod of punishment used after proper consideration endows the people with Dharma, Artha and Kāma”.

It appears to the present author that Kauṭilya instead of separately mentioning Bharadvāja reported in the Śāntiparva and Manu, probably lumps the three (viz. Bharadvāja, Vyāsa who quoted Bharadvāja’s views and Manu) and refers to the three as ‘ācāryāḥ’ here. It is remarkable that Kauṭilya [[no where|nowhere]] expressly mentions Vyāsa’s name or the name [[Mahā. bhārata|Mahābhārata]] either for supporting his own views or for differing from him or it. In I. 3. 1-2 the Kauṭilīya first [[mention’s|mentions]] Sāmaveda, Ṛgveda, Yajurveda as [[Trays|Trayī]], then adds [[Atharva veda|Atharvaveda]] and [[Itihagaveda|Itihāsaveda]] and thus makes the Vedas to be five. In the [[Chandogyopanigad|Chāndogyopaniṣad]] (VII. 1.2, VII. 1. 4, VII. 7. 1) [[Itibāsa-Purāṇa|Itihāsa-Purāṇa]] is said to be the 5th Veda and it seems that the Kauṭilīya follows it. So the Itihāsaveda was entitled to great [[res ect|respect]] as Veda. It is probable, therefore, that he does not expressly state anywhere that he differs from the Mahābhārata or from Vyāsa. In the Mahābhārata itself ‘Ākhyāna’ is frequently mentioned as the 5th Veda, as in Vanaparva 58. 9, Udyoga 43. 41-42, Karṇaparva 87. 42, Śalya 6. 14. The [[Arthasūstra|Arthaśāstra]] [[1. 5 14|I. 5. 14]] ([[Purauam-itivrttam-ākhya yikā-udābaranam-dharmaśāstram-arthaśāstram cetibāsaḥ|Purāṇam-itivṛttam-ākhyāyikā-udāharaṇam-dharmaśāstram-arthaśāstram cetihāsaḥ]]) produces a jarring note, including, as it does, even [[Dharma bāstra|Dharmaśāstra]] and Arthaśāstra under [[Itihāga|Itihāsa]]. This does not stand to reason and tradition and is probably an interpolation or a marginal note, which later came to be embodied in the original work.

The Parāśara-Mādhavīya (vol III, p. 231) quotes a verse of [[Bharudvaja|Bharadvāja]] which divides a pledge into four [[varie eties|varieties]]. A few quotations from Bharadvāja [[ou|on]] matters of vyavahāra are cited in other works. For example, the [[Hra svati-vilāsa|Sarasvatī-vilāsa]] cites a verse of Bharadvāja that a [[compromise the exchange|compromise, exchange]] and a partition, if fair and [[squel|equal]], could be annulled

[[P294]]

only for ten days, but could be annulled till the 9th year, if unfair.[[393|8]] It appears that the verses of Bharadvāja on vyavahāra are taken from a work other than the ancient work on politics.
