  1. Medhātithi

Medhātithi is the author of an extensive and erudite commentary ( [[bhusyu|bhāṣya]] ) on the Manusmṛti.

It is the oldest extant commentary on that smṛti. The bhāṣya of Medhātithi was first published about forty years ago by Rao Saheb V. N. Mandlik in Bombay and recently Mr. J. R. Gharpure of Bombay brought out an edition of Medhātithi which closely follows Mandlik’s edition. A critical edition of the bhāṣya based upon all the available [[Msg.|Mss.]] is a great [[clesideratum|desideratum]]. A new edition in two volumes based on several mss. edited by M. M. Dr. Ganganath Jha was published in the G. O. I. Series in 1932 and 1939. In this edition also ten verses in the 3rd adhyāya are wanting and in adhyāya nine there

[[P574]]

are substantial lacunas in the commentary on verses 1, 4-6 as in Mandlik’s edition. An English translation of the bhāṣya by [[M, M.|M. M.]] Dr. Ganganath Jha is in progress and several parts have been published so far. In the following, Mr. Gharpure’s edition has been used. The [[bhāsyu|bhāṣya]] as printed is corrupt in many places, particularly in the 8th, 9th and 12th [[adhyāyus|adhyāyas]]. In Mr. Gharpure’s edition there is no [[bhlisya|bhāṣya]] on verses 182-202 of the 9th chapter.

Būhler in his learned and exhaustive [[Introductiou|Introduction]] to the Manusmṛti ( S. B. E. vol. 25 ) brings together a good deal of information about Medhātithi ( pp. CXVIII-CXXVI ). In [[JBBRA S|JBBRAS]] for 1925, pp. 217-221, I have offered criticisms on some of Būhler’s views and have given some additional information.

In several [[M88.|Mss.]] of the [[bhūsyu|bhāṣya]] at the end of several adhyāyas occurs a verse1 which says that a king [[nained|named]] Madana, son of Sahāraṇa, brought copies of Medhātithi’s commentary from another country and effected a restoration ( [[jūrnoddhūru|jīrṇoddhāra]] ). This does not refer to the restoration of the text of [[Medbātithi|Medhātithi]], but to the completion of the library of the king, who was Madanapāla, son of Sādhāraṇa and flourished, as we shall see later on, in the latter half of the 14th century.

Dr. Jolly ( Tagore Law Lectures p. 6 ) holds Medhātithi to be a southerner on account of the fact that his father’s name was [[Virasvainin|Vīrasvāmin]] and on account of the attention paid to his [[blāsya|bhāṣya]] by southern writers. It cannot be said that names ending in ‘svāmin’ were a monopoly of the south. The [[Rājatara rigiṇi|Rājataraṅgiṇī]] gives literary celebrities whose names ended in [[‘svārniu’|‘svāmin’]] ( e. g. V. 34 mentions a Śivasvāmī ). [[Kśīrasvāmin|Kṣīrasvāmin]] was a Kashmirian. The south has always been famed for preserving Mss. of valuable works from the north. [[M88.|Mss.]] of the Kāvyālaṃkāra of [[Bhimaha|Bhāmaha]], a [[Kāshmiriau|Kāshmirian]], are very rare and have been found only in the south. Būhler ( p. [[CXX III|CXXIII]] )

[[P575]]

seems to be right in holding that Medhātithi was a [[Kashmi rinn|Kāshmirian]] ( or at least an inhabitant of Northern India ). In [[explain ing|explaining]] such words as [[‘svarā tre’and janapadah’|‘svarāṣṭre’ and ‘janapadaḥ’]] ( Manu VII. 32 and VIII. 42 ) Medhātithi introduces Kashmir. He gives ( on Manu VIII. 400 ) the monopoly of the sale of elephants as a privilege of the kings of Kashmir where saffron abounds.2 He says that the rainbow is called [[vijnana-chāyā’|vijñāna-chāyā’]] in Kashmir ( on Manu IV. 59 ). He very frequently refers to northerners i. e. on Manu III. 234 he says [[‘kutapa’|‘kuṭapa’]] is the word for what is well-known as ‘kambala’ among northern people and on III. 238 he says ‘northern people’ wrap their heads with [[sūtaukaus|śāṭakaus]] ( garments ). He says on Manu II. 24 that in the Himālayas in Kashmir it is not possible to perform the daily [[sundhyū|sandhyā]] in the open [[por|nor]] is it possible to bathe every day in [[&|a]] river in ‘Hemanta’ [[‘and Sisira’|and ‘Śiśira’]]. On Manu II. 18 he says in other countries, some say, people marry one’s maternal uncle’s daughter ; but that is opposed to the words of Gautama’ ( 4. 3 ) and proceeds ‘even in that country taking food in the same plate with ( or in the company of ) one whose thread ceremony is not performed is not at all [[re garded|regarded]] as dharma ( but as improper conduct ). This is clearly a reference to Baudhāyana Dh. S. ( 1. 1. 19 ) according to which [[‘mātulasuti-parinayana’|‘mātulasutā-pariṇayana’]] and taking food in the same plate with one whose upanayana is not performed are two of the five usages peculiar to the south. It is fair to add that later writers like Kamalākarabhaṭṭa ( Nirṇayasindhu, 3rd pariccheda on sāpiṇḍya ) regard Medhātithi as a southerner.

Medhātithi quotes from or names numerous smṛti writers, such as Gautama, Baudhāyana, [[Avasta mba|Āpastamba]], [[Vasistha Visuu|Vasiṣṭha Viṣṇu]], [[Saṅkba|Saṅkha]], Manu, Yāj., [[Nāruda|Nārada]], Parāśara, [[Brhaspati|Bṛhaspati]], Kātyāyana and others. He refers to [[Bșhaspati|Bṛhaspati]] as a writer [[ou|on]] ‘vārtā’ ( Manu VII. 43 and IX. 326 ) and to Bṛhaspati and [[Ufonas ās|Uśanas]] as writers on politics and government ( Manu VIII. 283, VII. 2 and 155 ). On Manu VII. 43 he refers to [[Canakya|Cāṇakya]] as a writer on ‘daṇḍanīti’. In numerous places he seems to have

[[P576]]

drawn upon Kauṭilya’s work. For example, on Manu VII. 155 in interpreting ‘pañcavarga’ as ‘kāpaṭika, udāsthita, gṛhapatika, vaidehika and tāpasavyañjana’ he explains them almost in the words of Kauṭilya ( I. 2 ). On VII. 148 he quotes the five angas of mantra in the very words of Kauṭilya.3 Vide also his remarks on Manu VII. 54 ( testing of ministers by [[upalhās|upadhās]] ). He names Asahāya ( on 8. 156 ) and certain writers as Smṛtivivaranakārāḥ ( on II. 25 ). Būhler is not quite accurate (p. cxx, n. 1) when he states that Medhātithi gives only once the name of an early commentator’. On VIII. 3 he refers to the interpretations of [[Bhartryajña|Bhartṛyajña]] He refers to the interpretations of Yajvan (on VIII. 151 and 156 ). Yajvan is only the last part of a name ( as in [[Devarāja yajvan|Devarājayajvan]] ). He quotes the interpretation of Manu by Upadhyāya (on II. 109, IV. 162, V. 43, IX. 141 and 147 ). Būhler holds that Medhātithi refers to his own teacher. It is more likely that Upadhyāya, like Yajvan, is the name or part of the name of a previous commentator of Manu. On VIII. 152 the explanations of [[Rju|Ṛju]] are twice cited. On IX. 253 Medhātithi cites the view of one Viṣṇusvāmin.4 From the tenor of the quotation it appears that Viṣṇusvāmin was a writer on [[Mimāinga|Mīmāṃsā]] and not a commentator of Manu [[us|as]] Būhler thought (p. cxx, n. 1). Some [[M88.|Mss.]] read the word preceding Viṣṇusvāmī as ‘kovara’, others as [[‘kõvara!|‘kāvara’]]. It is probably ‘kāvera’ ( residing on the Kāverī river ).

He quotes ( on Manu I. 19 ) a verse from the [[Sarikhyaka rikā|Sāṅkhyakārikā]] ( [[prakrter malūn|prakṛter mahān]] &c ). On Manu I. 5 Medhātithi remarks that the evolution set out (in Manu I. 5 ff’) is in some parts based on the Purāṇas and in some other parts based on the Saṅkhya system and that it does not matter much as regards dharma’ and ‘adharma’ [[whetber|whether]] it (i. e. evolution ) is well understood or not. He further states that [[*Mahān’|‘Mahān’]] occurring in Manu I. 15 is the [[Sājkhya Mahat tattva’|Sāṅkhya ‘Mahattattva’]] and that in Manu I. 16 [[‘ahankāra’|‘ahaṅkāra’]] and the five ‘tanmātrās’ of the [[Sān khya|Sāṅkhya]] system are mentioned. On Manu

[[P577]]

[[1. 17|I. 17]] he quotes a part of [[Saṅkhyakurikā|Sāṅkhyakārikā]] 22 ( [[pancabhyah|pañcabhyaḥ]] pañca bhūtāni ) and on I. 19 quotes the first half of the same kārikā. He further remarks that the Paurāṇic procedure of evolution is mentioned by Manu in [[I. 21.11.|I. 21 ff.]] He speaks of Vindhyavāsa5 as a Sāṅkhya and says that he does not admit a subtle interim body ( antarābhavadeha ). This is probably taken [[froun|from]] Kumārila’s words.6 He repeatedly refers to the purāṇas, tells us ( on [[11I.|III.]] 232 ) that they were composed by Vyāsa and contained accounts of creation. He quotes ( on XII. 118 ) a verse from the [[Vākyapradipa.|Vākyapradīpa.]]7

He tells us ( on II. 6 ) that the [[Pancarītras|Pāñcarātras]], Nirgranthas ( Jains ) and [[Pasupatas|Pāśupatas]] were outside the pale of Vedic orthodoxy.8

Medhātithi had drunk deep at the fountain of the [[Parva mimāṁsā|Pūrvamīmāṃsā]]. His bhāṣya is full of the terms [[virthi|vidhi]] and [[artha vāda|arthavāda]]. On Manu III. 45 ( [[rtu-kālābhigāmi|ṛtu-kālābhigāmī]] syāt ) Medhātithi discusses whether this is a [[niyamu|niyama]] or [[parisunkhyā|parisaṅkhyā]] just as the Mit. on Yāj. [[1. 79|I. 79]] and 81 discusses the same question and [[Medhātitbi|Medhātithi]] quotes the well-known verse [[vidhir-atyantama prāptau niyamah pāksike sati’|vidhir-atyantamaprāptau niyamaḥ pākṣike sati’]]&c. Medhātithi ( at the end of his commentary on Manu I. 11 ) remarks that these are [[arthavādus|arthavādās]] and are to be explained [[some-how|somehow]] in a metaphorical sense, and not literally ( Arthavādā… yathā kathāñcidguṇavādena nīyante ). For [[Arthavīda|Arthavāda]] and its three varieties, vide H. of Dh. Vol. V. part 2 pp. 1240-1. On [[Mapu|Manu]] II. 1 he remarks in the first adhyāya five or six verses are meant to set out the purpose of this śāstra, the rest are [[artha vādas|arthavādas]] and it does not matter much if they are not well understood ; instruction is directly given here ( in II. 1 and the following verses on ‘What Dharma is’. Even in Chap. II he remarks that II. 83, 87, 233 are mere arthavādas, Vide, for example, remarks on Manu III. 17, 19, 37, 50, 56, V. 53-54, VIII. 21, 22, 98 &c. He quotes Jaimini’s Sūtras frequently and applies them to the interpretation of smṛti texts at every step. Vide [[J BBRAS|JBBRAS]] for 1925 p. 219 for [[P578]] examples. He cites passages from Śabara’s bhāṣya ( e. g. on III. 1 ). He mentions Kumārila by name ( on I. 3 ) and as Bhaṭṭapāda ( on Manu II. 18 ).

Medhātithi mentions several nyāyas for explaining the Manusmṛti. On II. 29 he extends the Mīmāṃsānyāya ( [[sarva śākhāpratyayam-ekam karma’|sarvaśākhāpratyayam-ekaṃ karma’]] P. M. S. II. 4. 8-33 which applies to Vedic passages ) to Smṛtis also and states that where the smṛtis differ, there is either an option or all [[pro visions|provisions]] of the Smṛtis on a certain point are to be added up ; vide H. of Dh. Vol. V. p. 1350 for this nyāya. On Manu II. 101 he mentions the nyāya [[‘Gunalope|‘Guṇalope]] ca mukhyasya’ for an explanation of which, vide H. of Dh. Vol. V. p. 1343 ( where it is shown that it is based on P. M. S. X. 2. 63 ). On Manu II. 102 he quotes the verse [[‘arke|‘arke]] cet madhu vindeta kimartham parvatam vrajet’ ( quoted by Śabara on I. 2. 4 ). Vide H. Dh. Vol. V. p. 1340 for references. On Manu X. 127 [[Medbātithi|Medhātithi]] relies on ‘Yāvad-vacanam ‘vācanikam’, for which vide H. Dh. V. p. 1348. Another nyāya that M. mentions is [[nimittāvṣ. ttau naimittikāvșttiḥ’|nimittāvṛttau naimittikāvṛttiḥ’]] on Manu [[X1.|XI.]] 220, which is explained in H. of Dh. Vol. V. p. 1345.

Some common nyāyas mentioned by M. are [[·|‘]] Brāhmaṇa-[[parivrāja kanyāya|parivrājakanyāya]] ( on Manu I. 22 ), [[Kākatāliya|Kākatālīya]]-nyāya ( on Manu I. 12 ), Viśvajit-nyāya ( on Manu II. 2 ).

Būhler at first took the remark on Manu XII. 19 ) about [[Sārīraka’|‘Śārīraka’]] [[as780|as9]] referring to [[Śhaṅkara’s|Śaṅkara’s]] bhāṣya on the Vedānta [[sutra|sūtra]], but later on changed his opinion ( [[SBE|SB E]] vol. 25, p. CXXII ) and held that it probably implies a reference to the Śārīraka sūtras. Būhler’s considered opinion does not seem to be right. The words ‘yatheha rājā … apaiti’ are a summary of [[Śhaṅkara’s|Śaṅkara’s]] bhāṣya on Vedāntasūtra II. 1. 34 and II. 3. 42. and I. 2. 11-12. On Manu II. 83 he refers to the [[Upanisad|Upaniṣad]]-[[bhāsya761|bhāṣya10]] on Chāndogya II. 23. 4 and says that that passage

[[P579]]

has been differently explained in the bhāṣya. Śaṅkara does explain that passage of the Chāndogya differently. But this is not all. In various other places Medhātithi seems to have in view the [[Sārirakabhāsya|Sārīrakabhāṣya]] of [[Sarkara|Śaṅkara]]. For example, he makes an obeisance to Parabrahma like an advaitin [[vedānta vedya-tattvaya…parasmui Brahmane|vedāntavedya-tattvāya…parasmai Brahmaṇe]] namah’. On I. 80 he [[ha8782|has11]] before [[himn|him]] Śaṅkara’s bhāṣya on the sūtra [[‘lokavat tu Tilakaivalyam’|‘lokavat tu līlākaivalyam’]] ( [[Vedantasutra|Vedāntasūtra]] II. 1. 33 ). He, however, seems to have favoured the position that the attainment of [[noksa|mokṣa]] is [[due763|due12]] not to mere correct knowledge but to the [[combina tion|combination]] ( [[samuccurya|samuccaya]]) of knowledge and [[learma|karma]] ( vide remarks on Manu VI. 32, 74-75 and XII. 87-90 ). This was probably due, as [[Kullika|Kullūka]] [[remarks784|remarks13]] ( on I. 3 ), to his being a profound [[studeut|student]] of [[Mimamsā|Mīmāṃsā]].

Medhātithi’s [[bhūsya|bhāṣya]] is full of very interesting information. But for want of space it cannot be analysed in detail. The Mit. ( on Yāj. II. 124 ) refers to the view of Asahāya and Medhātithi ( on Manu 9. 118 ) about the fourth share to be given to an unmarried sister at a partition between brothers and follows it in preference to [[Bharuci’s|Bhāruci’s]]. On [[Yūj.|Yāj.]] III. 24 the Mit. tells us that certain texts of [[Rsyaśrriga|Ṛṣyaśṛṅga]] about varying periods of impurity for brāhmaṇas and others were not accepted as authoritative by Dhāreśvara, Viśvarūpa and [[Medhñtithi|Medhātithi]]. According to [[himnī07|him16]] [[sainyāsu|saṃnyāsa]] does not mean the giving up of all the obligatory duties laid down by śāstra, but the giving up of [[thankeira|ahaṅkāra]]. Medhātithi quotes several verses from his own work called [[Smrtiviveka|Smṛtiviveka]] on Manu II. 6 ( in all 24 verses ) and on X. 5 he says that he has dealt with the topic of mixed castes in [[Smrtiviveka|Smṛtiviveka]]. I, part I, p. 276 and part 2, p. 172 ). Hence the [[Smrti viveka|Smṛtiviveka]] cited by the Parāśara-Mādhavīya most probably is Medhātithi’s work. [[Lollata, o|Lollaṭa,19]] an early writer quotes several verses of Medhātithi in his work on [[srāddha’|śrāddha’]]. etc.) are ascribed to Medhātithi and another [[verse72|verse21]] is cited ( on the same page ) about the six duties of yatis. These quotations show that Medhātithi wrote an extensive work in verse on several topics of dharma. It is to be fervently hoped that this work of Medhātithi would be brought to light some day or other. Coming as it does from such an erudite and ancient writer, it would throw a flood of light on the development of dharmaśāstra.

As Medhātithi names Asahāya and Kumārila and most probably quotes the views of [[Saṅkarācārya|Śaṅkarācārya]], he is later than 820 A. D. As the [[Mit|Mit.]] looked upon him as an authoritative writer, he must be earlier than 1050 A. D. Most probably [[be|he]] flourished between 825 and 900 A. D. [[Kullūka"73|Kullūka22]] on Manu III. 127 says that Medhātithi is much earlier than Govindarāja ( 1050-1100 A. D. ). [[Lollata|Lollaṭa]] is mentioned as a predecessor in the [[Smrtyartha-sāra|Smṛtyartha-sāra]] of [[Sridhara|Śrīdhara]], which was composed between [[1150–1200|1150-1200]] A. D. So [[Lollata|Lollaṭa]] is much earlier than 1150 A. D. He looked upon Medhātithi as a writer whose work was as authoritative as a smṛti. A work called [[Prakāśa774|Prakāśa23]] which is quoted in the Kalpataru appears to have mentioned [[Medha tithi|Medhātithi]]. Hemādri quotes at great length Medhātithi’s comments in several places.[[ 776|24]] Hence the above date is amply [[corrobo rated|corroborated]]. This conclusion is further strengthened by the fact that, though he names Asahāya, he does not mention [[Vißve rūpa|Viśvarūpa]], Bhāruci or [[Srīkara|Śrīkara]]. If by [[Migra,778|Miśra25]], in his comment on Manu XII. 118 he refers to [[Vācaspati-migra|Vācaspati-miśra]], the author of the [[Bhāmati aud|Bhāmatī and]] other works, then he will have to be placed after 850 A. D.

  1. मान्या कापि मनुस्मृतिस्तदुचिता व्याख्या हि मेधातिथेः सा लुप्तैव विधेर्वशात् क्वचिदपि प्राप्यं न यत्पुस्तकम् । क्षोणीन्द्रो मदनः सहारणसुतो देशान्तरादाहृतैः जीर्णोद्धारमचीकरत् तत इतस्तत्पुस्तकैर्लिखितैः ॥ सहारण is a Prākrit for साधारण. ↩︎

  2. यानि भाण्डानि राजोपयोगितया यथा हस्तिनः काश्मीरेषु कुङ्कुमप्रायेषु पट्टोर्णादीनि प्रतीच्येष्वश्वा दाक्षिणात्येषु मणिमुक्तादीनि &c. Should we not read यथा हस्तिनः काश्मीरेषु कुङ्कुमं प्राच्येषु पट्टोर्णादीनि ? The meaning then would be elephants are the monopoly of kings everywhere, saffron in Kashmir &c. ↩︎

  9. ननु च धर्माधर्मयोरिच्छां प्रति नियन्तृत्वे ऐश्वर्यं हीयते । तथा शारीरके दर्शितं यथेह राजा सेवानुरूपं ददाति न च तस्येश्वरत्वमपैति अतो महत्परमात्मानौ पश्यत इति व्यपदिश्यते । ↩︎

  10. उपनिषद्भाष्ये चैतदन्यथा व्याख्यातं तत्त्विहानुपयोगान्न प्रदर्शितम् । मेधातिथि. The उपनिषद् passage is ’ तयथा शङ्कना सर्वाणि पत्राणि संतृण्णानि एवमोङ्कारेण सर्वा वाक् संतृण्णा ‘. मेधातिथि explains ’ सर्वो वाग्व्यवहारानतीतो वाचश्च सर्वस्या ओङ्कारो मूलम् । तथा च श्रुतिः । तयथा शङ्कना &c. … अन्तधानमनुस्मृतिराश्रयभावापत्तिर्वा । कथं पुनः सर्वा वागोङ्कारेण संतृण्णा । वैदिक्यास्तावदोङ्कारपूर्वकत्वमुक्तम् । लौकिक्या अपि तदादीनि वाक्यानि स्युरित्यापस्तम्बवचन्त् । ‘. Śaṅkarācārya explains ’ यथा शङ्कना पर्णनालेन सर्वाणि पर्णानि पर्णावयवजातानि संतृण्णानि विद्धानि व्याप्तानीत्यर्थः । एवमोङ्कारेण ब्रह्मणा परमात्मनः प्रतीकभूतेन सर्वा वाक् शब्दजातं संतृण्णा । आकरो वा सर्वा वागित्यादिश्रुतेः । ‘. ↩︎

  11. लीलयापि कौतुकेनापि लोके राजादीनां प्रवृत्तिर्दृश्यत इति ब्रह्मविदः । मेधा० ; ’ यथा लोके कस्यचिदाप्तेषणस्य राज्ञो … लीलारूपाः प्रवृत्तयो भवन्ति ’ Śaṅkarabhāṣya. ↩︎

  12. On I. 50 he says ’ परब्रह्मावाप्तिस्तु मोक्षलक्षणा केवलानन्दरूपा ज्ञानात् ज्ञानकर्मसमुच्चयाद्वेति वक्ष्यामः । ’ ; on मनु VI. 74-75 ’ इदं तु ज्ञानकर्मणोः समुच्चयान्मोक्ष इति श्लोकद्वयं ज्ञापकम् । ‘; on XII. 87 अतश्च ब्रह्मनिष्ठा परेणापि वेदाभ्यासादीन्यनुष्ठेयानि । ↩︎

  13. मेधातिथिस्तु कर्ममीमांसावासनया वेदस्य कार्यमेव तत्त्वरूपोर्थस्तं वेत्तीति कार्यः तत्त्वार्थविदिति व्याचष्टे । ↩︎
