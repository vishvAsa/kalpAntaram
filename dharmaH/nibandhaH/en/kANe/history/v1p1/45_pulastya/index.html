  1. Pulastya

Pulastya’s name is among the ten primordial sages [[men tioned|mentioned]] in the Manusmṛti I. 34-35.

Pulastya is one of the expounders of dharma enumerated by Vṛddha-Yājñavalkya. Viśvarūpa quotes a verse from him on śārīraśauca.1 The Mit. (on Yāj. I. 261) cites [[&|a]] verse from Pulastya that a Brāhmaṇa should principally use [[asce tic’s|ascetic’s]] food (i. e. vegetable food) in śrāddha, that kṣatriyas and vaiśyas should use meat and [[būdras|śūdras]] madhu.2 The Mit. (Yāj. III. 253) quotes two verses of Pulastya who enumerates eleven intoxicating drinks together with surā as the twelfth.3 Aparārka quotes several verses from Pulastya on [[saṁdhyā|saṃdhyā]], Śrāddha, [[asauca|āśauca]], duties of yatis, [[prayascitta|prāyaścitta]]. Aparārka quotes two verses from Pulastya propounding the view that a

[[P517]]

combination of jñāna and karma is the correct view.4 The first of these verses is ascribed by him to Yoga-Yājñavalkya elsewhere (note 336). The Smṛticandrikā quotes about forty verses from Pulastya on āhnika and śrāddha. In one place it quotes Pulastya on the efficacy of bathing on Sunday, Tuesday, and Saturday.5 In another place it refers to the japa of Rāma, Paraśurāma, Nṛsimha Trivikrama.

Aparārka (on p. 1136) quotes eight verses from Pulastya that provide various religious rites or actions for dog-bite in the case of brāhmaṇa men and women.

The Dānaratnākara of Caṇḍeśvara cites a prose text from Pulastya on the gift of deer-skin.6

The Pulastya-smṛti must have been composed between 4th and 7th century A. D.

  1. स्नातकस्य त्रयोऽपाने पञ्चापानेग्निहोत्रिणः । सर्वानेवं गृहस्थेषु शौचकल्पाभियोजयेत् । विश्वरूप on या. I. 17. ↩︎

  2. मुन्यन्नं ब्राह्मणस्योक्तं मांसं क्षत्रियवैश्ययोः । मधुप्रदानं शूद्रस्य सर्वेषां चाविरोधि यत् ॥ ↩︎

  3. पानसं द्राक्षमाधूकं खार्जूरं तालमैक्षवम् । मधूत्थं सैरमारिष्टं मैरेयं नारिकेलजम् । समानानि विजानीयान्मद्यान्येकादशैव तु । द्वादशं तु सुरामयं सर्वेषामधमं स्मृतम् ॥ ↩︎

  4. ज्ञानकर्मसमायोगात्परं प्राप्नोति पूरुषः । पृथग्भावान्न सिध्यन्ति उभे तस्मात्समाश्रयेत् ॥ ज्ञानं प्रधानं न तु कर्महीनं कर्म प्रधानं न तु बुद्धिहीनम् । तस्मादुभाभ्यां तु भवेत्प्रसिद्धिर्न ह्येकपक्षो विहगः प्रयाति ॥ अपरार्क on या. III. 57, p. 961. These verses occur in the Bṛhad-Yogi-Yājña (chap. 8-29), which probably borrows from Pulastya. ↩︎

  5. रव्यङ्गारशनैर्वारैः स्नानं कुर्वन्ति ये नराः । व्याधिभिस्ते न पीड्यन्ते मृगैः केसरिणो यथा ॥ स्मृतिच०. ↩︎

  6. अथातः कृष्णाजिनविधिं व्याख्यास्यामः । कार्तिक्यां पौर्णमास्यां वैशाख्यां चन्द्रसूर्यग्रहे विषुवयोर्वा कृष्णाजिनं सखुरं सशृङ्गमत्रणं मनोहरम् । ms. No. 124 of 1884-86 from the Govt. Mss. Library at the B. O. R. Institute Poona, (folio 51a). ↩︎
