  1. Aṅgiras

Aṅgiras is one of the ten primordial sages mentioned in Manusmṛti I. 34-35). It is [[&|a]] very ancient [[oame|name]] even in the [[Rgverle|Ṛgveda]]. On Yāj. I. 50 Viśvarūpa quotes a [[verge|verse]] of Aṅgiras that what is done according to one’s [[owo|own]] will without [[follow ing|following]] the dictates of Śāstra is fruitless.1 On Yāj. III. 248 Viśvarūpa says that the [[vrutie|vrata]] called Vajra [[wu8|was]] prescribed by Aṅgiras for [[Brāhmunas|Brāhmaṇas]] guilty of deadly sins. Viśvarūpa (on Yāj. III. 255) quotes two verses of [[Argiras|Aṅgiras]] on the prāyaścitta for [[killine|killing]] the wife of a [[brāhinana|brāhmaṇa]] who has kindled the sacred fires for killing wives of other [[brahmanas|brāhmaṇas]] and kṣatriyas and vaiśyas. On Yāj. III. 266 he quotes two verses of

[[P508]]

Aṅgiras laying down prāyaścitta for killing certain beasts and birds, wherein Aṅgiras himself is [[men tioned|mentioned]] with honour (bhagavān). Aparārka (pp. 22-23) quotes thirteen verses from [[Arigiras|Aṅgiras]] on the constitution of pariṣad, wherein such terms as [[cāturvilya|cāturvidya]], vitarkī, [[angavid|aṅgavid]], [[dharmapāthaka|dharmapāṭhaka]] are explained and the last of which says that

a [[pariṣcıd|pariṣad]] sitting in judgment over those who are guilty of [[mahāpāta kas|mahāpātakas]] may consist of hundreds. The Mitākṣarā (on Yāj. I. 86) quotes several verses on the practice of satī and [[escribes|ascribes]] them to both Śaṅkha and Aṅgiras.2 Aparārka (pp. 109, 112) quotes four other verses on the same practice, one of which is in the Indravajrā metre and another prohibits [[&|a]] brāhmaṇa wife from following that practice. Medhātithi (on Manu V. 157) quotes the view of Aṅgiras on satī and disapproves of it. The [[Mitakṣara|Mitākṣarā]], Haradatta and others quote numerous verses of Aṅgiras on [[āsauca|āśauca]] and [[priyaścitta|prāyaścitta]]. Haradatta on Gautama (20. 1) quotes a verse of Aṅgiras about the seven antyajas.3 Viśvarūpa (on Yāj. III. 237) quotes a [[sutra|sūtra]] of Sumantu in which [[Angirasa|Aṅgirasa]] is cited as an authority. The [[Suddhi-mayūkha|Śuddhi-mayūkha]] quotes a verse of Aṅgiras which relies upon Śatātapa.4 The Smṛticandrikā quotes Aṅgiras on the enumeration of Upasmṛtis (vide note 260 above). The [[Smșticandrikā|Smṛticandrikā]] also contains a few prose [[quota tions|quotations]] from Aṅgiras; the same work cites [[ā|a]] verse of [[Arigiras|Aṅgiras]] holding the dharmaśāstra of Manu as the supreme guide.5

The Aṅgiras-smṛti (in Jivananda part I, pp. 554-560) in 72 verses is probably an abridgment. It lays down [[prayas cittas|prāyaścittas]] for various occasions, such as taking food and drink from [[antyajus|antyajas]], for cruelly beating or causing various injuries to cows. It also lays down various rules for the wearing of the dark cloth (nīlivastra) by women. It cites Aṅgiras and [[Apastamba|Āpastamba]] by name. The penultimate verse condemns those who rob women of their wealth.

[[P509]]

In the Ānandāśrama collection of Smṛtis (published in 1905) there is an Aṅgiras-Smṛti in 168 verses. At the [[begin ning|beginning]] it is said that, when asked by Bharadvāja and others, Aṅgiras declared the Smṛti dealing with rules of prāyaścitta. It names the following Smṛtikāras, viz. Śaṅkhalikhita (in v. 15), Sumantu (in v. 26), Āpastamba (v. 76), Manu (v. 86), Aṅgiras (147 and 155). In 1953 Mr. A. N. Krishna [[Aiyan. gar|Aiyangar]] published an Āṅgirasasmṛti at Adyar. This is different from the preceding two works and contains over 1200 ślokas. Pandit Manoranjan Sastri (of Gauhati) claims that he has found the [[insg.|mss.]] of a different [[Argirasasmrti|Aṅgirasasmṛti]] compiled before 14th century A. D. (p. 100 of the [[Pragjyotisa|Pragjyotiṣa]] Souvenir [[pub lished|published]] by him at the time of the All India Oriental Conference at Gauhati in January 1965). As the text is not yet available to scholars, and no reasons are given except the learned Pandit’s opinion on reading the Kāmarūpa Ms., other scholars have to suspend their judgment. The Kalpataru (on [[Suddhi|Śuddhi]] p. 18) states that [[Vasistha|Vasiṣṭha]] and Aṅgiras have certain passages in common (vide note below).6

There are several mss. in the Govt. Mss. Library at the Bhandarkar Institute ([[Pooua|Poona]]) [[wbich|which]] contain a varying number of verses on prāyaścitta agreeing more or less with Jivananda’s text. For example, No. 53 of 1879-80 contains about one hundred verses, No. 205 of 1882-83 contains 54 [[verges|verses]], while No. 65 of [[Visrām bāg|Viśrāmbāg]] collection and No. 83 of 1895-1902 contain only 32; No. 81 of 1884-86 is styled [[Bșhad–Argiras|Bṛhad-Aṅgiras]] and contains 151 verses, many of which are identical with those in the Calcutta text.

The Mitākṣarā (on Yāj. [[111.|III.]] 277) and the [[Smṣtiratnāvali|Smṛtiratnāvalī]] of Vedācārya (I. O. cat. No. 1552 p. 475) quote [[&|a]] [[Bșhad|Bṛhad-]]Aṅgiras and the Mitākṣarā also quotes a Madhyama-Aṅgiras several times (on Yāj. III. 243, 247, 258 and 260).
