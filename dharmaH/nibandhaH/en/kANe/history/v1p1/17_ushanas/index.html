Kāvya Uśanas is an ancient sage in the Ṛgveda ; vide [[æg.|Ṛg.]] I. 83. 5, where Kāvya Uśanas is said to have been an helper of Indra and brought out the cows ( carried away and concealed by the Paṇis ). In Ṛg. VIII. 23. 17 that sage is said to have established Agni for Manu and in Ṛg IX. 87. 3 Uśanas is called a ṛṣi and vipra and one who knew the hidden place where cows were concealed.

That Uśanas wrote a work on politics follows from [[seve ral|several]] circumstances. The Kauṭiliya quotes the [[Auśanasāļı|Auśanasāḥ]] seven times. It is almost certainly referring to a work. That work contained directions on the administration of justice also, as Kauṭilya speaks of Auśanasa method of partition ( in allowing a tenth additional share to the eldest son, III. 6 ), as [[Auśana sāḥ|Auśanasāḥ]] prescribed fines in cases where witnesses proved stupid &c. ( III. 11, 44 ). The Mahābhārata, Śāntiparva1 ( chap. 56,

[[P265]]

29-30 and 57. 3 ) refers to a work on politics by Uśanas and quotes three [[werses|verses]], the last being found in Sabhā 55. 14. In other places also we have a Nītiśāstra ascribed to Bhārgava ( Śānti. 210. 20 ) and certain verses are ascribed to him ( Śānti. 57. 40-41 and 139. 70-71 ). The Anuśāsana-parva ( 139. 8-9 ) [[states.|states ‘]] The Śāstras known to Uśanas and [[Bșhaspati|Bṛhaspati]] cannot surpass the ( innate ) intelligence of women ; how can men guard them ( completely ) ’. This verse occurs in Pañcatantra ( B. S. S. ed. of 1873 p. 41 and Hertel’s ed. p. 30 ). Bhārgava is said to be the first expounder of Nītiśāstra in Śānti. 210. 20. Two verses from Śāntiparva, chap. 138. 113 and 139. 71 are interesting as having been declared [[hy|by]] Uśanas to Prahlāda viz.2 those that confide in persons who were once inimical are destroyed by the latter and that one should proceed cautiously even after making an alliance ( sandhi ). The Ādiparva ( 76. 6 ) says that Kāvya was the [[purohitu|purohita]] of the asuras. The Śalyaparva ( 39. 6 ) says that [[Kõvya|Kāvya]] performed austerities at Kajālamocanatīrtha and the whole science of Nīti ( Rājanīti ) appeared to him. The Udyogaparva states that everyone except Bhārgava follows the wrong nīti ( 39. 30 ). The [[Sintiparva|Śāntiparva]] ( 210. 20 ) states that Bhārgava expounded Nītiśāstra which is beneficial to the world and Śānti ( 289. Ch. ed. ) explains how Uśanas came to be called Śukra. Vide under ‘ Arthaśāstra of Kauṭilya ’ for further information. The Nītiprakāśikā of Janamejaya refers to [[&|a]] succession of teachers of politics from Brahmā

[[P266]]

down to [[Janainejaya|Janamejaya]] and says that Śukra was one of those who abridged the enormous extent of rājaśāstra.3 The Mudrārākṣasa ( I. p. 71 Telang’s ed. ) also speaks of Auśanasī Daṇḍanīti. Viśvarūpa ( on Yāj. I. 307 ) asks the king to [[appo int|appoint]] ministers in accordance with the views propounded in the śāstras of [[Bphaspati|Bṛhaspati]] and Uśanas and quotes a long passage in prose from [[Bșhaspati|Bṛhaspati]] in which the śāstras of Manu, Bṛhaspati and Uśanas are joined together. This work on politics [[proba bly|probably]] contained verses also. Medhātithi on Manu ( 7. 15 ) [[quo tes|quotes]] two verses speaking of eight activities of a king4 So also on Manu 8. 50 Medhātithi cites from Uśanas the words ‘ [[prakstīnām|prakṛtīnām]] balam rājā,’ which are an Anuṣṭubh pāda. This sūtra work on politics by Uśanas has not yet been discovered. The [[Tāndya-Mahā brāhmaṇa|Tāṇḍya-Mahābrāhmaṇa]] says that Kāvya Uśanas was the purohita of the Asuras ( 7. 5. 20 ).

In the Deccan College collection there are two mss. of an Auśanasa-dharmaśāstra in prose with a few verses, viz. No. 644 of [[Viśrām bāg|Viśrāmbāg]] ( i ) and No. 191 of A 1881-82. The first contains only two folios ( 2 and 4 ) and the available portion begins in the midst of the 2nd chapter. In the second ms. also the first folio is wanting and there are gaps in the fourth chapter. It is noteworthy that the second folio of the second ms. begins just at the beginning of the 2nd folio of the first.

[[P267]]

The work is a brief one ( 8 folios ) in seven adhyāyas. Their contents [[are:-|are :—]] II. impurity on birth and death ; purification of certain things in certain ways such as with water, [[ashies|ashes]] &c. III. the four [[varvas|varṇas]] and the mixed castes such as Rathakāra, Ambaṣṭha, Sūta, Ugra, [[Māgalha|Māgadha]] &c., the Vrātyas ; IV. no bodily injury to be done to brāhmaṇa ; prāyaścittas for killing a brāhmaṇa or a man of the other varṇas and for other [[Mahā pātakas|Mahāpātakas]], prāyaścitta for eating the flesh of certain animals and for eating plants like garlic, prāyaścitta for adultery ; discussion whether a Brāhmaṇa could marry a śūdra woman ; prāyaścittas for killing various beasts and birds ; fourteen vidyās ; V. [[śrūddhrakalpa|śrāddhakalpa]] ; what Brāhmaṇas are [[punkti pāvuna|paṅkti-pāvana]] ; details of śrāddha ; food and flesh at śrāddha ; who are unfit to be invited at śrāddha ; VI. what things a Brāhmaṇa could not sell ; VII. punishments for the [[Maha pātakas|Mahāpātakas]] ; what are pure things at all times. Some of the views of the Auśanasa-dharmaśāstra are worth special notice. The son of a Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya or Vaiśya from a wife of the [[varụa|varṇa]] immediately next to it belonged to the caste of the father.5 No sin or punishment attached to [[ope|one]] if one killed an ātatāyi ( armed ) Brāhmaṇa. The fourteen vidyās are said to be the six [[ungas|aṅgas]] and the four Vedas, [[Mimiṇsā|Mimāṃsā]], Nyāya, Dharmaśāstra and Purāṇa.6

The work very often quotes the views of others with the words ‘ eke ’; for example it says ‘ the impurity on birth is ten nights for the parents of the child, but according to [[sume|some]] only for the mother.’7 The son of a Brāhmaṇa from a śūdra woman is called pārasava, but according to some he is niṣāda. The whole of the portion in the ms. about the mixed castes bears a very close resemblance to Baudhāyana ( Dh. S. [[1.|I.]] 9 )

[[P268]]

except in a few particulars.8 At the end of all chapters from the second ( except the third ) there are verses and also in the midst. There are verses introduced with the words ‘ there is a śloka on this point ’ ( bhavati cātra ślokaḥ ) or with the words ‘ api codāharanti ’.

There are about 45 verses in the work, the 7th and last chapter being almost entirely in verse. As the mss. are corrupt and full of gaps, it is often difficult to find out whether a particular passage is prose or verse. In several verses the words ‘ Manur abravīd ’ occur and in one place ‘ tan manor anuśāsanam ’.9 The latter closely agrees with Manu. Several verses are common to Manu and Uśanas. The verse ‘ gurutalpe bhagaḥ kāryaḥ ’ ( Manu 9. 237 ) occurs in the ms. So also the verse ‘ yena yena cidaṅgena ’ occurs in both.10 The words of Manu ( V. 78 ) ‘ bāle deśāntarasthe ’ are expressly quoted with the words ‘ Manur-āha ’. There are many other verses which, though not cited expressly as from a particular author, are found in other ancient works. For example, the verse ‘ āpaḥ śuddhā [[bhūmiyatāh|bhūmigatāḥ]] ’ is Manu 5. 128 and Baud. [[Db. $.|Dh. S.]] I. 5, 57. The half [[versekāruhastaḥ|verse ‘ kāruhastaḥ]] śucir nityam ’ is Manu 5. 126 and Baud. Dh. S. I. 5. 48. The verse ‘ yadekarātreṇa karoti pāpam ’ occurs in Āp. Dh. S. ( I. 9. 27. 11 ) and Baud. Dh. S. II. 1. 42. The verse ‘ tryaham prātas tryaham naktam ’ is Manu XI. 211. It is a remarkable fact that some of the verses in this dharmasūtra agree closely with the verses in the [[Uśanas–smrti|Uśanas-smṛti]] in verse, published by Jivananda ; e. g. the verse ‘ dattvā tu Vedānatyartham ’ occurs on p. 525 and the verse ‘ [[nimantritustu|nimantritastu]] yo vipro [[adhyānam|adhvānam]] samprāpadyate ’ on p. 527. Even the prose passages bear a

[[P269]]

close resemblance to the versified smṛti on many points, e. g. the prose passages about the duration of the satisfaction of the Manes by the offerings of the flesh of various animals occur almost in the same words in the versified smṛti of [[USanas|Uśanas]] ( Jivananda, part I, p. 522 ).

The Auśanasa-dharmasūtra contains several passages in prose which are the same as some verses in Manu.11 The sūtra quotes in one place the divergent views of Vasiṣṭha,12 Hārita, Śaunaka, and Gautama on the question whether a brāhmaṇa could marry a śūdra woman. The view of Vasiṣṭha ( as quoted in the ms. ) is that a Brāhmaṇa could take to wife girls of all the four castes, but in the printed text of Vasiṣṭha ( I. 24 ) a Brāhmaṇa is allowed to marry girls of three higher castes only. It is possible that the ancient reading of Vasiṣṭha’s text was ‘ catasraḥ ’ ( as quoted in the Auśanasa ) which was changed into ‘ tisraḥ ’. The passage reminds us of Manu III. 16 ( Śūdrāvedi patatyatreḥ ), but the views ascribed to [[Suunaka|Śaunaka]] and Gautama in the ms. differ from those ascribed to [[thein|them]] in Manu. In another passage Vasiṣṭha’s views seem to be quoted.[[^48|13]]

From [[quotatious|quotations]] in Haradatta’s commentary on Gautama and from the Smṛticandrikā it appears that they had access to a sūtra work of Uśanas dealing with all branches of

[[P270]]

dharma viz. ācāra, vyavahāra and prāyaścitta. For example, the Smṛticandrikā quotes prose passages of Uśanas on anadhyāya ( [[1,|I,]] p. 59 ), on [[duntadhavana|dantadhāvana]] ( I. p. 106 ) and Haradatta quotes a prose passage on anadhyāya ( on Ap. Dh. S. I. 3. 10. I ) and a prose passage on the fine for a Kṣatriya abusing a śūdra ( on Gau. Dh. S. 12. 10 ) and on Niyoga ) on Gau. Dh. S. 18. 5 ). These passages are not found in the ms. But there are others that are found. For example, the Smṛticandrikā quotes two passages, which occur in the mss.14 It appears therefore that the mss. either contain a portion only of the complete Auśanasa-dharma-sūtra or an independent sūtra work. The former alternative looks more probable. The foregoing discussion has shown that the sūtra work is later than the dharmasūtras of [[Gautaina|Gautama]] and Vasiṣṭha and also than the Manusmṛti, which it probably knew in a very ancient version slightly differing from the extant one.

In Jivananda’s collection of smṛtis there is an [[Auśanasa dharmaśāstra|Auśanasa-dharmaśāstra]] in 51 verses ( part I. pp. 497-501 ). It deals with mixed castes and their avocations, such as sūta, māgadha[[ : |, ]] [[dāpṇāla,|cāṇḍāla,]] [[$vapaca,|śvapaca,]] pulinda-&c. The same work occurs in the Ānandāśrama collection, pp. 46-48. The Mit. ( on Yāj. [[1.|I.]] 94 ) remarks that the means of livelihood for the mixed [[custes|castes]] should be sought for in the works of Uśanas and Manu.[[280|15]] It is probably this smṛti that is meant. Kullūka on Manu X. 49 says that the sale of hides and working on hides were means of livelihood for Dhigvaṇas according to the Auśanasa.

In the same collection ( part I. pp. 501-544 ) there is another smṛti ascribed to [[Usapas|Uśanas]] in 9 chapters and about 600 verses. It treats of Upanayana, daily observances like [[ācamuna|ācamana]], Vedic study, occasions of [[unadhyāya|anadhyāya]], the dharmas of [[snātuka|snātaka]] ; śrāddha, proper Brāhmaṇas for [[brāddha|śrāddha]], impurity on birth and death, rites after death, prāyaścittas for the mortal sins and lesser sins.

[[P271]]

This smṛti quotes Uśanas himself, also Manu, Bhṛgu ( Bhṛguputra III ), Prajāpati and speaks of dharmaśāstras, Purāṇas, [[Mimārsā|Mimāṃsā]], Vedānta, Pāñcarātras, Kāpālikas and Pāśupatas. Numerous verses from the Manusmṛti are found in it ( e. g. Manu II. 42, 49, 50, 125 ) and occur in the first chapter alone. There is a brief prose passage towards the end of the 9th chapter. It speaks of Kāpālikas, Pāśupatas and atheists together ( [[1).|p.]] 525 ).

None of these smṛtis in verse contains the passages in verse on vyavahāra [[ascribel|ascribed]] to [[Uśauas|Uśanas]] that occur in the Mitākṣarā, Smṛticandrikā and other works.

The Mit. ( on Yāj. III. 260 ) and Aparārka ( e. g. p. 158, 192, 255 ) contain prose passages ascribed to Uśanas and the same works contain numerous verses ascribed to Uśanas, most of which are not found in the two smṛtis in Jivananda. Some of the verses in [[Apararka|Aparārka]] ascribed to Uśanas are found in the Auśanasasmṛti ( e. g. [[Aparūrka|Aparārka]] p. 418 ‘ kuryād-aharah śrāddham ’ is Jivananda I. p. 521 ).

In other cases the verses quoted from Uśanas in [[Aparā rka|Aparārka]], though not quite identical, bear a very close resemblance ( e. g. the four verses in Aparārka on p. 450 closely resemble Jivananda I, p. 525 and have some verses in common ).

Among the views on Vyavahāra quoted from [[Ugapas|Uśanas]] are some interesting ones. It was he who said that a son was not bound to pay his father’s debt, if it was a fine or unpaid toll or what is not vyāvahārika.16 He holds that even blind, deaf or old men, women, minors could be witnesses in charges of [[sākasa|sāhasa]] ( Aparārka p. 671 ). He is quoted also on ordeals, on svāmipālavivāda and on steya.

Uśanas is quoted in Kalpataru ( Gṛhasthakāṇḍa ) 8 times, in Śrāddha 13 times ( 8 times in prose ), in Vyavahāra eleven times ( all verses, except one prose passage on p. 814 on anuloma unions ), 6 times in Niyatakāla.

It is interesting to note that he states that the son of a brāhmaṇa from a kṣatriya wife is a brāhmaṇa and the son of

[[P272]]

a kṣatriya from a [[vaibya|vaiśya]] wife is a kṣatriya ( p. 814 on [[Vyava hāra|Vyavahāra]] ). Aparārka quotes Uśanas dozens of times, about nine quotations being in prose ( most of them on prāyaścitta ).

The [[Maskaribhāsya|Maskaribhāṣya]] on Gautama Dh. S. quotes prose [[pag sages|passages]] from Uśanas in many cases e. g. on I. 64, 66, II. 17 ( long ), 18, 48 ; V. 9, X. 27 ( about principles of taxation ), 31, 36, 52 ; XII. 6, 10, 36, 42 ; XIX. 14 ( long prose followed by six verses ) and also some verses as on XII. 19 ‘ Māṣo viṃśatibhāgastu jñeyaḥ Kārṣāpaṇasya tu | Kākani tu caturbhāgo māsasyaiva [[prakirtitahtiSmṛticandrikā|prakīrtitaḥ || ’. Smṛticandrikā]] quotes Uśanas 45 times on āhnika, vyavahāra and śrāddha ; vide Mit. on Yāj. II. 159 quoting Nārada.

A work styled [[Sukranitisāra|Śukranitisāra]] was edited by Oppert ( Madras 1882 ) and by Jivananda ( Cal. 1892 ) and translated by Benoy Kumar Sarkar in S. B. H. Series. That work is a very interesting one, but is comparatively of a late date.

It is probable that at least some parts of [[Sukranitisira|Śukranitisāra]] edited by Oppert in 1882 are not older than about 1800 A. D. ( [[viz,|viz.]] such as IV. 5. II prescribing fees for lawyers ). Vide Mr. Lallanji Gopal’s paper in the Bulletin of the London School of Oriental and African Studies, Vol. 25 for 1962 pp. 524-556, in which it is [[strenously|strenuously]] urged that it is a [[fabrica tion|fabrication]] of the 19th century ). I do not agree that the whole work is later than 1800 A. D. The Śukranitisāra provides on p. 157 ( of Oppert’s ed. ). ‘ नियोगितस्यापि भृतिं विवादात् षोडशांशिकीम् । विंशत्यंशां तदर्धं वा तदर्धां तदर्धिकाम् । यथा द्रव्याधिकं कार्यं हीना हीना भृतिस्तथा ॥ ’

Such passages may be interpolations made in the first quarter of the 19th century. In the year 1793 the [[Governer|Governor]]-General-in-Council enacted Regulation VII of 1793 in order to induce men of education and character to take up the practice of law as a distinct profession. That Regulation applied only to the provinces of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. In 1802 [[Regu lation|Regulation]] XIV of 1802 was enacted in Bombay which consisted of 33 sections providing among other matters for the [[appo intment|appointment]] of pleaders, for the issue of sanads to them, for remuneration payable to them and for penalties in cases of misconduct or negligence.

Vide Śāntiparva 57. 40-41 for a famous verse of [[Bhārgak|Bhārgava]] ( i. e. Uśanas ) ; राजानं प्रथमं विन्देत् ततो भार्यां ततो धनम् । राजन्यसति लोकस्य कुतो भार्या कुतो धनम् ॥
