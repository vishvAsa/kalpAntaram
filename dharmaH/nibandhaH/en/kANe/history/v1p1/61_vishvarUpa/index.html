  1. Viśvarūpa

The commentary of [[Viśvarūpia|Viśvarūpa]] called Bālakrīḍā on the [[Yājsavalkya-smrti|Yājñavalkya-smṛti]] has been recently published in two parts by M. M. T. Gaṇapati Śāstri in the Trivandrum Sanskrit Series. The Mit. states in the introductory verses that the dicta of [[Yij.|Yāj.]] were expanded by the voluminous or ample ([[vikata |vikaṭa]]) explanations of [[Viśvarūsra|Viśvarūpa]]. In commenting on Yāj. I. 81 the Mit. tells us that [[Viśvarūjia|Viśvarūpa]] looked upon the words of Yāj. I. 79 ([[tasmin yugmāsu saṁviset|tasmin yugmāsu saṃviśet]]) as a niyama. In Viśvarūpa’s commentary on Yāj. I. 80 ([[evam gacchan|evaṃ gacchan]] &c.) we do find that the verse of Yāj. and similar passages of Manu (3. 45), Vasiṣṭha and Gautama (5. 1) are understood to contain a [[niyam|niyama]] and not a [[prescinkhyu.?!!|parisaṅkhyā]]1 On Yāj. III. 24 the [[Mit, inforins|Mit. informs]] us that Viśvarūpa, Medhātithi and [[Dhāre svara|Dhāreśvara]] looked upon certain texts of [[Rsyasriiga|Ṛṣyaśṛṅga]] on [[āściucu|āśauca]] as in conflict with well-known [[sinitis|smṛtis]] and discarded them. Mr. S. Sitaram [[Sūstri|Śāstri]] published (in 1900 at Madras) the text and translation of [[Viśvarūpia’s|Viśvarūpa’s]] comment [[ou|on]] inheritance and Mr. Setlur also published the [[wavahūrco|vyavahāra]] section. In the following pages the Trivandrum edition is relied on.

The printed com. of Viśvarūpa on the [[vyavaharu|vyavahāra]] section is [[extreinely|extremely]] meagre and scarcely merits the epithet vikaṭa applied to it by the Mit. But the commentary of Viśvarūpa on the ācāra and [[grīyascitta|prāyaścitta]] sections is truly voluminous and [[coinpares|compares]] favourably with the Mit. The style of Viśvarūpa is simple and forcible and resembles that of the great [[Saṅkarā. cārya.|Śaṅkarācārya.]] He quotes profusely from Vedic works, mentions the Carakas and Vājasaneyins (on [[Yaj|Yāj.]] 1. 32), the Kāṭhaka (on [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] III. 237 and 243) and very often supports his position by [[quotatious|quotations]] from the Ṛgveda (e.g. on Yāj. [[11.|II.]] 121 and 206), the Brāhmaṇas (e.g. the [[Sata patha|Śatapatha]] on Yāj. 1. 53 and III. 257) and from the Upaniṣads (e.g. on Yāj. [[11.|II.]] 117, the well-known

[[P554]]

Chāndogya passage about the ordeal for theft and on Yāj. I. 50 Chāndogya II. 23. 10 about the three branches of dharma). He speaks of the [[pada-rātha|pada-pāṭha]] and the kramapāṭha as due to human agency (on Yāj. III. 242). He frequently quotes the [[Grhya sūtrus|Gṛhya-sūtras]] of Pāraskara and less frequently those of Bhāradvāja and Āśvalāyana. [[lle|He]] cites a host of smṛtikāras.2 Most of the quotations attributed to Svayambhū are found in the extant Manusmṛti, but this is not the case with the quotations ascribed to Bhṛgu (vide pp. 309, 310 above). Most of the [[quota tions|quotations]] from Bṛhaspati (even on such topics as repayment of debts, sureties, the rights of sūdrāputra) are in prose, only a few being in verse (e.g. a verse about ordeals on Yāj. II. 117, a verse about the method of partition on Yāj. II. 153). [[Ou|On]] Yāj. I. 307 Viśvarūpa quotes a long prose passage from [[Brbaspati|Bṛhaspati]] in which the qualities required in a Senāpati, Pratihāra, [[Hastyadhyaksti|Hastyadhyakṣa]], [[Asvādhyalist|Aśvādhyakṣa]], Dūta, Mantrin and Uparika are set out at length. It appears, therefore, that Viśvarūpa either knew a work of Bṛhaspati in prose on arthaśāstra in which occurred a few verses or he had before him a prose work of Bṛhaspati and a versified smṛti of Bṛhaspati, both of which he regarded as the compositions of the same author. He quotes a verse (on Yāj. I. 328) from [[Visā lāksa|Viśālākṣa]], a well-known writer on politics quoted even by Kauṭilya. He refers to the arthaśāstra of Uśanas along with that of [[Bșhaspati|Bṛhaspati]]. Kauṭilya is nowhere quoted by name. The learned editor of Viśvarūpa thinks (Intro. p. V) that [[Vißve rūpa|Viśvarūpa]] took Bṛhaspati and Viśālākṣa as ārṣa writers long anterior to Yāj. and therefore used their [[dictu|dicta]] to elucidate and support Yāj., while he omits Kauṭilya because he thought Kauṭilya to be posterior to Yāj. This argument contains several fallacies. In the first place it is wrong because [[Viśva rūpa|Viśvarūpa]] quotes verses from Nārada and Kātyāyana to [[supple ment|supplement]] Yāj. There is nothing to show that Viśvarūpa regarded Nārada and Kātyāyana also as anterior to Yāj. and we have

[[P555]]

seen above that they are several centuries later than the smṛti of Yāj. Moreover Kauṭilya himself looked upon both [[Bphaspati|Bṛhaspati]] and Viśālākṣa as high authorities and so Viśvarūpa might have quoted them rather than Kauṭilya. Even taking the latest date assigned to Kauṭilya (about 3rd century A.D.) he flourished several centuries before Viśvarūpa. It is [[im possible|impossible]] to believe that Viśvarūpa was in possession of the exact chronological relation of Yāj. and Kauṭilya. Many scholars, besides, place Kauṭilya’s work centuries before Yāj. It appears, however, that Viśvarūpa had the work of Kauṭilya before him. On Yāj. I. 307 he speaks of ministers tested by the four allurements ([[upachii|upadhā]]) of dharma, artha, [[kūma|kāma]] and bhaya. This is an echo of Kauṭilya (I. 10). On [[Yāj I. 343|Yāj. I. 343]] Viś. refers to the view of some that a march should be made when neighbouring chiefs are overwhelmed in calamities.3 This is the view of Kauṭilya almost in the same words. On Yāj. I. 341 Viś. speaks of the [[inanifold|manifold]] aspects of the work of a minister, some words of his comment being almost identical with Kauṭilya’s.

Some of the verses on pp. 25-27, for example, the first verse (govadham &c.) [[relers|refers]] to Yāj. III. 234 (where ‘govadha’ is mentioned as the first among [[Upapātikas|Upapātakas]] (51 in number). On p. 26 he has a [[larika|kārikā]] ‘Smārtopi govadhostyarghyamarhayet prathamam [[gavi|gavā]]’, which includes [[Mauusmrti|Manusmṛti]] III. 3 (last quarter); on same page the words

vedam-eva [[tvad:starthe…pramānam|tvadṛṣṭārthe…pramāṇam]] paramam dharme Śrutir’ embodies a part of Manu III. 13. The [[Karikū|Kārikā]] at the top of p. 27 quotes some words of Manu II. 10.

Viśvarūpa’s work is thoroughly saturated with the lore of the [[Pūrvamimiṁsā|Pūrvamīmāṁsā]]. He quotes Jaimini by name (on Yāj. I. 225 where Jaimini VI. 8. 15 is quoted). Curiously enough

[[P556]]

he applies the term nyāya to Mīmāṁsā. He takes ’nyāya-mīmāṁsā’ in [[Yīj.|Yāj.]] I. 3 as one vidyā, while he notes that others explain [[nyayu|nyāya]] as the system of logic propounded by [[Aksa pāda|Akṣapāda]]. He quotes the sūtras of Jaimini as those of Yājñikas who know [[nynya|nyāya]] (e.g. on Yāj. I. 53 [[be|he]] quotes Jaimini I. 3. 16 and on Yāj. I. 87 he quotes Jaimini VI. 8. 17). He applies the epithet [[Naiyāyikı,|Naiyāyika]] to a mīmāṁsaka like Śabara and speaks of the [[mimārsakas|mīmāṁsakas]] as nyāyavidaḥ.4 He mentions the [[Sūbarabhāsya|Śābarabhāṣya]] by name (on [[Yīj.|Yāj.]] III. 243) and [[in |]]in several places quotes the very words of Śabara (e.g. on Yāj. III. 181).5 He quotes the Ślokavārtika of Kumārila ([[1.|I.]] 12 the verse ‘[[survasyaiva|sarvasyaiva]] hi &c) in his introductory remarks. In his comment on Yāj. [[1.|I.]] 7 he cites over fifty verses in the nature of [[kārikis|kārikās]] dealing with the relation of śruti and smṛti and kindred topics. These verses are his own composition, as in one of them he assures us that a certain [[poiut|point]] will be dealt with by him in detail in the section on śrāddha.6 [[Iu|In]] interspersing his [[coin mentary|commentary]] with [[kārikūs|kārikās]] of his own and in their style and pithiness he greatly resembles Kumārila. Throughout his work he relies upon [[mimūinsā|mīmāṁsā]] maxims and methods of discussion. For example, on Yāj. I. 4-5 he discusses the rule of Jaimini II. 4. 8 [[ft|ff]] (about

[[sarvasākhūpratyayam ekam karmu’|sarvaśākhāpratyayam ekam karma’]] in its application to smṛtis; on Yāj. [[1.|I.]] 225 [[be|he]] relies upon the position that words like [[yuva|yuvan]] and [[varalice|varāha]] are to be taken as employed in the Vedas in the same sense in which [[sistus|śiṣṭas]] use them (vide Jaimini I. 3. 9); on [[Yīj.|Yāj.]] II. 144 [[be|he]] speaks of wealth (dravya) being [[puruṣūrthre,|puruṣārtha,]] where he alludes to the well-known distinction between [[krutvartha|kratvartha]] and [[puruṣūrthu,|puruṣārtha,]] the subject of Jaimini’s 4th chap. His [[cominentary|commentary]] on Yāj. III. 212, 237, 262 are fine examples of his superb skill in the [[interpreta tion|interpretation]] and reconciliation of apparently conflicting texts.

‘Though Viśvarūpa was a past master in Pūrvamīmāṁsā lore, his philosophical views seem to have been identical with those of the great [[Sajikara|Śaṅkara]]. According to him [[moksu|mokṣa]] results from correct knowledge alone and the whole saṁsāra is due to [[avidyu il?|avidyā.]]7 He quotes anonymously one of [[Gaudlapāda’s kārikāsiis|Gauḍapāda’s kārikās]]8 (III. 5) on Yāj. III. 134.

He speaks on Yāj. III. 103 of Nārada who knew the Veda of music (gītivedavid), of purāṇa (on III. 175), and quotes verses (on [[Yñj.|Yāj.]] III. 85) from an [[abhidhūnukośu|abhidhānakośa]] (lexicon) and from a [[Nāmaratnamila|Nāmaratnamālā]] (on III. 266). He speaks of the śloka of Bhikṣāṭana (on III. [[60.|66.]]). He is in this probably referring to the [[Bhikrītanakivya,719|Bhikṣāṭanakāvya,]]9 which is mentioned by the [[Sahityavarpani|Sāhityadarpaṇa]]. [[Amony|Among]] commentators he mentions Asahāya’s bhāṣya on Gautama by name (on Yāj. III. 263). On Yāj. III. 256 he explains Mleccha as pulindas and [[Tājikus|Tājikas]] (i. e. Arabs).

It has been shown above ([[$34|§34]] pp. 423-425) how [[Viśva rūpa’s|Viśvarūpa’s]] text of Yāj. varied in some respects from that of the Mit.; how he frequently refers to the views of commentators of Yāj. earlier than himself (in the words ‘apare,’ ‘anye’), how he proposes several explanations of the same words in several cases.

Dr. Jolly (Journal of [[Iulian|Indian]] History, 1924, pp. 7-8) says that the citations of [[Vis|Viś.]] in the Smṛticandrikā about his having refuted the views of [[Dhireśvara|Dhāreśvara]] cannot be traced in the printed Bālakrīḍā, as also the reference to [[Vis,|Viś.]] in the Mit.

[[P558]]

on Yāj. I. 81 and II. 135. It has been shown above (note 529) that the printed [[Vis|Viś.]] on Yāj. I. 80 does contain the view attributed to it by the Mit. As regards the Mit. [[un|on]] Yāj. II. 135 it has to be noted that the Mit. does not mention Viś. by name there, but only speaks of bhagavān ācārya,10 which words are interpreted by the Subodhinī and the [[Būlambhatti|Bālambhaṭṭī]] as referring to Viśvarūpa. It is true that the printed Viś. does not contain in so many words the [[explana tion|explanation]] attributed by the Mit. to ‘bhagavān ācārya.’ But it is worth considering that in the printed Viś. the two quotations from Manu and Śaṅkha do occur and are put in the mouth of an objector and are explained away in a way somewhat similar to that put forth in the Mit.11 As regards the passages of the Smṛticandrikā, the matter requires careful examination. The Smṛticandrikā, (II. p. 294 Gharpure’s ed.) says that according to the Saṁgrahakāra a widow was allowed to succeed to her [[souless|sonless]] husband’s wealth if she submitted to niyoga, that the same was the view of Dhāreśvara and that Viśvarūpa refuted the view of Dhāreśvara. In no place does the printed Viś. name Dhāreśvara. The words of the [[Smrti candrika|Smṛticandrikā]] are [[uot|not]] to be taken literally. It will be shown below that the author, [[Devanmabhatta,|Devannabhaṭṭa,]] flourished about 1200 A. D. while Dhāreśvara flourished between 1000-1050 A. D. Devanna had no correct idea of their relative chronological position. It has been shown above (p. 249) how though

[[P559]]

Asahāya is named by the Mit., the Sarasvatīvilāsa very often says that Asahāya does not like ( or tolerate ) the views of Vijñāneśvara. Similarly, the same work ( para 392 ) says that Dhāreśvara and Devasvāmiu do not tolerate the view of Vijñānesvara, but Dhireśvara is one of the predecessors of Vij. actually named by him. So all that the Smṛticandrikā means is that Dhāreśvara and Viśvarūpa differed in their views on the particular points mentioned by it. The word patni is taken by Viś. to mean a widow who is pregnant at the time of her husband’s death and quotes the sutras of Vasiṣṭha and Gautama in support of his view as jñāpokas. So this view entirely differs from the view of Dhāreśvara that the widow of a sonless person succeeds if she submits to niyoga. The Smṛticandrikā (II. p. 300 ) says that the Saṁgrahakāra placed the father’s mother immediately after the mother and before the father, that the Saṁgrahakāra relied on the same arguments that were employed by Dhāreśvara and that Viśvarūpa and others refuted those arguments. The passage in the printed Viś. is somewhat corrupt in this place. Vis. does place the mother before the father on the ground of the word nātā occurring first when the word ‘pitarau’ or the compound mātāpitruritu’ is expanded. The comment does mention the verse of Man (9. 217 ) about the grandmother, but it makes no clear sense, as it stands.12 For the reason given above Rai Bahadur M. M. Chakravarti ( JASB for 1922, p. 345 and for 1915, p. 322 ) is not right when he places Vißvarūpa later than Bhojadeva because of the remarks of the Smṛticandrikā.

In the works of Jimūtavāhana ( viz. the Dāyabhāga and the Y yavahāramñtrkā ), in the Smrticandrikā, the Hāralatā, and other later works like the Sarasvatīvilāsa, the views of Viśvarīpa are frequently cited and discussed. Several such citations have been already examined by me (JBBRAS for 1926, pp. 200-204 ). From considerations of space I do not repeat here the discussion of those passages. In the Grhastha. ratnākara13 of Candesvara (No. 44 of 1883-4, in the Govt.

[[P560]]

Mss. Lib at the [[B. 0. R.|B. O. R.]] Institute Poona, folio 133a ) the explanation of Viśvarūpācārya on Yāj. [[1.|I.]] 135 is cited, which does not exactly tally with the printed Viś. Hemādri14 refers to [[Viśvarūpa’s:|Viśvarūpa’s]] explanation given in his section on partition which does not occur in the printed text. The result of the examination of these citations is that the printed text of Viś. is in the main genuine, but that in a few cases ([[parti cularly|particularly]] in the vyavahāra section ) it is corrupt or deficient.

Though Viś. holds the same view as the Mit. that [[owner ship|ownership]] does not for the first time arise on partition but that partition takes place of what is already ( jointly ) owned, yet on numerous points the two disagree. A few of them may be set out here.

[[(1)|( 1 )]] [[Vis|Viś.]] allows ( on Yāj. II. 118 ) the father [[unrest rictd|unrestricted]] freedom of distribution of property among his sons during his lifetime, while the Mit. expressly says that this power of unequal distribution is restricted to [[self acquired|self-acquired]] property.

( 2 ) Viś. ( on Yāj. II. 119 ) allows a share of property to the widows of predeceased sons and grandsons of a man when a partition takes place during his lifetime. The Mit. restricts the word ‘[[patnyab|patnyaḥ]]’ to the father’s own wives when he effects a partition during his lifetime.

( 3 ) Viś. connects the words ‘without detriment to the paternal estate’ ( in Yāj. II. 122 ) with the words ‘whatever else is acquired by himself’ and not with ‘maitra’ ( gifts from a friend ) [[und|and]] ‘[[udvihika|audvāhika]]’ ( gifts on [[inarriage|marriage]] ), while the Mit. connects the half verse ‘whatever else is acquired by the man himself without detriment to the paternal estate’ as a

[[P561]]

qualifying clause to the next half verse and to another verse ‘kramād abhyāgatam &c.’ In the Mit. the two verses ‘[[pitfdravyĀvirodhena|pitṛdravyāvirodhena]] &c.’ and ‘[[kramās|kramād]] &c.’ occur [[consecuti vely|consecutively]], while in Viś. they are separated by three verses and Viś. takes the verse ‘kramād’ &c. as referring to the re-opening of a partition for a son [[horn|born]] after partition.

( 4 ) [[Vis|Viś.]] allows [[niyog|niyoga]] only to [[sūrus|śūdras]] in general and to [[knatriya|kṣatriya]] kings in case of danger of extinction of line ( vide com. on Yāj. I. 69 and II. 131 ), while Mit. forbids niyoga in general and [[holils|holds]] the texts speaking of it [[is|as]] applicable to a girl who is only betrothed and not married.

( 5 ) Viś. appears to allow one share out of ten to the son of a [[sūlra|śūdra]] wife from a [[brālmana|brāhmaṇa]] without restriction of any kind, while Mit. restricts the share to estates other than land acquired by [[vist|gift]].

( 6 ) Viś. interprets the expression ‘half share’ ( in Yāj. II. 138 ) with reference to the illegitimate son of a śūdra as meaning ‘some portion, not necessarily exactly half.’ while Mit. interprets it literally.

( 7 ) Viś. allows a widow to succeed to her husband if she is pregnant at his death, while Mit. allows a widow to succeed without any restriction except that of chastity.

( 8 ) [[Vis|Viś.]] restricts the word ‘duhitaraḥ’ in ‘patnī duhitaraścaiva’ ( Yāj. II. 138 ) to [[putrilā|putrikā]] only and so does not allow all daughters whatever to succeed, while Mit. does not introduce any such qualification.

( 9 ) Viś. reads ‘anyodaryasya [[saṁsisti|saṃsṛṣṭī]]’ for [[anyo daryastu|anyodaryastu]] &c.’ and ‘sodaro’ for ‘[[samsrsto|saṃsṛṣṭo]]’ in Yāj. [[11.|II.]] 143 and his interpretation of the verse is entirely different from that of the Mit.

( 10 ) [[Vis.|Viś.]] reads ‘[[Ādhivedanikam|ādhivedanikaṃ]] caiva’ for ‘[[ādhiveda nikadyam|ādhivedanikādyaṃ]] ca’ of [[Mit,|Mit.]] and holds that bandhudatta, [[sulku|śulka]] and [[anvāllheyaku|anvādheyaka]] [[strīlhana|strīdhana]] of a childless woman goes on her death to her full brother; while Mit. connects these three with the preceding verse and takes the half verse ‘atitāyāṃ &c.’ [[88|as]] laying down a general rule of succession to [[strīdhand|strīdhana]] of all kinds and interprets ‘[[biudhavāh|bāndhavāḥ]]’ [[us|as]] meaning ‘husband and the rest’.

( 11 ) Viś. takes the verse ‘[[adhiviuna-striyai|adhivinna-striyai]]’ &c. [[con|on]] Yāj. II. 152 ) as applicable to a wife superseded without [[arm|any]] ground of supercession allowed by the texts; while [[Middies!|Mit. does]] not introduce any such qualification.

[[P562]]

As Viśvarūpa quotes Kumārila’s Ślokavārtika and is mentioned by the Mit. as an authoritative commentator it follows that he flourished between 750 A. D. and 1000 A. D. A greater approximation as to the date of Viśvarūpa can be made, if the identity of Viśvarūpa with Sureśvara be held established. Sureśvara, as he himself tells us in the Naiṣkarmyasiddhi, the [[Taittiriyopaniṣad bhāsya vārtika|Taittirīyopaniṣadbhāṣyavārtika]] and other works, was a pupil of the great Śaṅkarācārya whose generally accepted period is 788-820 A. D. Mādhavācārya in several works of his quotes as Viśvarūpa’s passages from the the well-known works of Sureśvara. For example, the Parāśara-Mādhavīya ( vol. I, part I, p. 57 ) quotes a kārikā of Sureśvara as that of Viśvarūpācārya.15 In the [[Vivarana prameyasaṁgraba|Vivaraṇa-prameyasaṃgraha]] ( Vizianagaram series p. 92 ) also Mādhava quotes [[&|a]] verse from the [[Bșhadāranyakopanisad bhāsya vārtika|Bṛhadāraṇyakopaniṣadbhāṣya-vārtika]] as Viśvarūpa’s.15 In the [[Purusārthaprabodha7a7|Puruṣārthaprabodha16]] of Brahmānanda-bhārati ( ms. in Bhau Daji collection, Bombay ) composed in 1476 ( probably of the Śaka era ) the author speaks of the Naiṣkarmyasiddhi as a work of Viśvarūpa. In the [[Saṁksepa-Sarkara-jaya|Saṃkṣepa-Śaṅkara-jaya]], Viśvarūpa is said to be the author of the two vārtikas on [[Sarkara’s bhāsya.738|Śaṅkara’s bhāṣya17]]. According to tradition embodied in the various lives of [[Sarkara|Śaṅkara]], the latter had four pupils, Sureśvara, Padmapāda, Toṭaka and Hastāmalaka. Several works mention Viśvarūpa as one of the four pupils and omit the name Sureśvara. For example, in the Dvādaśa-vākya-vivaraṇa of Gopāla ( Aufrecht’s Oxf. Cat. No. 557, p. 227 b ) the four pupils of [[Śhaṅkara|Śaṅkara]] are named as Viśvarūpa, Padmanābha, Toṭaka and Hastāmalaka. In the Mānasollāsa-[[vșttānta|vṛttānta]]-vilāsa of [[Rāmatīrtba|Rāmatīrtha]] we are expressly

[[P563]]

told that Sureśvara is another name of Viśvarūpa, a pupil of [[Śhaṅkara|Śaṅkara]] ( vide Mitra’s Notices vol. V, No. 1763, p. 82 ). In the Saptasūtra-[[sanıyāsa paddhati|saṃnyāsapaddhati]] ( Mitra’s Notices, vol. VI, p. 296 ) the four pupils are said to be Svarūpācārya, [[Padmā cārya|Padmācārya]], Toṭaka and Pṛthvīdhara. The Guruvaṃśakāvya ( Vanivilas ed. ) identifies Sureśvara and [[Viśvarāpa|Viśvarūpa]] ( II. 59 ) and makes him a pupil of Kumārila and [[Śhaṅkara|Śaṅkara]]. It may therefore be held as fairly established that Viśvarūpa and Sureśvara are identical. Some corroboration is afforded by the fact that Viśvarūpa quotes [[Gauda pāda|Gauḍapāda]] the ‘paramaguru’ of [[Śhaṅkara|Śaṅkara]] and holds the same philosophical views as those of [[Sarkara|Śaṅkara]]. Just as Viśvarūpa quotes Kumārila’s [[Sloka vārtika|Śloka-vārtika]], Sureśvara also in his Taittirīyopaniṣadbhāṣya-vārtika quotes a kārikā of [[Kumirila|Kumārila]] and styles the latter Mīmāṃsakam-manya.18 This shows that Sureśvara treated Kumārila with scant respect, which seems unlikely if he was at any time Kumārila’s pupil. Viśvarūpa, in his introduction,19 performs an obeisance to the Sun, the great serpent ( Seṣa ), Tilakasvāmin and Vināyaka. The Bhāmatī of Vācaspati-miśra has a similar salutation. Vācaspati-miśra wrote his Nyāyasūci-nibandha in 841-42 A. D. i. e. he was almost a contemporary of [[Śhaṅkara|Śaṅkara]] and his pupils. The [[learn ed|learned]] editor of Viś. tells us that in a commentary on [[Viß.|Viś.]] called Vacanamālā Sureśvara is bracketed with Manu and Yogiśvara ( Yājñavalkya ) as an expounder of Śāstra ( i. e. dharma-śāstra ).20 Therefore that commentator looked upon Viś. [[aud|and]]

[[P564]]

Sureśvara as identical. From all these several considerations it follows that Viśvarūpa flourished about 800-825 A. D. But this problem presents further difficulties. The mutual [[rela tions|relations]] of Sureśvara, Bhavabhūti, Umbeka and Maṇḍana are a great puzzle. I have dealt with this question in JBBRAS for 1928, pp. 289-293. The conclusions arrived at there are that Maṇḍana’s literary activity lies between 690-710, that of Umbeka between 700-730 and Sureśvara’s between 810-840 A. D. and that Umbeka and Bhavabhūti are identical, but that Maṇḍana and Sureśvara are separated by about 100 years.

In the H. of Dh. Vol. V pp. 1188-1198 the present author has discussed at some length the relative chronological [[posi tion|position]] of Kumārila, Prabhākara, Śālikanātha, Maṇḍanamiśra, Umbeka, Bhavabhūti, Viśvarūpa and Sureśvara and the conclusions relevant to Viśvarūpa are that Maṇḍanamiśra is not identical with Viśvarūpa, that [[Viśvarapa|Viśvarūpa]] and Sureśvara are identical and that Sureśvara is the name of Viśvarūpa after the latter became a [[sunnyāsin|saṃnyāsin]]. The grounds in favour of the identity of Viśvarūpa and Sureśvara are overwhelming as evidenced by the writings of great scholars like Mādhavācārya.

An interesting matter about Viśvarūpa may be mentioned here. On Yāj. I. 162 ( which is one of [[tbe|the]] verses that state the persons whose food should not be partaken of ), ‘grāmayājin’ is one ( who is [[abhojyūnnu|abhojyānna]] ) and Viś. remarks that among the Mālavas a [[grāmayājaki|grāmayājaka]] is known as ‘[[Vaiśva devika|Vaiśva-devika]]’. It is possible that Viśvarūpa was an inhabitant of Mālava ( roughly present Malwa ) or that he had stayed there for some years.

Dr. Jolly has brought together in the Journal of Indian History ( vol. III. pp. 1-27 ) some valuable information about Viśvarūpa.

In several later works a digest called Viśvarūpa-[[uibandha|nibandha]] is frequently cited. That appears to be the composition of another Viśvarūpa altogether. For example, the [[ṣan-ụavant srāddhanirṇaya|ṣaṇ-ṇavati śrāddhanirṇaya]] of [[Śivablatta|Śivabhaṭṭa]] ( which is later than [[160 A, D.|1600 A. D.]] ) tells us that [[Viśvarūpācirya|Viśvarūpācārya]] composed a [[vivurder|vivaraṇa]]

[[P565]]

( commentary ) on the Śrāddhakalikā.21 The [[Kr̥tyacintāinani|Kṛtyacintāmaṇi]] of [[Śivarima|Śivarāma]] ( No. 221 of 1879-80 in the Govt. Mss. Lib. at the B. O. R. Institute, Poona 4 ) quotes several[[33|22]] verses from [[Viśvara panibandha|Viśvarūpanibandha]] on [[supinda|sapiṇḍa]] relationship in marriage, which are not found in the [[Balakridā.|Bālakrīḍā,]] but which agree remarkably with the words of the Mit. on Yāj. I. 53. The Varṣakriyākaumudī ( pp. 378, 380 ) mentions [[Viśvarupa nibandha|Viśvarūpa-nibandha]] and quotes two verses cited therein. The [[Tithi nirṇaya|Tithi-nirṇaya]]-sarvasamuccaya ( later than 1450 A. D. ) quotes certain [[lcarileti.s|kārikās]] of Viśvarūpa on the 18 varieties of Ekādaśī.23 The Kālanirṇayasiddhāntavyākhyā ( composed in 1653 A. D. ) quotes certain verses of Viśvarūpa on the question of the disposal of food prepared for a marriage when a period of impurity on death supervenes.[[135|24]]. The Nirṇayasindhu also quotes verses of [[Viśvarūjia|Viśvarūpa]]. From these data it follows that a Viśvarūpa composed a commentary on Śrāddhakalikā and also wrote a digest on matters of ācāra and other topics of dharma in prose and verse. Raghunandana in his [[Udvāha-. tattva|Udvāha-tattva]] ( ed. by Jīvananda, p. 116 ) names a [[Viśvarupa samuccaya|Viśvarūpa-samuccaya]]. It is likely that it is the same as [[Viśvarapa nibundlhn|Viśvarūpa-nibandha]].
