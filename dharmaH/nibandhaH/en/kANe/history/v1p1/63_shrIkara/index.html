  1. Śrīkara

The Mit. on Yāj. II. 135 alludes to the view of [[Srikara|Śrīkara]] and others that the widow [[succeeded is|succeeded as]] heir to her deceased husband’s estate if it was small.1 The [[Smytisāra|Smṛtisāra]]2 of Harinātha attributes the same view to [[Srikara|Śrīkara]] and [[disappro ves|disapproves]] of it. On Yāj. II. 169 the Mit.3 cites the view of [[Srikara|Śrīkara]] about that topic and disapproves of it. Viśvarūpa also gives two explanations of that verse of Yāj., the first of which agrees with that of the [[Mit,|Mit.]] and the second is akin to [[Srikara|Śrīkara]]’s.

The works of Jimūtavāhana ( viz. the Dāyabhāga and the Vyavahāramātṛkā ), the Smṛticandrikā and the Sarasvatīvilāsa contain very interesting notices of Śrīkara’s views. Many of them were brought together [[by ne|by me]] in JBBRAS for 1925, pp. 213-215. Śrīkara like Viśvarūpa held the view that ‘duhitaraḥ in [[Yūj.|Yāj.]] refers to the [[putriku|putrikā]], he allowed the parents of a [[chililless|childless]] person to succeed together at the same time. The [[Diyabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] very severely criticizes the views of Śrīkara on the succession to re-united members, on vidyādhana and on Yāj. II. 24 ( about enjoyment for 20 years ).4 Most of the views attributed to Śrīkara were also entertained by Viśvarūpa [[ur|or]] are more antiquated than Viśvarūpa’s.

[[P572]]

Śrīkara was probably a writer from Mithilā and seems to have propounded the view of spiritual benefit as the criterion for judging superior rights of succession. The Smṛtisāra5 of Harinātha ascribes such a view to a Śrīkara-nibandha.

Whether Śrīkara wrote a commentary on a smṛti or a general digest ( nibandha ) it is difficult to say. The Smṛticandrikā6 says that Śambhu, Śrīkara and Devasvāmin [[com piled|compiled]] digests of [[smitis|smṛtis]] and [[allded|added]] their own explanations of them. The Smṛtyarthasāra7 of Śrīdhara asserts in the [[in troductory|introductory]] verses that Śrīkaṇṭha and Śrīkarācārya filled up the gaps in the smṛtis that were scattered about ( by [[intro ducing|introducing]] order out of chaos ). Śrīkara’s explanations of Yājñavalkya are frequently cited by the Mit., the Dāyabhāga and others. But the Mit. does not connect Śrīkara’s name with Yājñavalkya as a commentator, though [[Viśvarūpe|Viśvarūpa]] is [[express ly|expressly]] so connected. It appears therefore [[inore|more]] likely that Śrīkara

[[P573]]

wrote a digest of smṛtis in which he paid particular attention to the explanation of the words of [[Yājsavalkya|Yājñavalkya]]. The [[Rāja nitiratnākara|Rājanītiratnākara]]8 of Caṇḍeśvara quotes the views of Śrīkara on Rājanīti, one of which is that the poor and helpless are entitled to a share of the royal wealth.

[[Hemālrist|Hemādri]]9 quotes the view of Śrīkara on Viṣṇu and [[dis approves|disapproves]] of the faults found therein by the author of the Paṇḍita-paritoṣa.

As Śrīkara is quoted by the Mit., he is certainly earlier than 1050 [[A. I).|A. D.]] As his views agree largely with Viśvarūpa’s he may provisionally be regarded as nearly of the same period as Viśvarūpa’s. He cannot be earlier than Asahāya who is named both by Viśvarūpa and Medhātithi, though both of them are silent about Śrīkara. Hence Śrīkara must be placed somewhere between 800 and 1050 A. D. and probably [[flourish ed|flourished]] in the 9th century.

This Śrīkara must be distinguished from another Śrīkara, the father of Śrīnātha.
