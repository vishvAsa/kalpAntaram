Kāvya Uśanas is an ancient sage in the Ṛgveda ; vide [[æg.|Ṛg.]] I. 83. 5, where Kāvya Uśanas is said to have been an helper of Indra and brought out the cows ( carried away and concealed by the Paṇis ). In Ṛg. VIII. 23. 17 that sage is said to have established Agni for Manu and in Ṛg IX. 87. 3 Uśanas is called a ṛṣi and *vipra* and one who knew the hidden place where cows were concealed.

That Uśanas wrote a work on politics follows from [[seve ral|several]] circumstances. The Kauṭiliya quotes the [[Auśanasāļı|Auśanasāḥ]] seven times. It is almost certainly referring to a work. That work contained directions on the administration of justice also, as Kauṭilya speaks of Auśanasa method of partition ( in allowing a tenth additional share to the eldest son, III. 6 ), as [[Auśana sāḥ|Auśanasāḥ]] prescribed fines in cases where witnesses proved stupid &c. ( III. 11, 44 ). The Mahābhārata, Śāntiparva[^236] ( chap. 56,

The Anuśāsana-parva ( 139. 8-9 ) [[states.|states ‘]] The Śāstras known to Uśanas and [[Bșhaspati|Bṛhaspati]] cannot surpass the ( innate ) intelligence of women ; how can men guard them ( completely ) ’. Two verses from Śāntiparva, chap. 138. 113 and 139. 71 are interesting as having been declared [[hy|by]] Uśanas to Prahlāda viz.[^237] those that confide in persons who were once inimical are destroyed by the latter and that one should proceed cautiously even after making an alliance ( *sandhi* ). [[P267]]

The work is a brief one ( 8 folios ) in seven adhyāyas. Their contents [[are:-|are :—]] II. impurity on birth and death ; purification of certain things in certain ways such as with water, [[ashies|ashes]] &c. III. the four [[varvas|varṇas]] and the mixed castes such as Rathakāra, Ambaṣṭha, Sūta, Ugra, [[Māgalha|Māgadha]] &c., the Vrātyas ; IV. no bodily injury to be done to brāhmaṇa ; prāyaścittas for killing a brāhmaṇa or a man of the other varṇas and for other [[Mahā pātakas|Mahāpātakas]], prāyaścitta for eating the flesh of certain animals and for eating plants like garlic, prāyaścitta for adultery ; discussion whether a Brāhmaṇa could marry a śūdra woman ; prāyaścittas for killing various beasts and birds ; fourteen vidyās ; V. [[śrūddhrakalpa|śrāddhakalpa]] ; what Brāhmaṇas are *[[punkti pāvuna|paṅkti-pāvana]]* ; details of śrāddha ; food and flesh at śrāddha ; who are unfit to be invited at śrāddha ; VI. what things a Brāhmaṇa could not sell ; VII. punishments for the [[Maha pātakas|Mahāpātakas]] ; what are pure things at all times. Some of the views of the Auśanasa-dharmaśāstra are worth special notice. The son of a Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya or Vaiśya from a wife of the [[varụa|varṇa]] immediately next to it belonged to the caste of the father.[^240] No sin or punishment attached to [[ope|one]] if one killed an ātatāyi ( armed ) Brāhmaṇa. The fourteen vidyās are said to be the six [[ungas|aṅgas]] and the four Vedas, [[Mimiṇsā|Mimāṃsā]], Nyāya, Dharmaśāstra and Purāṇa.[^241] 

The work very often quotes the views of others with the words ‘ eke ’; for example it says ‘ the impurity on birth is ten nights for the parents of the child, but according to [[sume|some]] only for the mother.’[^242] The son of a Brāhmaṇa from a śūdra woman is called *pārasava*, but according to some he is *niṣāda*. None of these smṛtis in verse contains the passages in verse on vyavahāra [[ascribel|ascribed]] to [[Uśauas|Uśanas]] that occur in the Mitākṣarā, Smṛticandrikā and other works.

The Mit. ( on Yāj. III. 260 ) and Aparārka ( e. g. p. 158, 192, 255 ) contain prose passages ascribed to Uśanas and the same works contain numerous verses ascribed to Uśanas, most of which are not found in the two smṛtis in Jivananda. Some of the verses in [[Apararka|Aparārka]] ascribed to Uśanas are found in the Auśanasasmṛti ( e. g. [[Aparūrka|Aparārka]] p. 418 ‘ kuryād-aharah śrāddham ’ is Jivananda I. p. 521 ).

In other cases the verses quoted from Uśanas in [[Aparā rka|Aparārka]], though not quite identical, bear a very close resemblance ( e. g. the four verses in Aparārka on p. 450 closely resemble Jivananda I, p. 525 and have some verses in common ).

Among the views on Vyavahāra quoted from [[Ugapas|Uśanas]] are some interesting ones. It was he who said that a son was not bound to pay his father’s debt, if it was a fine or unpaid toll or what is not vyāvahārika.[^251] He holds that even blind, deaf or old men, women, minors could be witnesses in charges of [[sākasa|sāhasa]] ( Aparārka p. 671 ). He is quoted also on ordeals, on svāmipālavivāda and on steya.

Uśanas is quoted in Kalpataru ( Gṛhasthakāṇḍa ) 8 times, in Śrāddha 13 times ( 8 times in prose ), in Vyavahāra eleven times ( all verses, except one prose passage on p. 814 on anuloma unions ), 6 times in Niyatakāla.

It is interesting to note that he states that the son of a brāhmaṇa from a kṣatriya wife is a brāhmaṇa and the son of

A work styled [[Sukranitisāra|Śukranitisāra]] was edited by Oppert ( Madras 1882 ) and by Jivananda ( Cal. 1892 ) and translated by Benoy Kumar Sarkar in S. B. H. Series. That work is a very interesting one, but is comparatively of a late date.

It is probable that at least some parts of [[Sukranitisira|Śukranitisāra]] edited by Oppert in 1882 are not older than about 1800 A. D. ( [[viz,|viz.]] such as IV. 5. II prescribing fees for lawyers ). Vide Mr. Lallanji Gopal’s paper in the Bulletin of the London School of Oriental and African Studies, Vol. 25 for 1962 pp. 524-556, in which it is [[strenously|strenuously]] urged that it is a [[fabrica tion|fabrication]] of the 19th century ). I do not agree that the whole work is later than 1800 A. D. The Śukranitisāra provides on p. 157 ( of Oppert’s ed. ). ‘ नियोगितस्यापि भृतिं विवादात् षोडशांशिकीम् । विंशत्यंशां तदर्धं वा तदर्धां तदर्धिकाम् । यथा द्रव्याधिकं कार्यं हीना हीना भृतिस्तथा ॥ ’

Such passages may be interpolations made in the first quarter of the 19th century. In the year 1793 the [[Governer|Governor]]-General-in-Council enacted Regulation VII of 1793 in order to induce men of education and character to take up the practice of law as a distinct profession. That Regulation applied only to the provinces of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. In 1802 [[Regu lation|Regulation]] XIV of 1802 was enacted in Bombay which consisted of 33 sections providing among other matters for the [[appo intment|appointment]] of pleaders, for the issue of sanads to them, for remuneration payable to them and for penalties in cases of misconduct or negligence.
