Cyavana is a famous name in the Ṛgveda. The Aśvins are often praised as having endowed the old and aging Cyavana with youth ( vide Ṛg. I. 117. 13, I. 118. 6, VII. 71. 5, X. 39. 4 ). The Mahābhārata spins certain stories on Cyavana in Ādiparva, chap. 6 and 8, Vanaparva chap. 122-124, Āśvamedhika 10. 32. He married Sukanyā, daughter of king [[Saryāti|Śaryāti]], and the Aśvins made him young and handsome. He [[theu helpeil|then helped]] the [[Asvips|Aśvins]] to get a share in Soma offerings after paralysing the arm of Indra who at first opposed this.

The Mit., Aparārka and other works cite several [[sūtres|sūtras]] and some verses from Cyavana. Aparārka quotes a very long prose passage from Cyavana ( on [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] I. 207 ) about the [[proce dure|procedure]] of making the gift of a cow ( when on the point of giving birth to a calf ) and about the [[inientras|mantras]] to be recited in that ceremony. The Mit. ( [[ou Yiij.|on Yāj.]] III. 30 ) and Aparārka and [[Kulpataru|Kalpataru]] ( [[Suddhikinda|Śuddhikāṇḍa]] p. 129 ) cite a sūtra of Cyavana on the prāyaścitta for coming in contact with a dog, a [[śvapūka|śvapāka]], smoke from [[&|a]] funeral pyre, wine, wine-vessel &c. Aparārka cites prose passages of Cyavana on the [[prāyag citta|prāyaścitta]] for killing a cow ( on [[Yij.|Yāj.]] III. 264-265 ), for touching, carrying or burning the corpse of one who commits suicide ( on Yāj. III. 292 ), on the method of the purification of houses, vessels, articles of food when touched by [[Cāụdālas|Cāṇḍālas]] ( on the same verse ).
