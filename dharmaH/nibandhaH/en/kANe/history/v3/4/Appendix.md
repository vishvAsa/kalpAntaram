Long lists of high functionaries and other officers occur in several [[inscriptiong.|inscriptions.]] Vide E. I. vol. VII at p. 91 (Kahla plate of [[Sodhadeva ),|Soḍhadeva),]] E. I. vol. XII at p. 40, E. I. [[vol,|vol.]] XIV at p. 327 (grant of [[Mahipāla|Mahīpāla]]). There are about 40 titles in some [[ins criptions.|inscriptions.]] They are generally arranged in order of dignity and importance, the highest coming first. Sometimes several offices are held by the same man. For example, in the Jesar plate of [[Silāditya|Śīlāditya]] (dated in Valabhi year 357) the high officer Mammaka is styled '[[sandhivigrahadhiksta,|sandhivigrahādhikṛta,]] [[divirapati,|divīrapati,]] [[mahā pratihāra,|mahā-pratīhāra,]] sāmanta' (E. I. XXII at p. 120). It is not claimed that the following is a thoroughly exhaustive list. It is, [[how over,|however,]] exhaustive enough. Titles and dignitaries in South Indian languages have been generally omitted. Vogel stands for Dr. Vogel's work on 'Antiquities of Chamba State' where, on pp. 120-136, he furnishes informing notes on some officers. There is difference of opinion among scholars about the exact [[mean ing|meaning]] of certain office names. The individual functions of several officers are not quite clear. The references given here as to where an office occurs are not exhaustive. Chronologically older references alone are generally given and they are mostly taken (so far as inscriptions are concerned) from the [[Epigra phia|Epigraphia]] Indica (E. I.) and the Indian Antiquary (I. A.). Many official titles have the word '[[maha|mahā]]' prefixed without any change in meaning in some cases. Vide India Office Plate of [[Laksmanasena|Lakṣmaṇasena]] in his 27th year (E. I. XXVI. p. 1 at [[p.)|p. 7)]] for many official titles beginning with 'mahā'.
