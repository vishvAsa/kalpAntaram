CHAPTER XXX

STRĪDHANA

[[Stridhana ( woman’s property ).|Strīdhana (woman’s property).]] This subject abounds with differing views on several topics that fall under it, as will be [[Been|seen]] below.

The germs of the topic of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] can be traced to the Vedic Literature. As Sir Gooroodas Bannerjee remarks (in [[’ :|’]]Marriage and [[Stridhana ‘|Strīdhana’]] p. 370), ’nowhere were proprietary rights of women recognized so early as in India; and in very few ancient systems of law have these rights been so largely conceded as in our own’. The wedding hymn in the [[Rgveda ( X|Ṛgveda (X.]] 85) contains two verses1 (13 and 38) which indicate that gifts were sent to the bridegroom’s house with the bride. They are: ’the bridal gifts of Sūryā that [[Savits|Savitṛ]] sent off have gone forth; in the [[Agbās (Māgha|Aghās (Maghā]] constellation) are struck the kine and in the [[Arjunis ( Phalguni|Arjunīs (Phalgunī]] constellation) is carried (the bridal gift); for thee in the beginning they carried about [[Sūrya|Sūryā]] together with the bridal gifts [[&o.’.|&c.’.]] Sāyaṇa explains ‘vahatu’ as ‘cows and other objects given for pleasing the girl to be married’, while Lanman (Harvard O. series vol. VIII p. 753) renders it as ‘bridal car’. Sāyaṇa’s interpretation suits the context much better. In the Tai. S. VI. 2. 1. 1 we read,2 ’the wife (of the sacrificer) holds on (to the cart), for the wife is the mistress of the household gear’. Manu IX. 11 uses the word [[‘pāriṇahya’|‘pāriṇāhya’]] (household gear) and states that the wife should be entrusted among other matters with the supervision over it. Jaimini3, according to [[Sabara,|Śabara,]] refers to [[P771]] this passage of the Tai. S. for establishing that women do own certain property. Medhātithi4 on Manu VIII. 416 (cited above on p. 452) quotes this passage of the Tai. S. and argues that if Manu VIII. 416 (saying that whatever a wife earns is her husband’s) were literally understood passages like the one in Tai. S. would have no scope and that therefore Manu simply means that though women may be owners they cannot dispose of the property independently. It appears from these ancient passages that the properties, which in early days were held to be owned by women, were presents made at the time of marriage (such as ornaments and costly dresses) and household articles that are generally under the control of women and that the later [[smrti|smṛti]] rules about the devolution of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in the female line arose from the peculiar nature of the articles over which dominion was conceded to women. This early state of things is indicated by some of the early sūtras. [[Ap. Dh.|Āp. Dh.]] S.5 (II. 6. 14. 9) states the view of some predecessors (which Āp. himself does not approve) that the ornaments belong to the wife and also such wealth as came to her from her agnates (father, brother &c.). Baud. Dh. S. II. 2. 49 says that daughters get the [[orna ments|ornaments]] of their mother and whatever else it is customary for them to get. [[Vag. 17. 46|Vas. 17. 46]] prescribes that [[women ( daughters )|women (daughters)]] should divide the nuptial presents given to their mother. [[Saṅkha (q. by Sam. Pr. p. 851 )|Śaṅkha (q. by Saṁ. Pr. p. 851)]] prescribes that in all forms of marriage ornaments and [[stridhana|strīdhana]] should be given to the girl. It may be that Manu VIII. 416 only gives expression to an ancient opinion, that the literal meaning of that [[verge|verse]] was given up long before and that all that Manu meant was that during coverture a woman was subject to the control of the husband as regards her [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]]

Three principal topics have to be discussed under [[stridhana|strīdhana]] viz. what constitutes [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] woman’s dominion over her [[P772]] [[stridhana|strīdhana]] and inheritance to [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] On each one of these topics great diversity of views prevails and the subject has become very complicated, as the [[Dayabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] remarks.6

Gautama contains three sūtras about the devolution of [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] but he does not describe it.7 [[Kaut.|Kauṭ.]] ([[III 2.|III. 2.]] [[p. 152 )|p. 152)]] defines:8 ‘means of subsistence and what could be tied on the body [[(i, e.|(i. e.]] ornaments and [[jewellery )|jewellery)]] constitute [[stridhana, The|strīdhana. The]] means of subsistence should be fixed at 2000 [[(panas)|(paṇas)]] as the maximum, while there is no limit to (what may be given) as [[ābadhya ( ornaments &c.)’.|ābadhya (ornaments &c.)’.]] This may be [[com pared|compared]] with a verse of Kāt. (902) and a similar9 [[verge|verse]] of Vyāsa viz. [[’ the|‘the]] father, the mother, the husband, brother and kinsmen should give [[stridhana|strīdhana]] to a woman according to their means up to two thousand [[(panas )|(paṇas)]] except immovable [[pro perty’.|property’.]] The Sm. C. and V. Mayūkha explain that this limit of 2000 applies to gifts made every year, but that if a gift is to be made once for all, then more may be given and even immovable property may be given.

The term [[stridhana|strīdhana]] literally means [[’ woman’s property ‘.|‘woman’s property’.]] But in the ancient [[smrti|smṛti]] works the word was restricted to certain special kinds of property given to a woman on certain occasions or at different stages of her life. Gradually such kinds of property went on increasing in extent and value. We have to follow this evolution of the meaning and content of the word [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] One characteristic feature of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] from the times of Gautama downwards has been that it devolved on females in the first instance. The oldest extant definition of it [[P773]] in Dharmaśāstra works is that of Manu10 IX. 194: ‘what (was given) before the nuptial fire, what was given on the bridal procession, what was given in token of love, what was received from her brother, mother or father, that is called the sixfold woman’s property’. The Mit. on [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] II. 143 explains that the number six is intended to exclude a smaller number of the kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] and does not exclude a larger number. In IX. 195 Manu appears to mention one more kind viz. [[’ anvā-dheya ‘|‘anvādheya’]] (gift subsequent)’. Nār. (dāyabhāga, 8) has the same six kinds as Manu IX. 194, but reads [[’ bhartrdāya ‘|‘bhartṛdāya’]] (the husband’s gift) for Manu’s [[’ what|‘what]] is given in token [[of affection ‘,|of affection’,]] the latter being thus wider than Nārada’s words. [[Yāj. (II. 143- 44 )|Yāj. (II. 143-144)]] enumerates11 the several kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as follows: ‘what was given (to a woman) by the father, mother, husband or brother or was received by her before the nuptial fire, or what was presented on her husband’s marrying another wife [[( adhivedanika )|(ādhivedanika)]] and the like—these are denominated [[stridhana ;|strīdhana;]] so also what is given by the cognate relations (of the woman), the [[sulka|śulka]] (fee), and gifts subsequent to marriage’. The Mit. and most of its followers read [[’ adhivedanikādyam ca ‘,|‘ādhivedanikādyam ca’,]] while Aparārka and the Dāyabhāga read [[’ adhivedanikam caiva ‘,|‘ādhivedanikam caiva’,]] the difference being that if one reads as the Mit. does, several other kinds not expressly enumerated can be easily included under [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] But it should be noted that even without reading [[’- kādyam ‘,|’—kādyam’,]] Aparārka explains the verse in the same way as the Mit. does. [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]]12 Dh. S. 17. 18 enumerates almost the same kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as [[Yaj.,|Yāj.,]] but omits the word ‘ādya’.

Among [[smrti|smṛti]] writers Kātyāyana gives the most elaborate treatment of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in about twenty-seven verses. He [[P774]] defines several kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] enumerated by Manu, [[Yaj.,|Yāj.,]] [[Nār.,|Nār.]] and [[Viṣnu|Viṣṇu]] (in all six) as follows:13 ‘what is given to women at the time of marriage before the nuptial fire is declared by the wise to be adhyagni14 [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] That again which a woman obtains when she is being taken (in [[ā|a]] procession) from her father’s house (to the bridegroom’s) is declared to be [[adhya vahanika|adhyāvahanika]] [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] Whatever is given to a woman through affection by the father-in-law or [[mother-in law|mother-in-law]] and what is received by her at the time of doing obeisance at the feet (of elders) is said to be [[pritidatta|prītidatta]] (gift through affection) [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] That is declared to be [[sulka|śulka]] (bride’s fee) which is obtained as the price of household utensils, of beasts of burden, of milch cattle, ornaments and slaves. Whatever is obtained by a woman after marriage from the family of her husband as also from the family of her (father’s) [[kingmen|kinsmen]] is said to be anvādheya (gift subsequent). According to Bhṛgu whatever is obtained by a woman after marriage through affection from her husband or from her parents is [[anvādheya ‘.|anvādheya’.]] It may be noted that [[Katya yana’s|Kātyāyana’s]] definitions of adhyagni and adhyāvahanika are wide [[P775]] enough to include gifts made even by strangers on those occasions.

These definitions given by Kātyāyana have been accepted by all digests including even the [[Dayabhāga.|Dāyabhāga.]]

“That is known as [[suudāyika|saudāyika]] which is obtained by a married woman or by a maiden in her husband’s or father’s house from her brother or from her parents” (Kāt.). Some of the divergences in readings and in interpretation deserve to be noted. Acc. to the reading adopted by the Mit. adhyāvahanika includes all gifts made by [[any one|anyone]] when the newly married girl is taken from her father’s house in a procession, while the reading adopted by the [[Dāyabhāga 14%|Dāyabhāga]]15 and some others [[( paitȚkāt )|(paitṛkāt)]] [[restriots|restricts]] the adhyāvahanika to gifts made by the father’s or [[inother’s|mother’s]] family only. The V. R. [[( p. 523 )|(p. 523)]] includes under [[adhyāvāhanika|adhyāvahanika]] also the gifts made by the father-in-law and others when the girl is taken back to her father’s house and the V. C. [[( p. 138 )|(p. 138)]] says that it is what is obtained at the time of [[’ dvirūgamana ‘.|‘dvirāgamana’.]] The Dāyabhāga16 (IV. 3. 19-20 p. 93) explains [[’ dohyabharapa-karmiṇām ‘|‘dohyābharaṇa-karmiṇām’]] differently as ’the [[gratifica tion|gratification]] paid by house-builders or goldsmiths to induce her to press her husband to engage [[thern ‘|them’]] or as Vyāsa says ‘what is given to a woman to induce her (cheerfully) to go to her husband’s [[house ‘.|house’.]] The Sm. C. and V. P. explain [[sulka|śulka]] as the price of the articles which the bridegroom was in the habit of presenting to the bride at the time of marriage or when he started a house.17 The V. [[P776]] Nir. (p. 468) states that [[sulka|śulka]] has two [[sensos ;|senses;]] (1) what is given as the price of the girl to the guardians of the girl which goes to the mother and brother (on her death); [[(%)|(2)]] what is given (by the bridegroom) as the price of the ornaments for the girl and of the household gear.18 Another [[verge|verse]] of Kāt. (904) has been quoted by many writers, which restricts the literal sense of the word [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] ‘over that wealth that is obtained by a woman by mechanical arts [[(such as spinning )|(such as spinning)]] or from a stranger through affection, the husband has [[ownership.|ownership,]] [[P777]] the rest is declared to be [[stridhana|strīdhana]]’.19 The prima facie view [[(purvapakṣa)|(pūrvapakṣa)]] in Jai. VI. 1. 12 urges that what a woman gets by serving cooked food or by cutting clothes does not belong to her. The gifts from strangers here referred to are other than those made by a stranger before the nuptial fire or at the time of the bridal procession. This definition makes it clear that what is obtained even from a stranger by a maiden or by a woman who is a widow at the time of the gift is pure [[stridhana,|strīdhana.]] Devala says, ‘maintenance (what was given for maintenance), ornaments, [[sulka (bride’s gratuity ),|śulka (bride’s gratuity),]] the profits of money-lending are a woman’s [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] She alone is entitled to enjoy it and the husband is not entitled to enjoy it except in the case of distress’.20 Manu IX. 200 states, [[’ the|‘the]] heirs of the husband should not divide [[( among themselves )|(among themselves)]] the ornaments worn by women during the lifetime of their husband; if they divide them they incur sin’. In the printed editions of Manu there is no comment of Medhātithi on this [[Verse,|verse,]] but the V. R. (p. 509), V. C. p. 139, [[Dayatattva|Dāyatattva]] p. 184 all say that acc. to Medhātithi [[oven|even]] ornaments, though not donated expressly, become [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] if worn with the husband’s consent.21 According to the [[Vaijayanti|Vaijayantī]] such ornaments should be assigned to the share of the woman’s husband so that she alone might put them on and that it is not meant that such ornaments are not to be taken into account at all at the time of partition.

[[P778]] Saudāyika is not a special kind of [[stridhana, It|strīdhana. It]] is rather a comprehensive term for several kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as the [[defi nition|definition]] given by [[Kat.|Kāt.]] shows and as the [[v.C.(p. 139)|V. C. (p. 139)]] expressly states.22 It is almost synonymous with [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in the [[tech nical|technical]] sense. Acc. to most writers, it is wealth received by a woman, whether as a maiden or as a married woman, in her father’s or husband’s house from her parents or relatives of the father and mother. The Sm. C. II. p. 282 and V. R. p. 511 hold that saudāyika is all wealth obtained by a woman whether as a [[maidon|maiden]] or as a married woman from her mother, father or paternal relatives only, while the [[Diyabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] IV. 1. 23 pp. 76-77 and V. C. hold that saudāyika includes also all property donated by the husband except immovable property, which latter she cannot alienate even after her husband’s death. There is a [[Verse|verse]] of Vyāsa23 that defines [[saudayika|saudāyika]] similarly, ‘whatever is obtained by a woman as a maiden at the time of marriage and after marriage from the house of her father or husband is termed [[saudayika.’|saudāyika.’]] The word [[’ saudayika ‘|‘saudāyika’]] is derived [[from sudaya ‘|from ‘sudāya’]] and means, according to the [[Dayabhaga|Dāyabhāga]] IV. 1. 22 p. 76, [[’ received|‘received]] from affectionate [[kindred ‘.|kindred’.]] The [[Amarakosa Bays|Amarakośa says]] that [[’ sudaya ‘|‘sudāya’]] means [[’ gifts|‘gifts]] of yautaka and the [[like ‘|like’]] and that [[saudayaka|saudāyaka]] is merely a derivative without change of meaning.24

Another word requiring explanation is ‘yautaka’. It occurs in Manu IX. 131, ‘whatever is the yautaka of the mother goes to the maiden daughter’ (and not to the married daughter or son). So yautaka seems to be a synonym for [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in [[P779]] the technical sense. The Sm. C.25 II p. 285, Madanaratna (folio 105 b) and V. Mayūkha remark ‘yautaka is that wealth that is received as a gift from anybody by a woman while she is seated together with her husband at the time of marriage and the like’. It is derived from yuta (joined). It should, however, be noted that Yāj II. 149 employs the word yautaka as an adjective in the sense of ‘separated’ and that Medhātithi on Manu IX. 131 explains ‘yautaka’ as the separate property of a woman, her [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] The Sm. C. II. p. 285 remarks that Devasvāmi derived ‘yautaka’ from ‘yu’ (to separate) and held it to mean ‘wealth obtained from the family of the woman’s father which is separate in its characteristics’. The V. C. p. 142 and Vivādacandra p. 74 read ‘yautuka’ and the former explains it as gifts from the father and the like at the time of marriage. The Dāyatattva (p. 186) states that both forms ‘yautaka’ and ‘yautuka’ are in vogue and relies on Vācaspati and [[Rāyamukuta|Rāyamukuṭa]] in support.

[[Kaut. ( III. 2. p. 153 )|Kauṭ. (III. 2. p. 153)]] names [[sulka,|śulka,]] anvādheya, [[adhivedanika|ādhivedanika]] and bandhudatta as kinds of [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]]

The above are the principal [[smrti|smṛti]] texts on the question of what constitutes [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] It will be seen that the [[smrtis|smṛtis]] only enumerate and describe certain kinds of property as [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] which word is not used by them in the etymological sense of ‘all kinds of property possessed by a woman’. They do not attempt a comprehensive definition of [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] From the texts of the [[smrtis|smṛtis]] it follows that [[stridhana|strīdhana]] was a technical term, which at first included only six kinds of property, then nine and ultimately by the time of Kātyāyana it included all property (whether movable or immovable) obtained by a woman, either as a maiden or at marriage or after marriage, [[P780]] from her parents or the family or relatives of the parents or from the husband and his family (except immovable property given by the husband) and that what was obtained by a woman after marriage by her own labour or from strangers did not become [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] Most [[smrtis|smṛtis]] employ the word ‘datta’ (donated), but Kāt. and Vyāsa sometimes use the word ‘obtained’ ([[prāpto|prāpta]] or [[labdha ),|labdha),]] which is ordinarily wider than ‘datta’. But it is clear that the commentators do not expressly say that ‘obtained’ includes ‘inherited’, though from their general remarks to be quoted below it is arguable that they probably included inherited property under property obtained.

This is [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in a technical sense.

It is now necessary to find how [[stridhana|strīdhana]] was defined by the several commentaries and digests that are held authoritative in the several schools. This is a most important matter for practical purposes, since the courts have to follow the opinions of commentators26 that are authoritative in each school and are not at liberty to put their own construction upon ancient [[smrti|smṛti]] texts, if such construction would run counter to the express opinions of the authoritative commentators. First then comes the [[Mitaksarā.|Mitākṣarā.]] Its gloss on Yāj. II. 143 may be translated as follows:27 ‘whatever is given by the father, mother, husband and brother; what was presented by the maternal uncle and the like at the time of marriage before the nuptial fire; and ādhivedanika, that is, gift made (by the husband) at the time of marrying a second wife as will be described subsequently in the words ‘he should give to the wife that is superseded’ (Yāj. II. 148); by the word ‘ādya’ (meaning ‘and the like’) is indicated property that is obtained by succession, purchase, partition, seizure or finding—all this is declared by Manu and others to be [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] The word [[stridhana|strīdhana]] is employed here in its etymological sense and not in a technical sense, since, when the etymological sense is possible, it is improper to resort to the technical sense.’ The Mitākṣarā expanded the [[P781]] definition of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] so as to include under it the five kinds of property of which a man becomes owner in various ways according to Gautama X. 39. The result is that, according to the Mit., property of any description belonging to a woman becomes [[stridhana|strīdhana]] even if it be inherited by her from a male as a widow or as a mother or even if it be obtained at a partition by a wife or mother (acc. to Yāj. II. 115 or 123 respectively). The same interpretation of the word ‘ādya’ has been accepted by several works, such as the Madanapārijāta (p. 671), the S. V. (p. 379), V. P. (p. 542), the Bālambhaṭṭī. Aparārka without reading ‘ādya’ gave the same explanation of ‘ca’ as stated above (on p. 773). On the other hand, the [[Dayabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] gave a restricted meaning to the word. [[Jimūtavāhang|Jīmūtavāhana]] reads Yāj. II. 143 as ‘ādhivedanikam caiva’, quotes the verse of Devala (in note 1501), remarks that [[stridhana|strīdhana]] is not restricted to the six kinds of Manu (IX. 194) but includes other kinds mentioned by other [[smrtis|smṛtis]] and finally28 observes: ’that alone is [[stridhana|strīdhana]] which a woman has authority to donate, sell or enjoy independently of her husband’s control’. The Dāyabhāga does not expressly state the kinds of property that a woman can dispose of independently of her husband, but immediately after defining [[stridhana|strīdhana]] it quotes the texts of Kāt. (about earnings from mechanical arts and gifts from strangers) and Nār. (IV. 28), ‘what has been given to a wife by her loving husband, she may spend or give away as she likes even after his death excepting immovables’. From this it follows that, according to the [[Dāya bhāga,|Dāyabhāga,]] all gifts from relations except a gift of immovable property made by the husband and gifts from even strangers made before the nuptial fire or on the bridal procession constitute [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] but property inherited by a woman or [[obtained:on, partitiou,|obtained on partition,]] gifts from strangers (other than the two kinds noted above) and property acquired by her by mechanical arts or by her labour are not [[strīdhana, The Dayatattva olosely|strīdhana. The Dāyatattva closely]] follows the [[Dayabhāga.|Dāyabhāga.]]

The Sm. C. does not give a [[defintion of stridbana,|definition of strīdhana,]] but it does not adopt the interpretation of the word ‘ādya’ given by [[P782]] the Mit. Therefore it may be said that it is in line with the [[Dayabbāga.|Dāyabhāga.]] The Par. M. [[IIL|III.]] p. 54729 (another Madras authority) appears to follow the Mit., since it says: “[[“adya’|"‘ādya’]] includes [[adhivedanika’|‘ādhivedanika’]] and what is acquired by inheritance, sale and the like.” The [[Vivādacintamani|Vivādacintāmaṇi]] (the leading authority in [[Mithila )|Mithilā)]] does not define [[stridbana|strīdhana]] in general, but enumerates the several kinds of [[strldhana|strīdhana]] described by Manu, Yāj., Viṣṇu, [[Kat., and Davala|Kāt., and Devala]] and so is on a par with the [[Dayabhāga.|Dāyabhāga.]] The V. Mayūkha divides [[stridhana|strīdhana]] into two kinds, [[pāridhāṣika (tech nical)|pāribhāṣika (technical)]] and [[aparibhā șika ( non-technical).|apāribhāṣika (non-technical).]] The former comprises properties expressly stated to be [[stridhana|strīdhana]] by the sages, while the latter includes all other property belonging to a woman (except what is called technical) and acquired by her by partition or cutting (sewing or other mechanical arts).30 This division is peculiar to the V. Mayūkha. The [[Viramitrodaya|Vīramitrodaya]] (the paramount authority in the [[Benaros|Benares]] school) agrees with Mit. as stated above.

It is beyond the scope of this work to go deeply into modern [[caselaw, But|caselaw. But]] it is necessary to show how the Indian Courts have in modern times dealt with [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as defined by the commentators. It may be said at once that the inclusion by the [[Mite|Mit.]] of property inherited by a woman or obtained at a partition under [[stridbana|strīdhana]] has been totally discarded by the Privy Council for all provinces in India except Bombay.31 A woman may inherit property from a male, such as from her husband, father or son or she may inherit property from a female, that is, from her mother, daughter &c. Both these kinds of property [[P783]] are [[stridbana|strīdhana]] according to the Mit., but the Privy Council has held as to both these kinds that they are not [[stridhana;|strīdhana;]] e. g. if a daughter inherits property from her father or inherits the [[stridhana property of hér. mother,|strīdhana property of her mother,]] it does not become her [[stri dhana,|strīdhana,]] that on the death of the daughter, the property does not pass to her heirs, but to the next heir of the person from whom she inherited it. The Bombay school makes a distinction. If a woman enters a family by marriage and then inherits to a male of that family (as e. g. a widow succeeding to her husband, a mother to her son or a predeceased son’s widow to her [[father in-law),|father-in-law),]] the property she inherits is not her [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] she takes only a limited estate in it and on her death it passes to the next [[hoir|heir]] of the male from whom she inherited. But if a woman inherits from a male belonging to the family in which she was born (e. g. a daughter succeeding to her father, a sister to her brother) or if a woman inherits property from a female, the property so [[inheritad|inherited]] becomes her [[stridbana|strīdhana]] (in Bombay). As to property acquired by a wife or mother on partition32, it has been held by the Privy Council that it is not [[stridhana|strīdhana]] even in provinces governed by the [[Mitakṣarā|Mitākṣarā]] (including Bombay).

Kāt. (903) declares:33 ‘whatever was given to a woman for wearing only on some occasion (or on condition) or with fraudulent intent by the father, brother or husband is not held to be [[stridhana.’|strīdhana.’]] The idea is that if the father or husband gives some ornament to his daughter or wife for wearing on some special occasion [[(upādhi )|(upādhi)]] or if a father or husband in fraud of his co-parceners gives some family property to his daughter or wife it cannot become [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]]

[[Dominion over stridhana.“What is stridhana and what dominion a woman has over stridbana depend on three things,|Dominion over strīdhana.—What is strīdhana and what dominion a woman has over strīdhana depend on three things,]] viz. the source of acquiring the property, her status at the time of acquisition (i. e. whether she was a maiden or a married woman whose husband was living or a widow), the school of law by which she is governed. [[Cortain|Certain]] verses of [[Kat,|Kāt.]] and [[Nar.|Nār.]] are the principal texts on this subject. Kāt. (905-907, 911) [[P784]] [[Bays:|says]]34 ‘on obtaining wealth of the saudāyika kind it is [[dec lared (lit. desired)|declared (lit. desired)]] that women have independent power over it, since it was given by them (the kindred) as support (or [[maintenance )|maintenance)]] in order that they (women) may not be reduced to a terrible (or wretched) condition. It has been declared that women always have independence in saudāyika property as [[re gards|regards]] sale or gift at their pleasure and even in immovables [[(if sandāyika).|(if saudāyika).]] A woman, when her husband is dead, may deal with the gift (of movables) given by the husband as she pleases, but she should preserve it as long as he is alive or she may spend it on his family. Neither the husband nor the son, nor the father nor the brothers have power to use or alienate the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] of a woman’. The [[verge|verse]] of [[Kat.|Kāt.]] about property [[acquir ed|acquired]] by labour or about the gift from strangers through affection and that of Nārada about the gift of immovable property made by the husband have already been quoted. From these texts it follows that a Hindu woman during her maidenhood35 could dispose of her [[stridhana|strīdhana]] property of every description at her pleasure, that during her widowhood she could dispose of every kind of [[stridbana|strīdhana]] including movable property given by the husband but not immovable property given by him and that a married woman whose husband was living could dispose of at her pleasure only that kind of property called [[saudūyika|saudāyika]] [[(i. e,| (i. e.]] gifts from relations except those made by the husband). Under modern decisions the distinction between saudāyika and [[P785]] non-saudāyika is retained,36 but the distinction between saudāyika given by the relations and that given by the husband is not kept.37 Now the woman’s power to dispose off as she likes depends on the question whether the gift was meant to pass an absolute estate or a limited estate even if the husband was the donor. During marriage when the husband is living her dominion depends under modern law upon the character of the [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] If it is of the kind called saudāyika she can dispose of it by sale, gift or will as she likes without her husband’s consent; but as to other kinds of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] property (such as gifts from strangers, property acquired by mechanical arts or property inherited) she cannot dispose of it without her husband’s consent. If she disposes of it without such consent the alienation is void. According to the D. B. IV. 1. 20 property acquired by a married woman by mechanical arts or by gift from a stranger is subject to the husband’s dominion during his life and may be taken by him even when there is no distress. No one else (except the husband) has got control over even such [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] After the husband’s death, she can dispose of even non-saudāyika [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as she likes. But even as regards saudāyika the texts [[coucede|concede]] certain rights to the husband in certain circumstances. Yāj. II. 147 says: ’the husband is not liable to return to the wife her [[stridhana|strīdhana]] taken (i. e. used) by him in a famine, for (indispensable) religious acts, in disease, or when imprisoned (by the creditor or by the king or an enemy)’.38

The meaning of [[’ mithunaṃ|‘mithunaṁ]] [[prajātayoh ‘|prajātayoḥ’]] and [[’ trivarsopabhuktam ‘|‘trivarṣopabhuktaṁ’]] in [[Kaut.|Kauṭ.]] is not quite clear. [[Succession to Stridhana.–The|Succession to Strīdhana.—The]] several schools of Hindu Law differ from each other perhaps more widely on this subject than [[P789]] on any other. But one thing is generally common to all schools viz. that as regards succession to [[stridhana|strīdhana]] daughters are [[prefer red|preferred]] to sons, though in later days sons were introduced by some writers as heirs along with daughters or even as preferable to daughters for certain kinds of [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] This was probably due to the fact that the value of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] property became considerable in later times and society which held to the doctrine of the dependence and inferior position of women could not tolerate the idea that large estates should go to women alone. The causes of the variance among writers on the devolution of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] are historical. The text-writers wrote at different times and in different countries and probably reflect the popular sentiments of their own countries and times tinged now and then by personal predilections also. The digests sometimes expressly say that their interpretation follows the usage of their times (vide V. Mayūkha in note 1529 below).

The succession to [[stridhana|strīdhana]] varies according as the woman is unmarried or married, according as the marriage was in an approved or an unapproved form, and also according to the species of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] and the school of law to which she is subject.

First, the important texts of the [[smstis|smṛtis]] on succession to [[stridhana|strīdhana]] may be brought together. The oldest extant text is that of [[Gaut. 28.22|Gaut. 28. 22]] which provides,48 ‘strīdhana devolves (first) on daughters; [[(in a competition among daughters )|(in a competition among daughters)]] it goes to those that are unmarried (apratta, lit. not given away in [[marriage )|(marriage)]] and [[( in a competition among married daughters )|(in a competition among married daughters)]] to those that are not well provided for (i. e. that are indigent).’ [[Manu (IX. 192-193 ) provides :|Manu (IX. 192-193) provides:]] ‘when the mother dies all the full brothers and full sisters should equally divide the mother’s estate. Even to the daughters of those daughters some thing should be given (that is) as much as would be seemly out of the estate of their grandmother on the ground of [[affection’. In Mapu|affection’. In Manu]] IX. 195 it is said that the six kinds of [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] as also anvādheya [[strldhana|strīdhana]] and gifts made by the [[busband|husband]] through affection, should go to her progeny if she dies in her husband’s lifetime. The verses of Manu (IX. 192-3) are variously explained by the commentators, [[Sarvajña-Nara yana|Sarvajña-Nārāyaṇa]] holding that mother’s estate means estate other than technical [[stridbana.|strīdhana.]] Most of them rely on a text of Bṛ. and [[P790]] hold that full brothers and unmarried sisters succeed together and married sisters (i. e. daughters of the woman whose heirs are to be found) get some trifle ([[# of each brother aco,|1/4 of each brother acc.]] to Kullūka). Manu IX. 196-197 further provide that when a woman was married in one of the five forms of marriage viz. [[Brahma, Daiva, Arsa, Prajapatya and Gandharva|Brāhma, Daiva, Ārṣa, Prājāpatya and Gāndharva]] and died without issue her [[stridhana|strīdhana]] goes to her husband and if she was married in the [[Asura|Āsura]] and the other two forms it went, if she died issueless, to her mother and father. [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] II.49 117 [[pro vides|provides]] that daughters take the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] estate of their mother and on failure of them the (male) issue. In II.50 144 [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] again says that [[stridhana|strīdhana]] goes to daughters, but that if the woman dies without issue then it goes to the husband if she was married in one of the four forms [[(brahma, daiva, arsa, praja patya )|(brāhma, daiva, ārṣa, prājāpatya)]] but to her parents if she was married in the other four forms. Viṣṇu Dh. S. (17. 19-21) and [[Nar. ( dayabhāga, 9)|Nār. (dāyabhāga, 9)]] contain the same rules as [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] II. 144-145, while [[Nar. (daya bhaga2)|Nār. (dāyabhāga 2)]] states that the mother’s wealth should be divided among the daughters and in the absence of daughters the latter’s issue takes it. [[Saṅkha-Likhita.|Śaṅkha-Likhita]]51 declare that the mother’s estate is taken in equal shares by the full brothers (sons of the deceased mother) and their unmarried sisters. [[Bç,|Bṛ.]]52 (S. B. E. [[P791]] 33 p. 383 verse 87) declares, [[’ stridhana|‘strīdhana]] goes to her progeny, but the unmarried daughter is preferred, while the married one gets only some trifle as a token of regard’. Parāśara holds that the unmarried daughters take all the [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] but in a competition between married daughters alone and sons they all take equal shares. Devala53 states, ‘on the death of a woman her [[stridhana|strīdhana]] is taken in equal shares by her sons and daughters; if she leaves no issue it would be taken by her husband, mother, brother or father’. Pāraskara54 (q. by Par. M. III. 552) states that [[stridhana|strīdhana]] devolves on the unmarried daughter, that the son does not get it, but that he shares equally with a married daughter. [[Kaut.|Kauṭ.]]55 (III. 2 p. 153) prescribes that if a woman dies during her husband’s lifetime her sons and daughters divide her [[stridhana;|strīdhana;]] if she has no son then daughters divide it, on failure (of both sons and daughters) the husband takes it and that the [[sulka,|śulka,]] anvādheya or any other kind of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] given by her relatives is taken by her relatives. Kātyāyana56 (917-920) whose treatment of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] is the most detailed of all [[smrti|smṛti]] writers has the following verses on succession to [[stridhana :|strīdhana:]] ‘Sisters whose husbands are living should share with their brothers the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] (of their mother); this is the rule of law about [[stridhana|strīdhana]] and partition (among brothers and sisters) is prescribed. On failure of daughters the [[( stridhana )|(strīdhana)]] wealth devolves on sons (of the deceased woman); the wealth given to a woman by her kinsmen (paternal or maternal) goes in the absence of the kinsmen (that gave it) to the husband. [[P792]] Whatever immovable property was given by the parents to their daughter always goes to her brother if she dies without issue. Whatever wealth was obtained by a woman from her parents in the forms of marriage beginning with āsura is declared as going to her parents on failure of her progeny.’57 The first two verses are apparently in conflict and must be read with Gaut. 28. 22. So probably the following propositions were laid down by Kāt. viz. (1) unmarried daughters are preferred; (2) married daughters whose husbands are living share along with their brothers, if there be no unmarried daughter; (3) widowed daughters take only if there are no daughters whose husbands are living or if there are no sons; (4) wealth given by paternal and maternal kinsmen goes to them and on failure of these, to the husband; (5) immovable property given by parents goes to the brother of the woman on failure of issue; (6) wealth given in the āsura, rākṣasa and paiśāca forms goes to parents on failure of issue. Yama has a verse very similar to Kātyāyana’s about a woman married in the āsura and the following forms. The commentaries and digests make valiant efforts to reconcile the conflicts among the [[smrti|smṛti]] texts quoted above. It is not quite necessary to go into that question here in detail for fear of encumbering this work too much.

How the devolution of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] was dealt with by the commentators has now to be seen. They all prefer daughters to sons for certain kinds of [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] Why the devolution of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] property should be different from that of the property of a male is not clearly explained anywhere. The Mit. on Yāj.58 II. 117 gives the reason that woman’s property goes to the daughter because in her more particles of the mother’s body inhere than in the son. Probably the daughters were preferred as heirs to [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as a sort of equity, when sons were allowed to exclude daughters in the inheritance to the father’s wealth.

According to the Mit. there are two lines of succession to [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] one for [[sulka|śulka]] and the other for all other kinds of [[stridhana. Sulka,|strīdhana. Śulka,]] acc. to the Mit. that quotes Gaut., devolves first [[P793]] on the full brothers, then on the mother.59 Some like the [[Subodhini, the Dipakalikā,|Subodhinī, the Dīpakalikā,]] Haradatta on Gaut. 28. 23 hold that it devolves on the mother first and then on the full brothers; but many works such as the Dāyabhāga IV. 3. 28 (p. 95), Sm. C., Par. M., V. P., V. C. follow the Mit. It is somewhat strange that the Madanapārijāta p. 668 (which was really composed by the author of the [[Subodhini|Subodhinī]] in the name of his patron [[Madanapāla,|Madanapāla),]] holds that the [[sulka|śulka]] is first taken by the brothers and then by the mother. This raises a doubt whether the printed [[Subodhini|Subodhinī]] is correct or whether the author changed his opinion.

As regards succession to the property of a maiden there is no difference at all between the Mit. and other works. The Mit. quotes a text of Baudhāyana60 that the wealth of a deceased maiden first devolves on her full brothers, then on the mother and then on the father. The V. P. adds that on failure of the father the maiden’s wealth goes to the parents’ nearest [[sapipda.|sapiṇḍa.]]61 Yāj. II. 146 provides that if a girl who had been promised in marriage dies before marriage, the intending bridegroom was entitled to take back the [[sulka|śulka]] or other gifts [[P794]] made by him after deducting his own expenses and those of her family.

The [[Mitakṣard|Mitākṣarā]] order of succession for all kinds of [[stridhana,|strīdhana,]] other than [[sulka|śulka]] and maiden’s property, is as [[foliows:|follows:]] (1) unmarried daughter; (2) married daughter who is indigent; (3) married daughter who is well provided for; (4) daughter’s daughters; (5) daughter’s [[Bon;|son;]] (6) sons; (7) son’s sons (here also the rule of per stirpes [[applies );|applies);]] (8) husband [[( if|(if]] the woman was married in one of the four approved [[forns);|(forms);]] (9) [[sapiṇdas|sapiṇḍas]] of the husband in the order of propinquity; on failure of any [[sapipda|sapiṇḍa]] of the husband, her mother, then her father and then the [[sapindas|sapiṇḍas]] of the father (before it goes to the Crown). But if the woman was married in one of the unapproved forms, then on failure of her [[descen dants|descendants]] her [[stridhana|strīdhana]] went to her mother, then to her father and then to the father’s [[sapiṇdas|sapiṇḍas]] in the order of propinquity. On failure of the [[sapiṇdas|sapiṇḍas]] of the father it would (by analogy) go to her husband and then to his [[sapiṇdas ( before going to the Orown).|sapiṇḍas (before going to the Crown).]] When grand-daughters directly inherit the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] of their grandmother they take per stirpes [[(aco.|(acc.]] to the Mit. which quotes Gaut. 28. 15), if they are the daughters of several [[daughters:|daughters.]]62 The Mit. It is not possible nor necessary to deal exhaustively with the devolution of [[stridhana|strīdhana]] according to the various schools of Hindu Law. But a few indications from the important text [[P795]] books having authority in the different provinces will be offered. According to the Sm. C. (which is the leading authority in Madras after the Mit.) C.) as to the devolution of Anvādheyaka and Bhartṛdatta and holds that certain kinds of non-technical [[stridhana|strīdhana]] (such as gifts by strangers during coverture) devolve acc. to the rules of the Mit. The [[Vivādacintāmani,|Vivādacintāmaṇi,]] the leading authority in Mithilā, restricts [[stridhana|strīdhana]] to the technical [[stridhana|strīdhana]] recognized by the [[smrtis|smṛtis]] and lays down the devolution of it as follows: (1) [[Sulka|Śulka]]64 (defined as property received by a woman at the time of her marriage, if in an unapproved form) passes as under the Mit. cited above; (2) Yautaka passes first to unmarried daughters, then in the way laid down by the Mit. for all [[stridhana ;|strīdhana;]] (3) all technical [[stridhana|strīdhana]] other than the two [[præceding|preceding]] varieties passes to sons and unmarried daughters together, then to sons and married daughters jointly, then to daughter’s daughters, then to daughter’s sons and then as under the Mit. system (for [[stridhana|strīdhana]] in general).

The Vyavahāramayūkha which is held to be of [[para mount|paramount]] authority in Gujarat, Bombay Island and Northern [[P796]] Konkan65 proposes various lines of succession to [[stridhana.|strīdhana.]] Its treatment is based on the Sm. C. and the Madanaratna. It first divides [[stridhana|strīdhana]] as stated above into technical ([[pāribhāṣika|pāribhāṣika)]] and non-technical ([[apāribhāṣika|apāribhāṣika)]]. The first is subdivided into four classes for the purpose of inheritance; (I) [[sulka|śulka]] which passes as stated above under the Mitākṣarā; (II) yautaka goes to unmarried daughters, (if there be no unmarried [[daughters )|daughters)]] [[P797]] then probably to the married daughters; (III) [[anvādheya 1445 a|anvādheya]]66 and [[bhatrpriti-datta|bhartṛprīti-datta]] (husband’s gift of affection) are inherited by sons and unmarried daughters together in accordance with Manu (IX. 192 and 195), (if there be no unmarried daughters) by sons and married daughters, then daughter’s issue, then son’s sons; (IV) other67 kinds of technical [[stridhana|strīdhana]] devolves as under the Mit.; (V) non-technical68 [[stridhana|strīdhana]] is inherited in the following order; sons, son’s sons, [[sons’ eons’ sons,|sons’ sons’ sons,]] daughters, daughters’ sons, daughter’s daughters. (III) [[Anyādheya|Anvādheya]] gifts72 (gifts or bequests) made subsequent to marriage by the father [[P799]] [[(Dayabhaga IV. 2.12-16 pp. 92-93)|(Dāyabhāga IV. 2. 12-16 pp. 92-93)]] devolve in the same order as for Yautaka except in a few respects, viz. the son takes before married daughters and in case the woman dies without issue the brother, mother, father and husband succeed in that order. IV. As regards Ayautaka (i. e. all technical [[stridhana|strīdhana]] other than the above three) there is a conflict of authority between the [[Dayabhāga (IV. 2. 1-12 pp. 79-81 )|Dāyabhāga (IV. 2. 1-12 pp. 79-81)]] on the one hand and Raghunandana and [[Śrikrṣna|Śrīkṛṣṇa]] on the other. Acc. to the former the order73 is: son and kumārī daughter (or to either on failure of the other); (on failure of both) married daughters that have sons or are likely to have sons; son’s sons; daughter’s sons; barren and widowed daughters. But Raghunandana and [[Śrikrspa|Śrīkṛṣṇa]] interpolate son’s son, son’s son’s son, stepson, stepson’s son, stepson’s grandson between daughter’s sons and barren and widowed daughters. In the decided cases the latter order has generally been followed.

If there be none of the above both Yautaka and Ayautaka [[stridhana|strīdhana]] pass in order (acc. to [[Dayabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] IV. 3. 37 p. 98) to six heirs74 viz. the husband’s younger brother, husband’s [[P800]] brother’s son, sister’s son, husband’s sister’s son, brother’s son, daughter’s husband. Bṛ. says that the mother’s sister, maternal uncle’s wife, paternal uncle’s wife, father’s sister, mother-in-law, elder brother’s wife are declared to be like one’s mother, that when these women have no aurasa child or a son of their co-wife or a daughter’s son or a son’s son or stepson’s son, then the sister’s son and the rest may take their wealth. This text, acc. to the Dāyabhāga, simply declares that a sister’s son and the others may be heirs to the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] of the maternal aunt and the rest but the priority is determined among the six heirs mentioned above in Bṛhaspati’s text by the principle of religious benefit. Though the sister’s son is the first in Bṛhaspati’s text, it is the husband’s younger brother who stands really the first among the six if regard is had to the principle of religious benefit. The [[Vir.|Vīr.]] (V. P. p. 554) states that in the absence of heirs up to the stepson and stepson’s sons, the sister’s son and the rest (mentioned in Bṛ.) are the heirs to a woman’s [[stri dhana|strīdhana]] even when [[sapipdas|sapiṇḍas]] like her father-in-law are alive, since mere [[sapipda|sapiṇḍa]] relationship would not count when in conflict with a special text. It does not appear that the V. P. holds that the six take in the order stated in the text of [[Br.;|Bṛ.;]]75 it rather states that among these six the choice of the preferential heir [[P801]] would be determined by greater or less propinquity76. On failure of these six the husband’s [[sapindas,|sapiṇḍas,]] sakulyas and samānodakas take and then the father’s kinsmen.

The question of an unchaste daughter’s succession to [[stri dhana|strīdhana]] would be decided in the same way as her succession to her father’s property. Under the [[Dayabhāga|Dāyabhāga]] an unchaste daughter will not succeed. But under the Mitākṣarā as interpreted by modern courts an unchaste daughter who is a kept mistress or a prostitute may succeed but only after virgin daughters or married daughters as the case77 may be. The Mit. on [[Ya. Il 290|Yāj. II. 290]] relies upon the [[Skandapurana|Skandapurāṇa]] for the proposition that prostitutes are a fifth caste descended from certain apsarases.78

It has been held by the courts that though prostitution entailed degradation according to ancient Hindu Law, it did not sever the tie of blood. So the [[stridhana|strīdhana]] of a naikin (a dancing girl) or of a married woman who becomes a prostitute may be inherited by her brother or sister or her husband or her husband’s relations.79

It may not be out of place here to suggest how [[stridhana|strīdhana]] should be dealt with when Hindu Law comes to be codified as it is likely to be in the near future. It may be urged that all distinctions on the ground of sex should be altogether done away with and whenever a woman succeeds to any property whether of a male or of a female she should take an absolute estate. But if this change is opposed by a large volume of opinion as it is quite possible, then it should at least be provided [[P802]] that all property which a woman [[Inherits|inherits]] from a female should be her absolute property. Then as regards inheritance to [[stridhana|strīdhana]] one simple and straightforward rule should be laid down that the woman’s sons and daughters should succeed together taking equal shares. A third suggestion would be that when there are no descendants of the woman herself, then the husband should be the next heir. When the [[stridbana|strīdhana]] is that of a maiden, the heirs in order should be her brothers and sisters, then her mother, then her father and after the father the latter’s nearest heir. It is not quite necessary at this stage to specify what further rules of devolution should be prescribed.

B.), where it was held that a passage of the Mayūkha wherein the view of the Mit. was first stated and then the view of others really meant that the latter was the view of the Mayūkha, that sons and daughters inherited anvādheya together and that if there were both married and unmarried daughters, the unmarried daughters and the sons inherited together. मृतायाः स्त्रिया धनं पुत्रैर्दुहितृभिश्च साम्येन ग्राह्यम् । तथा च मनुः—जनन्यां … … समांशतः ॥ एतच्चान्वाधेयभर्तृप्रीतिदत्तविषयम् । एतद्विविधधनविषयेऽपरिणीतपरिणीतपुत्रीसमवाये विशेषमाह बृहस्पतिः । स्त्रीधनं स्यादपत्यानां … । अप्रत्तानामभावे प्रत्ताभिरविधवाभिर्भ्रातृभिः सह विभज्य ग्राह्यमित्याह कात्यायनः । मदनरत्न (folio 105 b). Vide व्य. म. text pp. 157-58 for the two views. It will be noticed that the Mit. construes the verse of Manu distributively i. e. sons take equally when they succeed and daughters do so when they succeed and that Manu does not mean that sons and daughters inherit simultaneously. ↩︎

  68. Vide Manilal Rewadat v. Bai Rewa 17 Bom. 758 (as to inheritance of non-technical strīdhana) where it was held that as regards strīdhana under the Mayūkha the woman is recognized as a fresh source of devolution, that the words ‘sons and the rest’ mean no more than sons, grandsons and great-grandsons, that as regards property which does not class as pāribhāṣika strīdhana, the sons and the rest take precedence over the ‘daughters and the rest (i. e. their issue)’, that failing sons and daughters the heirs to pāribhāṣika strīdhana and apāribhāṣika strīdhana are the same, save that as between male and female offspring the latter have a preferential right as to pāribhāṣika, while the former have a similar right as to apāribhāṣika. पारिभाषिकातिरिक्तं मातृधनं दुहितृसत्त्वे पुत्रादय एव लभेरन् । व्य. मयूख p. 160. ↩︎

B.), where there is an elaborate discussion about the following passages of the Mit. and the Mayūkha. तदप्रजःस्त्रीधनं पितृगामि । माता च पिता च पितरौ तौ गच्छतीति पितृगामि । तदभावे तत्प्रत्यासन्नानां धनग्रहणम् । मिता. on या. II. 145 ; भर्तुरभावे तत्कुले तस्याः प्रत्यासन्नो लभते । पित्रभावे च पितृकुले तस्याः प्रत्यासन्नः । … यत्तु मिताक्षरायां भर्तृभावे तत्प्रत्यासन्नानां सपिण्डानां पित्रभावे च तत्प्रत्यासन्नानां सपिण्डानामिति तत्रापि तेनास्याः प्रत्यासन्नास्तत्प्रत्यासन्नास्तद्द्वारा तत्कुले प्रत्यासन्ना इति यावदित्येवं व्याख्येयम् । व्य. मयूख p. 161. ↩︎

  71. ततश्च परिणयनलब्धस्त्रीधनं दुहितुरेव न पुत्राणां तत्रैव च क्रमार्थे गौतमवचनं स्त्रीधनं दुहितॄणां … ष्ठितानां च । ब्राह्मादिषु विवाहेषु यल्लब्धमध्यग्निधनं स्त्रियाः तत्तस्यां मृतायां प्रथमं दुहितॄणामेव तत्रापि प्रथमं कन्यायास्तदभावे प्रत्तायास्तदभावे परिणीतायाः । सर्वदुहित्रभावे च पुत्रस्याधिकारः । अप्रजःस्त्रीधने भर्तुरधिकारात् । Dāyabhāga IV. 2. 22 and 25, pp. 85-86. It will be noticed that the D. B. distinguishes between un-betrothed, betrothed and married daughters. Vide Bannerjee’s ‘Marriage and Stridhana’ pp. 477, 489-491 for order of inheritance to Yautaka. ↩︎

  72. यत्पुनः परिणयनानन्तरं पितृमातृभर्तृकुलात् स्त्रिया लब्धं धनं तद् भ्रातॄणामेव । तदाह याज्ञवल्क्यः । बन्धुदत्तं…प्युः ॥ (या. २. १४५)…अतः प्रथमं सोदराणां तदभावे मातुर्मातुरभावे पितुः । एषां पुनरभावे तद्धनं भर्तुः । तथा कात्यायनः । बन्धुदत्तं तु बन्धूनामभावे भर्तृगामि तत् । Dāyabhāga IV. 3. 10 and 29. Vide Prosanno v. Sarat 36 Cal. 86 (son being held the preferential heir to a married daughter to stridhana given to a woman after marriage by her father), Ram Gopal v. Narain 33 Cal. 315 (mother held entitled to preference over the husband of a childless woman who died leaving property gifted to her by her father after marriage). In both these cases there is an elaborate examination of passages of the Dāyabhāga and it is shown how Śrīkṛṣṇa and even modern writers have created confusion by offering varying explanations. ↩︎

  73. तत्र मनुः । जनन्यां … समांशतः । … बृहस्पतिरपि … स्त्रीधनं तदपत्यानां … । अपत्यपदं पुत्रपरम् । तेषामप्रत्ताभिर्दुहितृभिः सह मातृधनविभागः । … किं तूक्तावेव हेतोः पुत्रकुमारीदुहित्रोस्तुल्यवदधिकारः । एतयोश्चान्यतराभावेऽन्यतरस्य तद्धनं द्वयोरप्येतयोरभावे तु ऊढाया दुहितुः पुत्रवत्याः सम्भावितपुत्रायाश्च तुल्याधिकारः स्वपुत्रद्वारेण पार्वणपिण्डदानसम्भवात् । … पौत्रदौहित्रयोस्तु सद्भावे पौत्रस्यैवाधिकारः … उक्तानां तु सर्वेषां दौहित्रपर्यन्तानामभावे वन्ध्याविधवयोरपि मातृधनाधिकारिता । तयोरपि तत्प्रजातत्वात् प्रजाभावे चान्येषामधिकारात् । दायभाग IV. 2. 1-2, 9, 11-12. Dāyabhāga employs the word aprattā elsewhere in the sense of ‘unbetrothed’ and so betrothed daughters cannot take along with sons. The last passage in the quotation shows that the doctrine of spiritual benefit was not to be rigidly employed in succession to stridhana, acc. to the Dāyabhāga. श्रीकृष्ण remarks (in his comment on दायभाग), दौहित्रपर्यन्तानामिति प्रपौत्रपर्यन्तोपलक्षणम् । ↩︎

  74. यदाह बृहस्पतिः । मातुःस्वसा मातुलानी पितृव्यस्त्री पितृष्वसा । श्वश्रूः पूर्वजपत्नी च मातृतुल्याः प्रकीर्तिताः । यदासामौरसो न स्यात्सुतो दौहित्र एव वा । तत्सुतो वा धनं तासां स्वस्रीयाद्याः समाप्नुयुः ॥ तदेषां पुत्रादीनां भ्रात्रादिभर्तृपर्यन्तानां चाभावे सत्स्वपि श्वशुरभ्रातृश्वशुरादिषु सपिण्डेषु भगिनीपुत्रादीनामधिकारिता अनन्यगतेर्वचनात्, स्त्रीणां मातृतुल्यत्वप्रतिपादनेनामीषां पुत्रतुल्यत्वज्ञापनेन पिण्डदातृत्वसूचनस्य दायभागप्रकरणे धनाधिकारज्ञापर्नकप्रयोजनकत्वात् । … मातुलॊ भागिनेयस्य स्वस्त्रीयो मातुलस्य च । श्वशुरस्य गुरोश्चैव सख्युर्मातामहस्य च । एतेषां चैव भार्याभ्यः स्वसुर्मातुः पितुस्तथा । श्राद्धदानं तु कर्तव्यमिति वेदविदां स्थितिः । इति वृद्धशातातपवचनात् अमीषां पिण्डदत्वप्रतिपादनात् अयं पिण्डदानविशेषादधिकारक्रमः । तत्र प्रथमं देवरः तत्पिण्डतद्भर्तृपिण्डतज्जनैदेयपूर्वपुरुषत्रयपिण्डदातृत्वात्सपिण्डत्वाच्च तद्धनेऽधिक्रियते । … तस्याप्यभावे श्वशुरयोः पिण्डदानात् जामाता श्वश्रूधनेऽधिकारीति । अयं क्रमो ग्राह्यः । स्वस्रीयाद्या इति तु न क्रमार्थं किं त्वधिकारिमात्रावज्ञापनार्थपरम् । दायभाग IV. 3. 31, 35, 36-38 pp. 96-98. Vide also pp. 187-188. The verses of Bṛ. occur in Sm. C. II. p. 287, V. Nir. p. 472, Par. M. III p. 555, V. Mayūkha p. 161, V. P. pp. 553-554. The above verses of Bṛ. mean that males who are a woman’s sister’s son or her husband’s sister’s son or her husband’s brother’s son or her own brother’s son or her daughter’s husband or her husband’s younger brother are like her son and inherit her stridhana in certain circumstances. ↩︎

  75. In Bai Kesserbai v. Hunsraj L. R. 33 I. A. 176 the above text of Bṛhaspati was discussed in a Bombay case at great length (pp. 190-197) and it was held that under the Mayūkha a co-widow was entitled to succeed to the stridhana of a widow in preference to her husband’s brother or brother’s son, that the text of Bṛ. must be taken distributively, that is, when the marriage is in an approved form the husband’s sister’s son will succeed and that when the marriage is in an unapproved form the brother’s son or sister’s son will succeed, that the text does not indicate the order of succession which is governed by the principle of propinquity under the Mit. or the Mayūkha. It may be noted that the V. Nir. p. 472 expressly gives the distributive order. Acc. to the Sm. C. the six heirs referred to in the text of Bṛ. take in the order stated therein.
