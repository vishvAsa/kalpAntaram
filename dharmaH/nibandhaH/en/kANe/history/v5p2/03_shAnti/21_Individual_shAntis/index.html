[[P748]] CHAPTER XXI

Individual Śāntis

It is now time to turn to individual śāntis, mostly post-vedic. The first is Vināyaka-śānti or Gaṇapatipūjā. This is [[perfornied|performed]] at the commencement of all saṁskāras such as [[u punajanı|upanayana]] and marriage in order that the fruit thereof may be had without [[obsta cles|obstacles]] or for averting the evil effects of portents or in order to [[mitigato|mitigate]] the adverse effects of the death of a [[sapinda|sapiṇḍa]] or the like. When it is performed for its own sake it should be performed on the 4th tithi of the bright [[halt|half]], on Thursday [[ild|and]] the auspicious nakṣatras [[Pusya|Puṣya]], Śravaṇa, [[Uttarī|Uttarā]], [[Rohini|Rohiṇī]], [[Lasti|Hasta]], Aśvinī, Mṛgaśīrṣa, but when performed at the [[commencument|commencement]] of Upanayana or the like, one may [[perfurm|perform]] it at a time suited to the time of the principal rite. The [[sunluluu|saṅkalpa]] is given below.1 In H. of Dh. vol. II pp. 213-216 it has been shown [[bow|how]] in the earliest stages represented by the [[Mānava-gruya|Mānava-gṛhya]] and [[Baijavāpa-gshya|Baijavāpa-gṛhya]] which speak of four Vināyakas, all [[woru|were]] evil spirits, how in the next [[stage’|stage]] represented by the Yājñavalkyasmṛti (I. 271–294) Vināyaka is not only represented as causing obstacles ([[Vighna krt|Vighnakṛt]]) but also as bringing success in all actions and rites (Vighnahṛt) and how later on it was prescribed that [[Gapapati pūjā|Gaṇapatipūjā]] must be done first in all rites (Gobhila I. 13). Yāj. I. 293 provides2 that by worshipping Vināyaka in the way [[pres cribed|prescribed]] and also the planets, a person [[socures|secures]] success in his undertakings and the highest prosperity. The Viṣṇudharmottara II. 105. 2-24 borrows the verses of [[Yuj. l. 71-292|Yāj. I. 271-292]], though not in the same order and adds a [[fuw. ’l ho Brahmapda|few. The Brahmāṇḍa]] provides3

[[P749]]that Gajānana must be worshipped for success in all desires, in all [[samskūras|saṁskāras]] such as Garbhādhāna and Jātakarma, when [[start ing|starting]] on a journey or engaging in a commercial undertaking, at the time of battle, in the worship of gods, in troublous times. The [[Bhavisyottara|Bhaviṣyottara]] chap. 144 has a śānti called [[Gananāthaśānti|Gaṇanāthaśānti]] which resembles the Vināyakaśānti of Yāj.

In the [[Yājñavalkya-smrti|Yājñavalkya-smṛti]] (I. 294-308), the [[Vaikhānasa smārta-sūtra|Vaikhānasa-smārta-sūtra]] (IV. 13-14), the [[Baudhāyana-gļhyaśoṣasūtra|Baudhāyana-gṛhyaśeṣasūtra]], the [[Matsya-purāpa|Matsya-purāṇa]] (93. 1-105), the Viṣṇudharmottara I. 93–105 and other purāṇas, in the [[Bșhad-Yogayātrā|Bṛhad-Yogayātrā]] (chap. 18. 1-24), and in the [[medioval|medieval]] digests provision is made for a śānti rite to the nine [[grahus|grahas]], viz. the Sun, the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn (mentioned in the order of the week-days), Rāhu and Ketu. This [[naruyrahasūnti|navagrahaśānti]] is the model ([[prakļli|prakṛti]]) of all śānti homas in all medieval digests. The Vaikhānasa-smārtasūtra4 provides that all religious rites should be preceded by this [[naya graha-śānti|navagraha-śānti]]. [[Yājižavalkya|Yājñavalkya]]5 says ‘one desirous of prosperity, of removing evil or calamities, of rainfall (for crops), long life, bodily health and one desirous of performing magic rites against enemies and others should perform a sacrifice to planets.’ The Matsya (93. 5-8) states that the [[narugrahamukla|navagrahamakha]] is of three kinds, viz Ayutahoma (in which 10000 oblations are made), Lakṣahoma and Koṭihoma. The first is described at length in Matsya 93. 7-84, [[Laksahoma|Lakṣahoma]] in 93. 85-118, Koṭihoma in 93. 119-139. The Matsya further provides6 that [[Ayutaloma|Ayutahoma]] should be [[per formed|performed]] in marriages, festivals, sacrifices, establishment of images and other rites, in order that no obstacles should arise therein and on occasions when the mind is perturbed or when some evil omen or unusual event happens.

[[P750]]The procedure in Yāj. being concise and probably [[tho|the]] earliest among the extant works on [[grahayajṅu|grahayajña]] is set out [[hore|here]], with a few additions from Matsya and Vaikhānasa. The images of the nine planets should respectively be made of copper, crystal, red sandalwood, gold (for both Mercury and Jupiter), silver, iron, lead, bronze or (if all these be not [[avail able|available]]) they should be drawn on a piece of cloth with powders having colours appropriate to each planet or should be drawn on circles with fragrant substances (such as sandalwood paste). The Matsya7 (93. 11-12) prescribes that in drawing the images the Sun should be in the middle, that Mars, Jupiter, Mercury, Venus, Moon, Saturn, Rāhu and Ketu should be established with grains of rice respectively in the south, north, north-east, east, south-east, west, south-west and north-west. The Matsya remarks at the end of [[tho|the]] description of Ayutahoma: ‘just as armour is a protection against the wounds by arrows, so śānti (graha-yajña) is protection against the strokes of Fate.’12

The Matsya (93. 92) declares13 that [[Laksahoma|Lakṣahoma]] is ten times of the Ayutahoma and Koṭihoma is one hundred times of [[Laksa homa|Lakṣahoma]] in the matter of oblations, fees and rewards, that the [[pro cedure|procedure]] of invoking and bidding goodbye to the planets and [[presid ing|presiding]] deities, the mantras for homa, bath and gifts are the same in Lakṣahoma and Koṭihoma. I. 297-298 and by [[Kr̥tyakalpataru|Kṛtyakalpataru]] (on śānti) folio 5a. Vide Koṭihoma in list of vratas (p. 290).

The Grahayajña in Yāj. is short and simple, but in some [[purāpas|purāṇas]] such as the [[Bhavisyottara|Bhaviṣyottara]] (141. 6-85) and medieval and modern works it has become an elaborate affair by the addition of numerous details. One or two details may be pointed out. Each planet was supposed to have a gotra and was15 deemed to have been born in a certain country (vide note 875 p. 588 for the countries of the birth of planets). Therefore, in invoking the presence of each planet these two details have to be added (as specified below in the note for the sun by way of [[illustra tion).|illustration).]] The gotras of the grahas from the Sun to Ketu are respectively Kāśyapa, [[Atreya,|Ātreya,]] Bhāradvāja, [[Atreya,|Ātreya,]] [[Āngirasa,|Āṅgirasa,]] Bhārgava, Kāśyapa, [[Paithinasa,|Paithīnasa,]] Jaimini. The [[Sainskāra-tattya|Saṁskāra-tattva]] of Raghunandana (p. 946) sets out from Skanda the gotras and countries of birth of the nine [[grabas|grahas]] and provides that if worship is offered to them without mentioning the gotras and countries that would be disrespectful to them. The Agni (149. 12) says ‘Ayutahoma [[confors|confers]] slight success, [[Laksahoma|Lakṣahoma]] drives away all distress, while [[Kotihonia|Koṭihoma]] tends to destroy all kinds of trouble and confers all [[desirad|desired]] objects.’ The Viṣṇudharmottara (II. 36. 3-4) states that [[Asyapati,|Aśvapati,]] father of the [[fanious polirratū Savitri|famous pativratā Sāvitrī]] performed a [[Laksahoma|Lakṣahoma]] with the [[Savitri (Ctiyatri)|Sāvitrī (Gāyatrī)]] mantras for securing a son. ‘There is no [[atpūln|utpāta]] in the world that is not conjured away by Lakṣahoma; there is no more auspicious thing that surpasses Lakṣahoma. In the case of the king who gets a Koṭihoma performed by [[brāhinanas|brāhmaṇas]] the enemies cannot stand up against him in battle: excessive rainfall, drought, mice, locusts, parrots, evil spirits and the like and all enemies on the battle-field [[ara|are]] conjured away from him.’17 The [[Bhavi. syottara|Bhaviṣyottara]] (142. 11-12) calls koṭihoma a śānti rite, which yields all desired objects, by which even grave sins like [[brahmana-murder|brāhmaṇa-murder]] are removed at once, all utpātas are conjured away and great [[happiness. follows.|happiness follows.]] [[Bharisyottara|Bhaviṣyottara]] (chap. 142. 7-54) contains an elaborate procedure of Koṭihoma and also a briefer one (in chap. 142. 56-80). Some of the medieval works like the [[śāntimayūkha|Śāntimayūkha]] (p. 12) quote verses from the Skandapurāṇa that state how the [[unfavo urable|unfavourable]] aspect of Saturn led Saudāsa to eat human flesh, that of Rāhu made Nala wander over the earth, that of Mars led to Rāma’s banishment to forest, that of the Moon led to the death of [[Hiranyakasipu,|Hiraṇyakaśipu,]] that of the Sun brought about the fall of [[Rāvana,|Rāvaṇa,]] that of Jupiter led to the death of Duryodhana, that of Mercury made the [[Pandavas|Pāṇḍavas]] do work not fit for them, that of Venus led to the death of [[Hiraṇyāksa|Hiraṇyākṣa]] in battle.

Some of the medieval digests ([[ribandhus|nibandhas]]) such as the Dharmasindhu lay down that certain special gifts should be made when any one of the planets is unfavourable to a person. They are set out here from the Dharmasindhu (p. 135). For the Sun—Ruby, wheat, cow, red garment, jaggery, gold, copper, red sandalwood, lotuses; for the Moon—rice grains in [[veggel|vessel]] made from bamboo, camphor, pearl, white garment, jar full of ghee, a bull; for Mars—coral, wheat, [[masūra|masūra]] pulse, red bull, jaggery, gold, red garment, copper; for Mercury—blue garment, gold, bronze vessel, [[mulya|mudga]] pulse, emerald, slave girl, ivory, flowers; for Jupiter—[[topan,|topaz,]] turmeric, sugar, horse, yellow corn and yellow garment, salt, gold; for Venus—[[garmont|garment]] of various colours, white horse, cow, diamond, gold, silver, unguents, rice grains; for Saturn—sapphire, [[māsa|māṣa]] beans, sesame and sesame oil, [[kulitthe (pulse ),|kulittha (pulse),]] she-buffalo, iron, dark cow; for Rāhu—[[gomeda|gomeda]] (a kind of gem of four varieties), horse, blue garment, blanket, sesame and sesame oil, iron; for Ketu—cat’s eye gem, [[sesarne|sesame]] and sesame oil, blanket, musk, lamb, [[garnients.|garments.]] In the author’s youth these directions about dānas (gifts) were followed by many people and even now they are being followed to some extent. The Madanaratna (on [[Sāntika-paustika,|Śāntika-pauṣṭika,]] folios 5 a to 7 a) gives separate śānti procedure for each of the grahas from the Sun to Ketu from the [[Bhavisyottara.|Bhaviṣyottara.]]

[[P756]][[Anothor śīnti rolers|Another śānti refers]] to the placating of Saturn when that planet [[occupios|occupies]] the 12th, 1st and 2nd [[rūśis|rāśis]] from the rāśi of a man’s birth. This is [[rougbly|roughly]] a period of seven years and a half and is called [[‘sūrilha-apilar șiku-piya’|’sārdha-saptavārṣika-pīḍā’]] in Sanskrit and [[‘sīble sāli’|‘sāḍesātī’]] in Marāṭhī. The śānti consists in worshipping an image of Saturn made of iron placed in a vessel of iron or clay, covered with two dark garments or a blanket and offering to it dark and fragrant flowers, food or rice mixed with sesame. That food and [[tho|the]] image are to be donated to a dark [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] or to some brāhmaṇa with the mantra [[‘san no devir (Rg. X. 9. *)|‘śaṁ no devīr’ (Ṛg. X. 9. 4)]]. If the worshipper be a [[sūdra be|śūdra he]] is to repeat [[it paurānika|a paurāṇika]]19 mantra ([[notod|noted]] below) which refers to Nala getting back his kingdom by placating Saturn. This should be done every Saturday for a year or one should every day repeat the mantra containing ten names of Saturn (in note 1214) and should also repeat a [[Sanistotra|Śanistotra]] (eulogy of Saturn) every morning. By doing so the trouble that Saturn causes for seven and half years is [[arerted.|averted.]]

Some of the medieval digests try to furnish an [[accurato|accurate]] definition of Śānti. Only one [[inay|may]] be cited here. The [[Santi mayukha|Śāntimayūkha]]20 of [[Nilakantha|Nīlakaṇṭha]] (first half of 17th century A. D.) defines it as a [[rito|rite]] prescribed by the [[sastra,|śāstra,]] which ([[rite )|(rite)]] has its motive or urge sinfulness that is not clear (i.e. that is only inferred or presumed), that removes evil [[etfects|effects]] relating only to this world, and the performance of which does not lead on to sin.

The first [[clauses|clause]] excludes gifts made to remove diseases like tuberculosis;21 the 2nd clause distinguishes śāntis from sacri-

[[P757]]fices (intended to [[socuro other worldly rowards)|secure other worldly rewards)]] and [[prūyuscittas|prāyaścittas]] (that yield [[consoquences|consequences]] in this world as well as in the next) and the last clause distinguishes śāntis from rites of black magic (for destroying one’s [[nemies|enemies]] or securing a married woman’s love &c.) which is sinful.

The number of śāntis is legion. They are prescribed for conjuring away the effects of rare natural phenomena such as [[eclipsos,|eclipses,]] earth-quakes, rainfall (of peculiar kinds, of blood &c.) hurricanes, fall of meteors, comets, halos, Fata Morgana; for protection against the evil effects of the positions and movements of planets and stars for the world and for individuals; for strange births among human beings and animals; for the good of horses and elephants; for certain untoward happenings about Indra’s banner and about images of gods falling [[of|or]] weeping, the cries of birds and [[boasts,|beasts,]] the fall of lizards and the like on a person’s limbs and on certain stated periods or on solemn occasions.

All rites of Śānti, [[Paustika|Pauṣṭika]] rites and the Mahādānas were to be performed in ordinary fire, since there is no authority to prove that they are to be performed in [[srauta|śrauta]] fires or in smārta fire. Manu 3. 67 and Yāj. I. 97 refer only to ceremonies laid down in the [[Grhyasūtras.|Gṛhyasūtras.]] On Yāj. [[I, 285-86 the Mitaksarā|I. 285-86 the Mitākṣarā]] prescribes ordinary fire for offerings in [[Vinayaka-śānti.|Vināyaka-śānti.]] Vide also [[Santi. mayūkha|Śāntimayūkha]] p. 4.

Both Manu22 and the Viṣṇudharmasūtra prescribe that homas in which the Sun is the deity worshipped and Śāntihomas should be [[perforined by i|performed by a]] householder on the [[partans (i. e. on Paurpamāsi and Amīvāsyā).|parvans (i. e. on Paurṇamāsī and Amāvāsyā).]] These were śāntis at fixed periods. Similarly, when a person, male or female, of any [[casto|caste]] completed sixty years, there was the possibility that he may die soon, or that he may lose his mother or father or his wife or sons or that various diseases may affect him; for removing this danger a śānti is prescribed (and is often performed even now) in order that he may enjoy a long life, be free from all calamities and for his complete prosperity. This is called Ṣaṣṭyabdapūrti (completion of sixty years) or Ugrarathaśānti.23

In the Sundarakāṇḍa (chap. 27, 23 ff) Trijaṭā (a rākṣasī) details several dreams that she saw and that indicated the destruction of rākṣasas and that were favourable to Rāma. In the Mausalaparva of the Mahābhārata (chap. 3. 1–4) the Yādavas saw in dreams a black woman with whitish teeth running to Dvārakā with a laugh and kidnapping their women and terrible vultures were seen to be devouring the Vṛṣṇis and Andhakas in their own houses in which sacred fires had been established &c.

All ancient countries and peoples believed in dreams and satisfied their curiosity to know the future by interpreting them. The Chaldean astrologers and dream-interpreters were in high favour at Babylonian and Assyrian courts. The Book of Daniel (chap. 2) tells us how Nebuchadnezzar, a great king of Babylon, asked the Chaldeans not only to interpret dreams but commanded them on threat of death to make known to him the dream which he had forgotten and then to interpret it. Plato, the greatest of the Greek philosophers, regards dreams as important physical and [[paychio|psychic]] symptoms while certain dreams are [[con coded|conceded]] as of supernatural origin and explains in his Timaeus (chap. 46 and 47) that dreams are prophetic visions received by the lower appetitive soul (through the liver). In JRAS (old series) [[vol, 16|vol. 16]] pp. 118-171 N. Bland [[contributos|contributes]] a long and interesting article on ’the Mahomedan science [[of.|of]] Tābir or [[inter pretation|interpretation]] of dreams.’ There are striking parallels between the rules to be observed by the dreamer and by the interpreter and the principles of interpretation (of dreams) by [[contrarios|contraries]] and by dependence upon the religion, country and bodily conditions (such as total fast or full stomach) of the dreamer. On p. 141 of JRAS there is an interesting account of a dream of Nushirwan, a Sassanian king (531-579 A. D.), who saw [[&|a]] dream that he drank from a golden goblet and that a black hog put its head in the goblet and drank from it. Then we are told ‘Buzurmihr, his minister, whom he consulted, told him it [[signi fied|signified]] that his favourite princess had a black slave who was her [[loyer|lover]] and suggested that the women of his harem should be ordered to dance undressed in the presence of the king. One of them showing some hesitation in complying and being protected by the others was discovered to be a Hindu male slave and the [[P782]]Vazir’s interpretation was verified.’ The name of the Vazir [[romarkably|remarkably]] agrees in sound with the name Varāhamihira and it is not very fantastic to suppose that Varāhamihira, probably the most famous astrologer and astronomer of early times in India, had been patronized by [[Nushiryan|Nushirvan]] and held a high position in his court. Chronology is quite in favour of this identification since [[Varāhamibira|Varāhamihira]] [[takos|takes]] [[śaka|Śaka]] 427 (505 A. D.) as the starting point for finding out the [[ahargana|ahargaṇa]].

In modern69 times many educated people think dreams as of no consequence whatever, while there are others who regard dreams as almost infallible indicators of coming events; there is also a third class of people who are willing to listen to the [[argu ments|arguments]] advanced on behalf of the [[interpretors|interpreters]] of dreams and also of those who regard thoughts on the use and value of dreams as frivolous. To those who want to hear arguments on both sides and to form a judgment of their own about dreams, I would recommend the ‘Fabric of Dreams’ by Catherine Taylor Craig (Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner, London, 1918); Freud’s ‘Interpretation of dreams’ is the most important of his works, in which he develops his psycho-analytic technique. Then there is the work ‘Second sight in daily life’ by W. H. W. Sabine who has a theory of his own. His work deals with precognition (or foreknowledge) which according to him is a [[sub division|subdivision]] of Extra-sensory perception. He propounds the theory that the mind comprises of not only normal memory appearing to derive from physical perception but also ‘anticipatory memory’ deriving from the Basic Experience which from time to time passes to some degree into conscious awareness. Time is one and does not exist except as a verbal [[convonience|convenience]] and that what we call ‘future’ has already happened but it is not indicated in what form it has happened. Considerations of space and [[rele vance|relevance]] preclude further discussion of this subject here.

It is difficult to decide whether Varāhamihira borrows from the Matsya or vice versa or whether both borrow from a common source. It is not unlikely that Varāhamihira might

They all agree that throbbing112 of the right side of the body is auspicious and of the left inauspicious in the case of males, while the reverse is true in the case of women. They all set out the consequences indicated by the throbbing of the several parts and limbs of the body from the top of the head to the soles of the feet. To set out all these in detail would occupy much space. A few are mentioned by way of illustration. The fruit indicated by the throbbing of the top of the113 head is the acquisition of land (or earth); of the forehead, the prosperity of the position already occupied; of the region between the eyebrows and nose, union with persons dear to one; of the region of the eye, death; of the portion near the eye the acquisition of wealth; of the upper arm, union with friends; of the hand, the acquisition of wealth; of the back, defeat; of the chest, success; of the upper part of the foot, the securing of a position; of the soles, journey with gain therein. The [[Matsya-purāṇa|Matsyapurāṇa]]114 prescribes that when inauspicious indications (by throbbing) occur, brāhmaṇas should be placated with the gift of gold.
