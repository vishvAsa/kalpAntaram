Brief Notes on individual Purāṇas and Upapurāṇas 

Agnipurāṇa—'Present Agni' (by H.) in [[L.|I.]] H. Q. vol. [[Xi|XII]] pp. 683-691; 'Studies in genuine [[Agneya|Āgneya]] alias Vahnipurāṇa' by H. in 'Our Heritage' vol. I part 2 pp. 209–245 and vol. II part 1 pp. 76-109; 'Discovery of genuine [[Āgneyapurāṇa '|Āgneyapurāṇa']] by [[E.|H.]] in J. O. [[L|I.]] (Baroda) vol. V (1956) pp. 411-416 (shows that present Agni published by [[Anan.|Ānan.]] Press is not the original [[Purana,|Purāṇa,]] that this was compiled late, while the original [[Agneya|Āgneya]] or Vahni is not yet printed); the Dānasāgara p. 7. verse 63 speaks of an Āgneya which it has discarded apart from one which it has utilized (p. [[% Vorso|2 verse]] 11). As usual with most Purāṇas, the printed [[Agneya|Āgneya]] glorifies itself by saying (in chap. 272. 13 and 17) that in that Mahāpurāṇa Hari resides in the form of [[diffe rent|different]] lores and the Āgneya is a great purāṇa full of Veda and all vidyās. 

[[Adipurāṇa|Ādipurāṇa]] (U.)—B. V. [[VOL|vol.]] VI (1945) pp. 60-73 (H. [[posta lates|postulates]] an earlier and a later version). Vāyu 104.7 mentions an [[Adika|Ādika]] among the 18 [[prinoipal|principal]] purāṇas (including the [[Brahma|Brāhma]]). Alberuni's list (which mixes up Purāṇas and Upapurāṇas) mentions an [[Adipurāpa;|Ādipurāṇa;]] an [[Adipurāṇa|Ādipurāṇa]] is published by the [[Venk. Preas|Veṅk. Press]] in 29 chapters. H. in 'Studies &c.' vol. I pp. 279-303 deals at [[longth|length]] with this and tries to establish that there was an earlier [[Adi|Ādi]] Purāṇa of which no [[msg.|mss.]] are yet available (p. 211) and holds that it is to be dated between [[1203|1200]] to 1525 [[AD.|A. D.]] ('Studies' p. 288). [[Adi|Ādi]] and Ādya mean the same thing. But Kullūka on Manu II. 54 quotes some verses from [[Adi|Ādi]] which are [[asoribed|ascribed]] to [[Brihma|Brāhma]] by [[G, R.|G. R.]] p. 314. The digests make a [[con fusion|confusion]] between [[Adi|Ādi]] and [[Adityapurāṇa.|Ādityapurāṇa.]] Vide H. in 'Studies' vol. I. pp. 302–303. The printed one is a late compilation and none of the verses quoted by [[Lakṣmidhara|Lakṣmīdhara]] and [[Apararka|Aparārka]] occur therein (vide 'Studies' vol. I pp. 286–289). 

[[Adityapurūna|Ādityapurāṇa]]—Mentioned by Matsya 53.62 as U. and [[Al beruni|Alberuni]] (Sachau I. p. 130, 229, 248); quoted by [[Krbyakalpataru|Kṛtyakalpataru]] on Rājadharma (about 2 verses), Dāna (about 125), [[Sraddha|Śrāddha]] (about 20), Vrata (about 22); the Sm. [[O.|C.]] quotes many verses on [[Ahnika|Āhnika]] and [[Śrāddba|Śrāddha]] from both [[Adi|Ādi]] and [[Aditya|Āditya]] and quotes both [[P888]]separately on 'śauca' on the [[game|same]] page (I p. 94). Aparārka also quotes many verses from both [[Adi|Ādi]] and [[Aditya.|Āditya.]] The [[Dana sāgara|Dānasāgara]] quotes about forty verses from both the [[Adi|Ādi]] and [[Aditya|Āditya]] purāṇas. 

Ekāmra (a work of Orissa):—H in P. O. vol. 16 pp. 70-76 and 'Studies &c.' vol. I. p. 341 (assigns it to 10th or 11th [[contury|century]] A. D.) 

[[Kalikū:|Kālikā:—]](an U. in 93 chapters pr. by [[Vank, Prosa,|Veṅk. Press,]] Bombay); vide H. in A.B.O.R.I. vol. XXII. pp. 1-23; Sharma in I. H. Q. vol. 23 pp. 322-326 (holding that [[Kalika|Kālikā]] was completed during the reign of king Dharmapāla of Kāmarūpa); H. in B. V. vol. 16 (1956) pp. 35-40 questions the view of Sharma; 'Date of Kālikā' by Prof. Gode in J. O. R. (Madras) vol. X. pp. 289-294; Dr. Raghavan in J. O. R. (Madras) vol. XII. pp. 331–360 (shows that there are three separate recensions of the [[Purana|Purāṇa]]). H. distinguishes between an earlier version and the extant one and places the latter in the 10th or 11th century; on the words of Matsya 53. 60-64 'tad-etebhyo vinirgatam', the Kalpataru explains 'vinirgatam' as 'udbhūtam' (sprung from) and gives the Kālikā as an illustration (Brahmacārī p. 30) of an [[Up& purāṇa|Upapurāṇa]] sprung from Mahāpurāṇas; the Kalpataru quotes from it about 100 verses on vrata, on [[dāda|dāna]], 14 on [[gshastha|gṛhastha]], [[1%|12]] on vyavahāra (about witnesses and ordeals), 5 each on niyatakāla and [[tirtha|tīrtha]] and 2 on brahmacārin; Aparārka quotes it on pp. 15, 226, 377 (15 [[versos|verses]] on marriage and house settlements for brāhmaṇas), 924; the Sm. C. [[IL|II.]] p. 442 quotes one verse from it on śrāddha. The Dānasāgara quotes many verses from it. The [[Venk.|Veṅk.]] ed. [[montions|mentions]] Viṣṇudharmottara in chap. 91. 70 and 92. 2[^1414b]; the extant [[Kalikā|Kālikā]] would have to be placed before 1000 A. D. Vide p. 809 n 1317 above for the Kālikā saying that the howl of a female jackal is auspicious.

[[41-42 J14140|41-42)]][^1414c] expressly names the Kūrma and quotes a verse from it. Kalpataru (on śrāddha p. 119) quotes two verses from it. [[598);|59 ff);]] then it speaks of Dhanvantari receiving from Bhāradvāja Āyurveda in eight [[angas;|aṅgas;]] in part IV. it refers to Manus, jñāna, karma, mokṣa &c.

[[Matsya purana|Matsyapurāṇa]] ([[Anandāśrama|Ānandāśrama]] ed.). It has 291 chapters and 14062 verses. It is one of the ancient [[Purāpas|Purāṇas]] and has perhaps the largest number of [[smrti|smṛti]] chapters among Purāṇas. Many [[Verses|verses]] of the Manusmṛti and the Mahābhārata occur in it. [[Somo|Some]] verses of Yāj. also occur in Matsya e. g. Yāj. I. 295 is Matsya 93. 2, [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] II. [[279.295-6|279, 295-6]] and 303 are the same as Matsya 227. 200, 202–203 and 204. It appears that the Matsya holds the balance between Śiva and Viṣṇu. It glorifies not only Viṣṇu in the [[Mataya|Matsya]] avatāra but devotes 1570 verses to the slaughter of [[Tārakāgura|Tārakāsura]] and 623 to the destruction of Tripura, both by Śiva. The Vāmanapurāṇa (12. 48) speaks of it as the principal among Purāṇas. 

The Mit. on Yāj. I. 297 expressly quotes all the nine verses of chap. 94 of the Matsya (about the form of the images of the planets) and two verses from chap. 93 (11-12) about the position to be assigned to each with white grains of rice in a [[mandala|maṇḍala.]] The Kalpataru quotes hundreds of verses from Matsya on vrata, quotes about 750 on dāna from Matsya (all of which except 20 are identified in the present [[Mataya|Matsya]] by the editor); about 410 on Rājadharmakāṇda, 157 verses on [[tirtha|tīrtha]] (of which 100 are identified by the editor in the Matsya), 115 verses in [[Gphastha kaṇda,|Gṛhasthakāṇḍa,]] 112 [[Verses|verses]] on [[śrāddba,|śrāddha,]] about 67 [[OD Diyatakāla|on niyatakāla]] (all except 12 identified), 18 in [[vyavahārakānda,|vyavahārakāṇḍa,]] 6 in [[brahmacāri|brahmacārī]] and [[%|2]] in mokṣa, in all about 2000 verses. [[Apararks|Aparārka]] quotes about 400 [[verkos|verses]] from Matsya (about 250 being on [[dana).|dāna).]] It is not necessary after the preceding statement to go into the large number of verses quoted in Dānasāgara, [[Smrti-candrikā|Smṛti-candrikā]] and [[Homādri.|Hemādri.]] It may be said without fear of contradiction that long before 1000 A. D. the Matsya-purāṇa had the same [[arrange ment|arrangement]] and presented almost the same appearance as at present. This is more than what can be said about the other [[Purapas|Purāṇas]] except [[Viṣpu,|Viṣṇu,]] Vāyu, probably Bhaviṣya (I), [[Mārkandeya.|Mārkaṇḍeya.]] 

In the [[prosent|present]] author's opinion the Matsya is among the best preserved and the [[earlieat|earliest]] of the 18 [[Puranas.|Purāṇas.]] It may be [[P900]]dated between 200 A. D. to 400 A. D. The author does not wish to deny that a few verses here and there might have been [[inter polated|interpolated]] at a later [[dato.|date.]]

[[P909]]The date of the Viṣṇupurāṇa is a difficult question. There is no doubt that it is one of the early Purāṇas and that its text is not very much inflated. Hundreds of verses quoted in the three early digests viz. Kalpataru, Aparārka and [[smrticandrikā|Smṛticandrikā]] can be traced in the current text and this leads to the inference that the text has remained fixed for at least a thousand years. One important fact is that in this [[Parāṇa|Purāṇa]] Vyāsa and the Sūta do not play a prominent part as in many other Purāṇas. It states as some other Purāṇas do, that Vyāsa had four pupils to whom he imparted the four Vedas and a fifth pupil the Sūta [[Romaharsana|Romaharṣaṇa]] (III. chapters 3-7). But the sūta does not appear anywhere as the narrator of this Purāṇa. In the 4th [[am a|aṃśa]] one remarkable matter is the mention of Śākya, Śuddhodana and Rāhula and it is stated that Śuddhodana was 23rd in descent from [[Bṛbad-bala|Bṛhad-bala]] of the Ikṣvāku line (chap. 22). The possibility of interpolations, particularly in the prose passages, would have to be admitted. Rāśis are referred to in III. 14. 5 (rāśiṣvarke [[oa|ca]] gacchati) and the words _lagna_ and _horā_ also appear (in a [[prosto|prose]] passage). Vācaspati in his commentary on the [[Yogabhāsy&|Yogabhāṣya]] 2. 32 quotes it by name on yamas and niyamas (Viṣṇu VI. 7. 36-38) and a half [[yerse|verse]] 'evaṃ bhadrāsanādīnām' [[eto.,|etc.,]] [[Vispa|Viṣṇu]] VI. 7. 49 in com. on [[Yogabhāsya|Yogabhāṣya]] III. 49. Vācaspati wrote his [[Nyāyagūcinibandha|Nyāyasūcīnibandha]] in _vatsara_ 888 which should be taken as Vikrama year as he was a northerner and as the word 'vatsara' is used (and not śaka) i. [[.|e.]] 841 A. D.

[[śkanda|Skanda]]—This is the most extensive of Purāṇas and [[pogos|poses]] perplexing problems. It is found in two forms, one being divided into seven khaṇḍas, viz. Māheśvara, Vaiṣṇava, Brāhma, Kāśī, Āvantya, Nāgara and [[Prabhāga,|Prabhāsa,]] the other being divided into six saṃhitās, viz. Sanatkumāra, Sūta, [[Sārkarl,|Śāṅkarī,]] Vaiṣṇavī, Brāhmī and Saura. The Skanda in seven khaṇḍas has been published by the [[Venk,|Veṅk.]] Press and the [[Sūtāsambitā|Sūtasaṃhitā]] with the commentary of Mādhavācārya has been published by the [[Anan. Prese,|Ānan. Press,]] Poona. The extent of the Skanda is variously given as 81000 ślokas, at 100000 ślokas (vide PRHR p. 158), at 86000 (in PRHR p. 159). The god Skanda does not figure prominently in this Purāṇa named after him. The Skanda is named in the Padma V. 59. 2.
