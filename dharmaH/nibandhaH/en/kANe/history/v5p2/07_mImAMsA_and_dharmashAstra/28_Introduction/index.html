CHAPTER XXVIII [[MĪMĀŃSA AND DHARMASĀSTRA|MĪMĀṀSĀ AND DHARMAŚĀSTRA]]

[[P1152]]The Yājñavalkya-smrti provides that there are fourteen sources of vidyās ([[knowledge )|knowledge)]] and of [[Dharma, 1863|Dharma]],1 viz. Purāṇa, Nyāya, [[Mimāṁsā,|Mīmāṁsā,]] Dharmaśāstra, [[Angas|Aṅgas]] (six) and the Vedas (four). A similar verse is quoted by some as Manu’s, but it is not found in the extant [[Manusmrti.|Manusmṛti.]] It is therefore necessary to consider the origin and meaning of the word [[Mimāmsā,|Mīmāṁsā,]] to set out the principal doctrines of that [[Sāstra,|Śāstra,]] to mention the important rules of interpretation and the maxims of the [[Mimāṁsā|Mīmāṁsā]] in their application to topics of Dharmaśāstra and to refer to some of the important works of that Śāstra together with their approximate dates.

The word [[mimāmgā|mīmāṁsā]] has a great antiquity behind it. The [[Tai. 8. VII, 5. 7. 1|Tai. S. VII. 5. 7. 1]]2 says ’the expounders of Brahma (Veda) discuss ([[ the question)|the question)]] whether a day should be omitted or not’. Here the verbal form [[‘mimamsante’|‘mīmāṁsante’]] is employed in the [[sonse|sense]] of discussing or investigating a doubtful point and arriving at [[&|a]] decision thereon. In another passage of the [[Tai. 8.|Tai. S.]] the same form

[[P1153]]and another [[‘mimamseran’|‘mīmāṁseran’]] are used in the same [[sense..|sense.]] In [[Beveral|several]] other places the [[Tai, 8.|Tai. S.]] raises points for discussion with the words ’the [[brahmayadins|brahmavādins]] discuss’ but without employing the word mimamsante or a cognate word. Vide [[Tai.s. II 5.3.7|Tai. S. II. 5. 3. 7]] (about the deity of sānnāyya), [[V.5. 3. 2|V. 5. 3. 2]], VI. 1. 4. 5., VI. 1. 5. 3-5. The [[Kathaka Samhita 1870|Kāṭhaka Saṁhitā]]3 [[guts|puts]] forward a doubtful point for investigation without the words ‘Brahmavādins say’, but with the word [[‘mimāṁgante’|‘mīmāṁsante’]]. The [[Atharvaveda 1871|Atharvaveda]]4 [[etates|states]] ‘men separately discussing many times noted on the earth her actions’. In another place it employs the words [[mimāinsita|mīmāṁsita]] and [[mimamsamana|mīmāṁsamāna]]. The [[Saṅkhayana 182|Śāṅkhāyana]]5 Br. has [[they|‘they]] discuss the question whether one should offer homa after the sun rises or before the sun rises’. The Tai. Br. employs the word [[mimamsa|mīmāṁsā]] and the [[Satapatha|Śatapatha]] also in the Kāṇva recension [[doos 80|does so]] (vide S. B. E. vol. 26 note 1). In the [[Chandogyopaniṣad 1873|Chāndogyopaniṣad]]6 it is stated that five scholars very learned in the Veda and possessed of large houses (wealth) named Prācīnaśāla Aupamanyava and others got together and entered upon the consideration of the question ‘what is (the nature of) our self and what is [[Brahma-2’.|Brahma?’.]] In the Tai. Up. occur the words [[this|‘this]] is the investigation into ([[ ( the nature of )| (the nature of)]] Bliss (Brahma)’. In both [[thege|these]] passages the word [[mimamsa|mīmāṁsā]] is used in the sense of ‘consideration’ ([[vicarana|vicāraṇā]]) of high philosophical topics.’

Pāṇini provides7 for the formation of seven roots with san affix (Desiderative), one of which is [[‘mimamsate’|‘mīmāṁsate’]] from

[[P1154]]‘mān’ and the Kāśikā adds that it means [[’ desire|‘desire]] to know [[1e.|i. e.]] investigation and final [[conolusion’,|conclusion’,]] having in view probably the sūtras [[‘Athāto dharma-jijñāsa’|‘Athāto dharma-jijñāsā’]] and [[Athāto|‘Athāto]] [[brahma-jijñākt ‘.|brahma-jijñāsā’.]]

The foregoing brief discussion shows that long before the Upaniṣads the word [[‘mimamgā’|‘mīmāṁsā’]] had come to mean [[‘investi. gation|‘investigation]] into a [[topio|topic]] of discussion and coming to a conclusion thereon’. The same word acquired a restricted sense ([[ as|as]] in the [[verge|verse]] of Yāj. quoted above) viz. investigation into Dharma and arriving at conclusions on doubtful matters by interpretation and reasoning.

Some of the Dharmasūtras disclose familiarity with purely [[Mimāmsā|Mīmāṁsā]] terms and principles. For example, Gaut. states8 that an option is allowed only when there is a conflict between two texts of equal authority. The [[Apastamba|Āpastamba]] Dharmasūtra is the only Dharmasūtra that contains numerous [[Mināmsā|Mīmāṁsā]] terms and doctrines. It says ‘a positive Vedic text [[bas|has]] more force than [[an|a]] usage which merely leads to [[ao|an]] inference (of its being based on a Vedic text now lost)’.9 This is similar to Jaimini I. 3. 3 [[• |’]]If there is a conflict (between an express Vedic text and a [[gmrti|smṛti]] text) the latter is to be discarded but if there be no conflict an inference may be drawn (that the smrti text is based on some Śruti)’. [[Ap. say,|Āp. says]] ’the rules about anadhyāya (stopping Veda study on parvan &c.) are applicable only to learning the Vedic mantras and not to their application in sacrifices’. In another place Āp. provides [[where|‘where]] activity takes place because of finding pleasure therefrom (i. e. from a worldly motive), there is no (inference of its being based on) [[sāstra’,|śāstra’,]] which is similar to Jaimini’s rule. For reasons of space other examples of close agreement between Āp. Dh. S.10 and Jaimini are simply referred

[[P1155]]to in the note below and not explained at length. These examples show that in [[Apastamba’s|Āpastamba’s]] day [[Mimārsā|Mīmāṁsā]] doctrines had [[beon|been]] far advanced and since he employs the words [[‘Nyayavit. Bamaya’|‘Nyāyavit-samaya’]] (the siddhānta or doctrine of those who know Nyāya) and ‘Nyāyavidaḥ’, it follows that he is referring to some work on [[Mimāmgā|Mīmāṁsā]] or some author who composed a [[mlmāmgā-sūtra.|mīmāṁsā-sūtra.]] The correspondence in ideas and words between Āp. and the Pūrvamīmāṁsāsūtra is so [[olose|close]] that one is tempted to argue that Āp. [[know|knew]] either the extant [[mlmārsāsūtra|mīmāṁsāsūtra]] or at least an earlier version of it containing almost the same expressions. It will not do to say that all the above passages are later [[intor. polations.|interpolations.]] They have all been commented upon by Haradatta.

The Vārtikas of Kātyāyana on Pāṇini and the Mahābhāṣya show that [[Mimāmsā|Mīmāṁsā]] technical terms and doctrines had been elaborated long before them. If it were contended that there is only one Jaimini (and not two, much less three) a serious difficulty arises. Why should the author of PMS (containing about 2700 sūtras) refer to himself by name in five places only. The usual explanation offered
