[[P1152]]The Yājñavalkya-smrti provides that there are fourteen sources of vidyās ([[knowledge )|knowledge)]] and of [[Dharma, 1863|Dharma]],[^1868] viz. A similar verse is quoted by some as Manu's, but it is not found in the extant [[Manusmrti.|Manusmṛti.]] It is therefore necessary to consider the origin and meaning of the word [[Mimāmsā,|Mīmāṁsā,]] to set out the principal doctrines of that [[Sāstra,|Śāstra,]] to mention the important rules of interpretation and the maxims of the [[Mimāṁsā|Mīmāṁsā]] in their application to topics of Dharmaśāstra and to refer to some of the important works of that Śāstra together with their approximate dates.

Some of the Dharmasūtras disclose familiarity with purely [[Mimāmsā|Mīmāṁsā]] terms and principles. Having declared in the first sūtra that after a person has studied the Veda and because he has done so, PMS proceeds to say that he should start on the consideration of what dharma[^1928] is. The word 'upāsita-gurutām' (when introducing the 2nd intro. verse of the [[Slokavārtika)|Ślokavārtika)]] is applied by Umbeka to [[Kumarila|Kumārila]] himself in relation to the [[Slokavārtika|Ślokavārtika]] verse [[abhi vandya gurūpādau'.|'abhivandya gurūnādau'.]] It [[means the characteristic|means 'the characteristic]] or quality of one who has served his teacher i. e. who has carefully listened to what [[tbe guru|the guru]] expounded and [[understood it,'|understood it.']] The [[exprossion|expression]] 'anupāsitaguru' (which, according to Prof. Raja, always refers to [[Prabbākara)|Prabhākara)]] frequently [[(vide also pp. 14, 52, 75, 291, 441 )|(vide also pp. 14, 52, 75, 291, 441)]] used by Umbeka means 'he or those who have not waited upon the guru and listened to his words carefully and therefore have failed to grasp the correct meaning of the passages'. The [[infer ence|inference]] suggested by these words of derision is that the objector [[(either Prabhākara or some one else )|(either Prabhākara or some one else)]] has not understood the doctrine taught by Kumārila to him and the present author is disposed to hold that the frequent emphasis on the word 'guru' [[in anupasitaguru'|in 'anupāsitaguru']] is a veiled reference to Prabhākara-guru (as [[Sālikanātha|Śālikanātha]] puts his [[nanie|name]] in his [[Prakarana-pancikā Prabba kara-guror drstyā),|Prakaraṇa-pañcikā 'Prabhākara-guror-dṛṣṭyā),]] who was Kumārila's pupil but deviated from [[bis guru's|his guru's]] doctrines and wrote against them and Umbeka attacks and derides him for this. Supposing that Prabhākara had once [[been & pupil|been a pupil]] of Kumārila and later on propounded views that very much diverged from Kumārila's, it is natural if Umbeka took up the cudgels on behalf of [[Kumarila|Kumārila]] and attacked [[Frabbā. kara|Prabhākara]] as one who had forsaken [[lis Guru's|his Guru's]] views, had not digested them properly and had written a work (not now available but well-known to Umbeka) severely attacking Kumārila. Prof. Raja (Intro. pp. XLIII and XLIV) throws out the suggestion that on p. 14 and p. 291 Umbeka possibly suggests that [[Prabhā kara|Prabhākara]] preceded Kumārila. There is nothing of the kind. Umbeka [[holda|holds]] that Prabhākara had not grasped his master's doctrines and tries to show what the real doctrine misunderstood by Prabhākara was.
