[[AGHAMARSANA-( The|AGHAMARṢAṆA—(The]] hymn [[Rg.|Ṛg.]] X.190.1–3).

This is prescribed as a penance for all sins by even the most ancient Dharmaśāstra works such as Gaut. 24.11, [[Baud. Dh. S. IV. 2.19-20,|Baud. Dh. S. IV. 2.19–20,]] Vas. 26.8, Manu XI.259–260, Yāj. [[CANDRAYAN A-The|CĀNDRĀYAṆA—The]] word is derived in this way. That rite, where there is action by means of increase and decrease (in the intake of food) in imitation of the course of the phases of the moon, is [[cāndrāyana. 308|cāndrāyaṇa.[^308] ]] The word occurs in [[Pān. V.1. 172 (pārāyana-turāyaṇa-cāndrāyanam vartayati).|Pāṇ. V. 1. 72 (pārāyaṇa-turāyaṇa-cāndrāyaṇaṁ vartayati).]] From very ancient times [[Cāndrāyana|Cāndrāyaṇa]] has been divided into two varieties, called [[Yavamadhya 309|Yavamadhya [^309] ]] (lit. being large in the middle like a grain of yava, and therefore tapering or thin at the ends) and [[Pipilikāmadhya (lit,|Pipīlikāmadhya (lit.]] having a middle like that of an ant, that is, slender in the middle and large at the ends). [[Baud. Dh.S.|Baud. Dh. S.]] III. 8. 33 names these two varieties. According to [[Jābāli 310|Jābāli [^310] ]] there are five [[P135]]varieties of [[Cândrāyana|Cāndrāyaṇa]] viz. Yavamadhya, [[Pipilikāmadhya,|Pipīlikāmadhya,]] [[Yaticāndrāyana,|Yaticāndrāyaṇa,]] Sarvatomukha and [[Siśucāndrāyana.|Śiśucāndrāyaṇa.]] These will be described later on. Yājñavalkya says (III. 326) that where no specific penance is prescribed in the [[smrti|smṛti]] texts, [[cândrāyaṇa|cāndrāyaṇa]] effects purification and [[cândrāyana|cāndrāyaṇa]] may be undergone not as a penance but for the purpose of [[accumu lating|accumulating]] merit and that in the latter case (when performed for a year) the performer enjoys the happiness of going to the world of the moon after death.[^311] Manu XI. 221 and Gaut. 27.18 state the same view about [[cāndrāyana|cāndrāyaṇa]] for accumulating merit. When the vrata of [[cāndrāyana|cāndrāyaṇa]] is performed for the sake of merit, there is no tonsure (vide Gaut. 27.3 'vapanam vratam caret' and Haradatta thereon). Gaut. 19.20 (= Vas. 22. 20) prescribes that [[Kịcchra, Atikrochra and Cāndrāyana|Kṛcchra, Atikṛcchra and Cāndrāyaṇa]] are the same penance for all sins (collectively for grave sins, for lesser ones separately, according to Haradatta and [[others ).|others).]] Compare Manu V. 21 and XI. 215 [[(=Baud. Dh. S. IV. 5. 16).|(= Baud. Dh. S. IV. 5. 16).]] [[Manu XI. 27, Yāj. IUI, 323, Vas, 27.21, Baud, Dh, S. IV.5.18 and others define Cândrāyana (of the Yavamadhya type )|Manu XI. 217, Yāj. III. 323, Vas. 27.21, Baud. Dh. S. IV.5.18 and others define Cāndrāyaṇa (of the Yavamadhya type)]] as follows: On the first day of the bright half of a month only one morsel of food [[( grāsa or pinda)|(grāsa or piṇḍa)]] is taken, on the second tithi two and so on; on the full moon [[(Paurnamāsi)|(Paurṇamāsī)]] 15 morsels are taken; then on the first of the dark half 14 morsels, one being reduced on each succeeding day. So on the 14th of the dark half only one morsel is eaten and on the [[Amāvāsyā|amāvāsyā]] there is a complete fast. Here in the middle of the month of thirty days the largest number of morsels is taken (and so this is Yavamadhya) just as on the [[Paurnamāsi|Paurṇamāsī]] day the moon becomes full and begins to wane from the next day. Here [[Paurnamāsī|Paurṇamāsī]] is in the middle of the period of the observance as [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. 47.6 says. If one begins the vrata on the first tithi of the dark half, he reduces one morsel on the 1st i. e. takes 14 morsels on that day, 13 on the 2nd tithi of the dark half and so on. On the 14th of the dark half he eats one morsel and on the amāvāsyā he eats nothing. Then on the 1st of the bright half he takes one morsel and goes on increasing the intake till on the [[Paurnamāsi|Paurṇamāsī]] he takes 15 morsels. The month is taken in this latter case to be [[Pūrnimānta.|Pūrṇimānta.]] Here in the middle of the observance there is no food taken and a large number of morsels is taken at the beginning and at the end. So this is called [[Pipilikamadhya.|Pipīlikāmadhya.]] This latter is described by [[Vau3745|Vas. 23.45]] [[P136]]and Manu XI. 216. [[Vispu Dh. S. 47.5-6 say: Yasyāmamāvāsyā madhye bhavati sa pipilikā-madhyah yasya paurnamāsi sa yavamadhyah.|Viṣṇu Dh. S. 47.5–6 say: Yasyāmamāvāsyā madhye bhavati sa pipīlikā-madhyaḥ yasya paurṇamāsī sa yavamadhyaḥ.]] If there are 14 or 16 tithis in any particular [[paksa (half of a month )|pakṣa (half of a month)]] then the number of grāsas must be regulated accordingly. There are other descriptions of [[cândrāyaṇa.|cāndrāyaṇa.]] Vide Haradatta on Gaut. 27.12–15. The Kalpataru appears to have [[held ( explaining|held (explaining]] two verses of Jābāla) that on the first of the dark half of a month 15 morsels were taken, then one morsel less was taken on each succeeding tithi; so that on amāvāsyā one morsel was eaten, then on the first of the bright half two morsels were eaten and one morsel was added every day so that on the 14th of the bright half fifteen morsels were taken and there was a complete fast on [[Paurnamāsī.|Paurṇamāsī.]] But this seems to be opposed to the very idea of increase and decrease of the intake of food on the analogy of the waxing and waning of the [[Moon and'|Moon and]] to several [[smrti|smṛti]] texts such as [[Vas, 23.45|Vas. 23.45]] and Parāśara X. 2. Therefore the Pr. Prakāśa [[(folio 58b )|(folio 58b)]] finds fault with it.[^311a] [[Cândrāyana may be divided from another point of view into two varieties : (1) MUKHYA (in the primary sense, viz. following exactly the waxing and waning of the moon) and GAUNA (having a secondary or metaphorical sense ).|Cāndrāyaṇa may be divided from another point of view into two varieties: (1) MUKHYA (in the primary sense, viz. following exactly the waxing and waning of the moon) and GAUṆA (having a secondary or metaphorical sense).]] The former is either yavamadhya or [[pipilikā-tanu madhya ), while the latter is of four kinds, viz, sāmānya, rsi cāndrāyana, śiśucāndrāyana and yaticandrayana.|pipīlikā-(tanu-madhya), while the latter is of four kinds, viz. sāmānya, ṛṣicāndrāyaṇa, śiśucāndrāyaṇa and yaticāndrāyaṇa.]] The last two will be described later. The Sāmānya (or sarvatomukha) consists in eating only 240 morsels of sacrificial food in a month of 30 days, distributing them according to one's choice (not necessarily in imitation of the phases of the moon) as stated by [[Manu XL220,|Manu XI. 220,]] Baud. IV.5.21, [[Yāj. III, 324 and the Mit. (on Yaj.|Yāj. III. 324 and the Mit. (on Yāj.]] III. 324), the Madana-pārijāta and other works. Here the penance, though it is not regulated by the phases of the moon, is called [[Candrāyana|Cāndrāyaṇa]] for the purpose of prescribing the same procedure and observances in it as in the [[Cândrāyana|Cāndrāyaṇa]] which follows the phases of the moon. Here the [[Mimānsā rule of Kupdapāyinām-ayana applies, 312|Mīmāṁsā rule of Kuṇḍapāyinām-ayana applies.[^312] ]] From [[th words|the words]] of Gautama [[P137]]27.12–15 it appears that he envisaged a cāndrāyaṇa of 32 days [[(pipilikāmadhya)|(pipīlikāmadhya)]] or 31 days, when he says that the performer should fast on the 14th of the bright half, eat fifteen morsels on the Full Moon day, then reduce it by one morsel, so that there is a total fast on the amāvāsyā and then on the first of the bright half one morsel is taken until on the full moon 15 morsels are taken. Thus from the 14th of the bright half [[( when there is a complete fast )|(when there is a complete fast)]] to the full moon of the succeeding month there are in all 32 days and the penance is of the [[pipilikā-madhya|pipīlikā-madhya]] type.

The first is that described by Manu XI. 211 and Vas. 21. 20 viz. four periods of three days each following one another in which there is respectively eating once only by day, once only by night, eating once only without asking for it and complete fast.
