CHAPTER II

MEANS OF REDUCING CONSEQUENCES OF SINS

[[P41]]Confession—In [[Ap.|Āp.]] [[Dh,|Dh.]] S. [[1.|I.]] 9. 24. 15, [[1.|I.]] 10. [[28, 19,|28. 19,]] [[1. 10.29.1|I. 10. 29. 1]] it is prescribed that the person undergoing a penance for being an abhiśasta or for unjustly forsaking his wife or for the murder of a learned brāhmaṇa has to declare his misdeed while begging for alms to support himself. The Vedic student who was guilty of sexual intercourse had also to proclaim his lapse while begging at seven houses ([[Gaut,|Gaut.]] 23.18, Manu XI. [[122 ).|122).]]1

Repentance ([[anutūpa|anutāpa]])—Manu2 XI. 229-230 ([[=Vispudharm oftara II. 73.231-32 )|(=Viṣṇudharmottara II. 73.231-32)]] provide: “The more a man’s mind loathes his own evil deed the more does his body become freed from the unrighteousness (committed by him). If after committing a sin a man feels repentance for having done it he becomes free from that sin. He becomes purified only by the resolution of ceasing to sin and thinking ‘I shall not do so again’”.3 The [[Visnupurāṇa|Viṣṇupurāṇa]] II. 6.40 lays emphasis on repentance and humble devotion to [[Krspa.|Kṛṣṇa.]] Pr. V. (p. [[30 )|30)]] quotes a verse of [[Angiras|Aṅgiras]] [[“A man|‘A man]] after committing sins, if he is consumed by repentance and is sorry day and night (for his misdeed), is purified by [[prānāyāma’.|prāṇāyāma’.]] The view of digests like the Pr. [[Prakasa|Prakāśa]] is that [[P42]]repentance by itself is not enough to destroy sins, but that repentance renders the sinners fit to receive penance, just as paring the nails and the like make the person intending to perform a Vedic sacrifice fit to be a [[dikṣita.|dīkṣita.]] Yama quoted by Aparārka (p. 1231) states that repentance and stopping from repeating the sin are merely the [[angas|aṅgas]] ([[ subsidiary elements)|(subsidiary elements)]] of prāyaścittas and cannot independently take the place of [[penances,|penances.]]

Prāṇāyāma (restraint of the breath). Vide H. of Dh. vol. [[II|II.]] p. 317 for a description of this. Manu XI. 248 (which is the same as Baud. Dh. S. [[IV, 1.31,|IV. 1. 31,]] [[Vas, 26.4,|Vas. 26. 4,]] Atri II. 5, [[Sankhasmrti|Śaṅkhasmṛti]] [[XII.18-19 )|XII. 18-19)]] lays down: [[sixteen|‘sixteen]] prāṇāyāmas attended by the [[Vyāhrtis|Vyāhṛtis]] and the [[Pranava|Praṇava]] ([[omkāra),|oṁkāra),]] if [[per formed|performed]] every day, would purify after a month even the murderer of a [[bhrūna|bhrūṇa]] (a learned [[brāhmana).|brāhmaṇa)’.]] [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. [[55.2|55. 2]] (in prose) is to the same effect. Vas. [[26.1-3|26. 1-3]] provide that on the proper performance of three [[prānāyāmas|prāṇāyāmas]] all the sins committed by day or in the night are at once destroyed. Yāj. III. 305 [[statas|states]] that for the removal of all sins, of upapātakas and of sins for which no special expiation is provided by the texts one hundred [[prānāyāmas|prāṇāyāmas]] should be performed. This refers to sins not known to others for which expiation is to be performed secretly. Baud. Dh. S. [[(IV.1,5-11)|(IV. 1. 5-11)]] provides for varying numbers (3, 7, [[12 )|12)]] of prāṇāyāmas to be spread over one day to a year for different kinds of sins ranging from the eating of the food of a [[sūdra|śūdra]] to [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] murder. This whole passage [[is.|is]] quoted by the Mit. on Yāj. [[III,305|III. 305]] which points out the kinds of sins it deals with. The Agnipurāṇa [[173.21|173. 21]] has the same half verse as [[Yaj.|Yāj.]] [[III.305|III. 305]] (first [[half ).|half).]]

Tapas (austerity). Even [[Rg.|Ṛg.]] X. 154. 2 speaks of tapas as leading to heaven and as unassailable. The Chān. Up. V. 10. 1-2, [[Mundaka|Muṇḍaka]] Up. I. 2. 10-11 place tapas over yajña. Gaut. [[19.15|19. 15]] says that celibacy,4 truthfulness, bathing thrice daily (in the morning, at noon and in the evening), wearing wet clothes (till they are dried on the body), sleeping on the ground (on a raised part or altar) and fasting constitute austerities. Baud. Dh. S. III. 10. 13 adds to these [[‘ahimsā’|‘ahiṁsā’]] (non-injury to any being), astainya (not depriving any one of his property) and [[puru. sugrūṣa|guruśuśrūṣā]] (attendance upon a teacher). Gaut. 19. 17 [[sates|states]] that [[P43]]the periods for which tapas is to be observed are a year, six months, four months, three months, two months, one (month), 24 days, 12 days, 6 days, 3 days, a day and a night (according to the nature of the sin). Manu XI. 239-241 declare that those guilty of [[mahāpatakas|mahāpātakas]] and others guilty of evil deeds are freed from sins by well-practised tapas and that whatever sins are committed by people in thought, word and bodily actions are all quickly burnt by tapas. This doctrine is shared by [[th|the]] Jainas as stated in the [[Uttaradhyayana|Uttarādhyayana]] XXIX. 27 [[by|‘by]] austerities he cuts off karman’ ([[S B. E,|S. B. E.]] vol. 45 p. 166).

Homa (sacrifice into fire). The Tai. [[A.|Ā.]] ([[II,|II.]] 7-8) describes5 the [[kūsmānda|kūṣmāṇḍa]] homa and [[diksa|dīkṣā]] and II. 8 prescribes that the man who regards himself as impure should offer a sacrifice with the [[kūsmanda|kūṣmāṇḍa]] mantras (mentioned in Tai. [[A.|Ā.]] II. 3-6, viz ‘yaddevā deva-hedanam’ ([[Vaj.|Vāj.]] S. [[20.14-16=Tai.|20. 14-16 = Tai.]] [[A.|Ā.]] II. [[3.1).|3. 1).]] The [[Mahārnava karmavipaka|Mahārṇavakarmavipāka]] (folio 8 a) prescribes the procedure of [[Kūsmānda|Kūṣmāṇḍa]] homa taken from Jamadagni which is brief. It is as [[follows:|follows:—]] After shaving the hair on the head and the moustache and taking a bath in holy water one should offer the [[ajyabhāgas|ājyabhāgas]] and then offer oblations of clarified butter with the three Anuvākas beginning with ‘yad devā devahedanam,’ and then he worships fire with the verses beginning with [[‘Vaisvānarāya pratipā. dayāni.’|‘Vaiśvānarāya pratipādayāmi.’]] He then offers samidh with the mantra [[‘yadadiya. yam’|‘yadadivāyam’]] and then he repeats the Jayā mantras. He has to observe the rules for a [[dikṣita|dīkṣita]] viz. not eating flesh, avoiding sexual intercourse, not telling a falsehood and not sleeping on a cot. He has to subsist on milk (if he is a [[brāhmana),|brāhmaṇa),]] on barley gruel if a [[ksatriya|kṣatriya]] and on [[āmiksā|āmikṣā]] if he is a [[vaisya.|vaiśya.]] The [[Kuṣmāṇda-homa|Kūṣmāṇḍa-homa]] as prescribed by Baudhāyana is a longer one. The [[Baud,|Baud.]] Dh. S. III. [[7,1|7. 1]] prescribes that he who considers himself impure should offer burnt oblations reciting the [[kūṣmānda|kūṣmāṇḍa]] mantras, that he who has had forbidden intercourse becomes even like a thief or [[evən|even]] like a slayer of a learned [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] [[P44]]and that (by performing homa with [[kūsmāndas)|kūṣmāṇḍas)]] he becomes free from any sin that is less than the murder of a learned brāhmaṇa. Yāj. III. 309 provides that whenever a twice-born man feels himself mixed up in heavy sins he should perform a homa with sesame with the sacred [[Gāyatri|Gāyatrī]] mantra. The Mit. following a verse of Yama says that one lakh of offerings of sesame must be made. Both [[Manu.|Manu]] XI. 34 and Vas. 26. 16 say that a [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] becomes free from all [[distrasses|distresses]] by Vedic recitations (japa) and sacrifices (homa). When the wife [[con fessed|confessed]] to the existence of a paramour, the Śat. Br. II. 5. 2. 20 states that she made an offering into the Dakṣiṇāgni with the mantra ([[Vaj.|Vāj.]] S. [[IIT.|III.]] 45) ‘whatever6 sin we have committed, whether in the village, in the forest, in society or by the organs of sense, we cast that away by this sacrifice, svāhā.’ Both Manu (VIII. 105) and Yāj. [[(II, 83)|(II. 83)]] prescribe that when a witness tells a falsehood in order to prevent an accused person from [[be ing|being]] sentenced to death, he should offer for expiating his perjury an oblation of boiled rice to Sarasvatī. There are other homas also prescribed such as the Gaṇahoma, in which such verses as ‘agne naya supathā’ from the [[Taittiriya|Taittirīya]] [[sākhā|śākhā]] are recited ([[Mahārnava).|Mahārṇava).]]

The old idea of sacrifice seems to have been that it was propitiatory in character. Sacrifice was not demanded or required by God. Sacrifice was probably regarded as a gift which pleased God, induced Him to forgive a man for his transgressions and enabled erring man to regain God’s favour which had been lost by some shortcoming or some sinful act. Sacrifices therefore had an expiatory or cleansing function or character. Sacrifices may be performd by offerings either an animal (meant as substitute for the sinner himself who had forfeited his life for his sins and transgressions) or a burnt offering or any other thing offered to God and then [[distri buted|distributed]] to others.

Japa (muttering of Vedic passages as prayers). Japa is of three kinds, vācika (audibly uttered), [[upārsu|upāṁśu]] (inaudibly uttered) and mānasa (mentally revolved), each succeeding one being ten times superior, acc. to Laghu Hārīta [[IV, 186,|IV. 186,]] [[P45]]([[Jivananda I.)|Jīvānanda I).]]7 Śabara (on Jai. XII. [[4.1)|4. 1)]] distinguishes japa from stuti, the former being simply an utterance or recitation of a mantra or mantras.8 Inaudible japa is praised9 in [[Sānkhāyana|Śāṅkhāyana]] Br. 14. 1. The Āśv. [[Śr|Śr.]] I. 1. 20 provides that japa, [[anumantrana,|anumantraṇa,]] āpyāyana and upasthāna are uttered [[upāṁsu.|upāṁśu.]] The Āp. Śr. (24. 1. [[8-10 )|8-10)]] provides that mantras from the [[Rgveda|Ṛgveda]] and the [[Samaveda|Sāmaveda]] are uttered in sacrifices loudly and that passages of the Yajurveda are muttered [[upamśu|upāṁśu]] and the Tai. [[Pratisakhya23.6|Prātiśākhya 23. 6]] states that [[upāmsu|upāṁśu]] recitation is made by means of the effort of speech organs but without sound (i. e. in a very low voice) and without the mind being directed (to utterance with udātta and other accents) i. e. [[‘Karanavad asabdam-amanah-prayogam-upamsu’.|‘Karaṇavad-aśabdam-amanaḥ-prayogam-upāṁśu’.]] Gaut. 19. 12 (= Baud. Dh. S. III. 10. 10 = [[Vas.22.9)|Vas. 22. 9)]] prescribes the following Vedic texts as purifiers: [[Upanisads,|Upaniṣads,]] [[Vedanta|Vedānta]] texts, the [[Samhita|Saṁhitā]] texts of all the Vedas (and not the [[padapātha|padapāṭha]] or [[kramapātha),|kramapāṭha),]] the Yajus texts in which the word ‘madhu’ occurs, the [[Aghamarsana|Aghamarṣaṇa]] hymn ([[Rg.|Ṛg.]] [[X, 190. 1-3),|X. 190. 1-3),]] the [[Atharvasiras,|Atharvaśiras,]] (the [[anuvakas|anuvākas]] called) Rudras, the [[Purusa|Puruṣa]] hymn ([[Rg.x.90),|Ṛg. X. 90),]] the two [[samans|sāmans]] called [[Rajata.|Rājata]] and [[Rauhineya,|Rauhiṇeya,]] the [[Br̥hat-sāma|Bṛhat-sāma]] and the Rathantara, the [[Puruṣagati saman,|Puruṣagati sāman,]] the [[Mahānāmni|Mahānāmnī]] verses, the Mahāvairāja (sāman), the [[Mahādivākirtya|Mahādivākīrtya]] (sāman), any of the [[Jyestha|Jyeṣṭha]] sāmans, the [[Bahispavamāna|Bahiṣpavamāna]] (sāman), the [[Kuṣmāndas,|Kūṣmāṇḍas,]] the [[Pāvamānis|Pāvamānīs]] ([[Rg.|Ṛg.]] IX) and the [[Sāvitri|Sāvitrī]] ([[Rg.|Ṛg.]] III. [[62.10).|62. 10).]]10 The [[P46]]original idea of japa was highly spiritual. Deep knowledge of the Upaniṣads and other texts would have made the soul pure and enabled it to realize the supreme reality and induced in the mind love of all human beings as sparks of the one Divine Essence. Japa at the highest level was contemplation of God and endeavour to attain unity with God. The habit of [[repeat ing|repeating]] the sacred texts should lead to maintaining the soul in the presence and thought of God. Japa demands three things, purity of heart, detachment and placing oneself in the hands of God.

Manu XI. 46 provides that a sin unintentionally committed [[jsexpiated|is expiated]] by the recitation of Vedic texts as prayers, but that which men in their folly commit intentionally is expiated by [[prayascitta.|prāyaścitta.]]

[[P47]]Manu II. 85-87 (which are the same as [[Vas,|Vas.]] 26. 9-11 and [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. 55. 10-21) lay11 down: ’the performance of japa is ten times more efficacious than the sacrifices (like [[Darsapūrna māsa)|Darśapūrṇamāsa)]] prescribed by the rules (of the Veda), japa when carried on inaudibly is a hundred times superior (to sacrifices) and mental revolution of mantras is a thousand times superior. The four pākayajñas12 (Vaiśvadeva, bali, daily [[Śrūddha,|śrāddha,]] and [[hono uring gueste)|honouring guests)]] together with the Vedic sacrifices do not come up to even the 16th part of japa. A [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] attains perfection (the highest goal) by japa; he may or may not engage in other duties (such as Vedic sacrifices); a brāhmaṇa befriends all creatures’. Great eulogy is bestowed on the inaudible japa of the [[Gāyatri|Gāyatrī]] mantra ([[Rg.|Ṛg.]] III. [[62,10)|62. 10)]] for which pp. 303-305 of H. of [[Dh,|Dh.]] vol. II may be consulted. Where no particular number is specially mentioned the recitation of a mantra is to be [[per formed|performed]] a hundred times.13

[[Manu1104|Manu]]14 XI. 261-262, Vas. [[27.1-3,|27. 1-3,]] [[Angiras|Aṅgiras]] 101 and other works go so far as to assert that the fire of Veda study [[con sumes|consumes]] guilt caused by evil deeds just as a fire burning strongly [[eonsumes|consumes]] even green trees or that a [[brahmana|brāhmaṇa]] who remembers [[Rgveda|Ṛgveda]] (studied by him) is not tainted by any guilt, though he may have destroyed the three worlds and have eaten the food of any person whatever. But these are simply [[laudatory(arthavadas)|laudatory (arthavādas)]] and are not to be taken literally, as [[Vas, 27, 4|Vas. 27. 4]]15 ([[=Angiras|=Aṅgiras]] 102) gives the warning ‘one should not become addicted to (or take pleasure in committing) evil deeds relying on the power of the Veda (stated in some [[smrti|smṛti]] passages). [[The effects of)|(The effects of)]] actions done through ignorance and negligence are alone consumed (by Veda study) and not those of other actions (intentionally done).’

S. 56. 3-27, Atri VI. 1-5 (which are almost the same as Vas.16 28. 10-15), [[Sankha,|Śaṅkha,]] chap. 11 (=Vas. 28. 10-15), [[Samvarta|Saṁvarta]] [[227 228,|227-228,]] Baud. Dh. S. IV. 2. 4-5, IV. 3. 8, IV. 4. 2-5, Yāj. [[IJI.|III.]] 302-305, prescribe several Vedic hymns or single Vedic verses or prose passages as specially efficacious for removing all sins. So much importance and such occult powers came to be attached to the words of the [[Rgveda|Ṛgveda]] that the [[Rgvidhāna|Ṛgvidhāna]] of [[Saunaka|Śaunaka]] (which is later than Manu) concerns itself with prescribing numerous hymns of the [[Rgveda|Ṛgveda]] for the removal of various diseases, of sins, for the conquest of enemies.

The Sāma. Br. I. 5. 2 says20 generally that when no [[parti cular|particular]] hymns are prescribed for the removal of sins Vedic mantras are powerful and become purifiers if accompanied by tapas and the Sāmans may be repeated for the same purpose from ten times as the minimum to 100 times as [[prāyagcitta, Gautama|prāyaścitta. Gautama]] 19. 13 prescribes the kind of food on which one should subsist when one is engaged in japa [[viz,|viz.]] one was to live on milk alone, to eat vegetables only, to eat fruits only, to live on barley gruel prepared from a handful of grain, to eat gold21, to eat clarified butter, and to drink soma and so forth. Gautama 19. 14 further lays down that all mountains, all rivers, holy lakes, places of pilgrimage, the dwellings of sages ([[reis).|(ṛṣis),]] [[cowpans|cowpens]] and temples of the gods are places (which help to destroy sin).

As the Veda could be studied in and after the [[sutra|sūtra]] period only by the male members of the first three classes, the japa of Vedic texts for the removal of sins was not possible for [[sūdras;|śūdras;]] therefore, the Mit. (on Yāj. III. 262) says that although the [[sūdra|śūdra]] (and women and those born of pratiloma marriages also) has no adhikāra (power or capacity or authority) for the recitation of [[Gāyatri|Gāyatrī]] and other Vedic mantras, still the [[sūdra|śūdra]] and women after revolving in the mind the name of the deity put in the dative case can engage in japa with the word [[namah|namaḥ]] (i. e. he can only utter the words ’namo [[namah’|namaḥ’]] and not om &c) [[P50]]as stated by Gaut. Dh. S. X. 66-67 and Yāj. I. [[121117.|121.]]22 When Manu IV. 80 says ’let him not explain to a [[sūdra|śūdra]] the sacred law (dharma) nor [[imposé|impose]] upon him a vrata’ all that is meant is, according to the Mit. on Yāj. III. 262, that it applies only to a [[sūdra|śūdra]] who does not wait upon or serve higher [[varpas.|varṇas.]] Similarly, when Gaut. IV. 20 says that those born in the inverse order are outside the pale of the sacred law, the meaning is that [[samskāras|saṁskāras]] like upanayana are not to be performed on them. The mystic syllable ‘om’ is said by [[Ap.|Āp.]] Dh. S. (I. 4. 13. 6) to be the door to heaven and was to be employed before the recitation of any Vedic passage. The Yogasūtra (I. 27) avers that om (called praṇava) is expressive of the idea of God and that the repetition of om and revolving the meaning of it in the mind lead to concentration.23

Not only were certain Vedic hymns and passages [[recom mended|recommended]] for recitation with the object of removing sins some works, particularly the [[Purānas,|Purāṇas,]] proposed an easy way for all viz. that the remembrance of God [[Nārāyana|Nārāyaṇa]] (or Hari or [[Krsna)|Kṛṣṇa)]] removed all sins. The Brahmapurāṇa (chap. 176) contains a stotra (hymn of praise) of [[Visnu,|Viṣṇu,]] which when recited is said to free a man from all sins (mental, vocal or bodily). The Pr. V. p. 31 quotes a verse from the [[Bhavisyapuranal18a|Bhaviṣyapurāṇa]]24 and three verses from the [[Visnupurāṇa|Viṣṇupurāṇa]] as follows:—’even a great sin is removed by (the resolve of) not doing it again, by gifts, by confessing it to others, and by meditation (on [[Visnu)|Viṣṇu)]] and not only by prāyaścittas’ ([[Bhavisya);|Bhaviṣya);]] ‘of all the expiations (for sins declared by the sages) such as tapas ([[Candrāyana|Cāndrāyaṇa]] and [[others )|others)]] and other actions (japa, homa, [[gifts )|gifts)]] the meditation on [[P51]][[Krsna|Kṛṣṇa]] is the highest. If a man remembers [[Nārāyana|Nārāyaṇa]] in the morning, at night, at twilight, at midday and the like, he at once secures the removal of sin’ ([[Visnupurāṇa).|Viṣṇupurāṇa).]] The [[Brahma purāṇa|Brahmapurāṇa]]25 (216. 87-88) gives expression to a popular belief as follows:—“Men, even after committing sins many times through folly, do not go to Hell if they prostrate themselves before Hari who destroys all sins. Those men also who always remember Janārdana [[eyon|even]] by roguery go to the world of [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] after death.” The [[Visnupurāṇa|Viṣṇupurāṇa]] affirms that those who contemplate upon the mantra of twelve letters (viz. ‘om namo bhagavate Vāsudevāya’) do not return to the cycle of births and deaths. The great power of mantras is mentioned in the [[Adiparva|Ādiparva]] (161. 14) by [[Kunti.|Kuntī.]] The [[Nrsimha-purāṇa|Nṛsiṁha-purāṇa]] in chap. 18 eulogises the mantra of eight letters (om namo [[Nārāyanāya)|Nārāyaṇāya)]] and chap. 63. 6, states “what is the use of many mantras and the performance of many vratas, when the mantra ‘om namo [[Nārāyanāya’|Nārāyaṇāya’]] is capable of securing all desired objects”. The mantra of five letters ‘[[namah|namaḥ]] [[Sivaya’|Śivāya’]] is dealt with in [[Lingapurana|Liṅgapurāṇa]] (pūrvārdha, chap. 85) and [[Saura purāṇa|Saura-purāṇa]] 65. Brahma-purāṇa speaks of vaidika mantras and [[āgamolta|āgamokta]] mantras (41. 63). The Nityācāra-paddhati (p. 67) says that it is necessary to understand the meaning of Vedic mantras in [[srauta|śrauta]] rites but in smārta rites it is not so.

Dāna (gifts). Gaut. XIX. 16 provides26 that gold, a cow, a dress, a horse, land, sesamum, clarified butter and food are the gifts (that destroy sin) and may be optionally undergone as penance if no express mention is made thereof. Vas. quotes several verses on gifts one of which (29. 16) states: “whatever sin a man distressed for livelihood commits he is purified from that by giving land even if it be as small in extent as ‘gocarma’ ‘27. [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. [[92.4|92. 4]] is to the same effect. [[Sam varta|Saṁvarta]]28 (204) prescribes that the gifts of gold, cow and land [[P52]]quickly destroy sins even if committed in former lives. [[Medha tithi|Medhātithi]] (on Manu XI. 139) remarks that the verse implies that as to sins arising from injury to living beings gifts are the [[princi pal|principal]] expiations. The subject of dāna has been dealt with at length in H. of Dh. vol. II pp. 837-888. A few further remarks may be made here. In many inscriptions and copperplate grants of lands or villages the donor usually states that the grant is made for the increase of the merit of himself and of his parents (e. g. E. I. vol. [[XI,|XI.]] p. 219 at p. 221). [[Bphaspati|Bṛhaspati]]29 as quoted in the Madanaratna (vyavahāra p. 66) expressly provides that in land-grants the king should state that he made the gift for securing merit to his parents and to himself30. The [[Rājatarangiṇi|Rājatarangiṇī]] ([[I. 143 )|I. 143)]]31 refers to the founding of vihāras (monasteries).

Upavāsa (fasting). Fasting strictly means total [[abstin ence|abstinence]] from food and drink, but generally it means light diet restricted in quantity and also as regards the nature of the articles taken. Even the Tai. S. (I. 6. 7. 3-4) refers32 to three ways of observing vrata on the days of the [[Darsapūrnamāsa-iṣti|Darśapūrṇamāsa-iṣṭi]] either by subsisting only on food available in the village or on forest food or partaking of no food at all. Gaut. 19. 11 includes upavāsa among the several means of removing sins. Tapas is also one of such means acc. to him. But in Gaut. 19. 15 as [[stat ed|stated]] above, [[Gaut,|Gaut.]] includes fasting (anāśaka) among [[’tapāmsi’.|‘tapāṁsi’.]] [[P53]]Haradatta explains [[upavāsa187|upavāsa]]33 as giving up the eating of boiled rice (bhaktatyāga) and adds that upavāsa is again included under [[tapāmsi|tapāṁsi]] to show the great importance attached to it. Haradatta notes that a predecessor of his relying on a Purāṇa passage explained [[u pavāsa|upavāsa]] in Gaut. 19. 11 as ‘restraint of senses’. In many of the [[Grhya|Gṛhya]] sūtras upavāsa means only eating light food prepared from corn usable in sacrifices (such as rice) once in the day excluding vegetables, māṣa (pulse), salt and flesh (compare Gobhila [[gr.|gṛ.]] I. [[5.26,|5. 26,]] Khādira [[gr.|gṛ.]] II. [[1.4|1. 4]] and 6, [[Kausika sūtra|Kauśika-sūtra]] I. 31-32, [[Kathakagrhya 46.2|Kāṭhakagṛhya 46. 2]]34). The [[Br.|Bṛ.]] Up. IV. 4. 22 closely associates ‘anāśaka’ (fasting) with tapas as a means to the mystic realisation of the Supreme Soul35. Jaimini (III. 8. [[9 11)|9-11)]] appears to regard fasting as tapas.36 Manu XI. 203 (=[[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. [[54.29)|54. 29)]] provides that fasting (for a day) is the penance for omitting the rites ordained by the Veda (such as the [[Darsapūrnamāsa|Darśapūrṇamāsa]] sacrifice and the performance of [[sandhya|sandhyā]] adoration) and for neglecting the special duties of a snātaka (laid down in Manu IV. 34 ff). When a person was observing a fast he had to avoid doing certain other actions. Acc. to Devala a fast becomes bereft of its good results by drinking water often, by chewing [[tâmbulat’by|tāmbūla, by]] sleep in the day-time and by sexual intercourse37; but the [[Garudapurāṇa|Garuḍapurāṇa]] I. [[128.6|128. 6]] and [[Bhavisya purana (I. 184.27)|Bhaviṣyapurāṇa (I. 184. 27)]] allow even when one is fasting the use of flowers, ornaments, rich clothes, garlands, unguents, applying collyrium to the eye and the cleansing of teeth. Dakṣa quoted by the Par. M. states that if a man observes a fast for the day and night on the day on which the sun first turns towards the [[P54]]north or south, or on the [[Vișuva|Viṣuva]] day (day of equinox), and on an eclipse of the sun or the moon and bathes he is freed from all sins.38 Manu XI. 166 (=Agnipurāṇa [[169.31)|169. 31)]] prescribes a fast for three days as a penance for theft of grass, firewood, tree, dry food (i. e. grains of rice), jaggery, clothes, hide and [[flesh,|flesh.]] The Anuśāsanaparva (106. 1) remarks39 that men of all [[varnas|varṇas]] and even Mlecchas thought highly of fasts and that chapter contains an exaggerated eulogy of fasting. Almost all religions (except Zoroastrianism) such as the Hebrew, Christian (in Lent) and Moslem (in Ramjan) emphasize the necessity of fasting as a discipline or as a preparation for penitence. The [[Bhavisya purāṇa|Bhaviṣyapurāṇa]] (I., chap. 16. 12-14) states that those who have not kindled sacred fires can please the gods by vratas, restraints, gifts and particularly by upavāsas and points out (verses 18-22) what substances (eatables) should be given up from the first to the 15th tithi in each fortnight. In the [[Satapatha|Śatapatha]] [[Brāhmana|Brāhmaṇa]] and the [[srauta|śrauta]] and [[grhya|gṛhya]] sūtras the word upavasatha is used in the sense of [[‘upavasa’|‘upavāsa’]] and is derived from ‘vas’ (to dwell) with ‘upa’. Vide. H. of Dh. vol. II. p. 1010 and n. 2276 for the [[Sat.|Śat.]] Br. The Khādira [[gr|gṛ]] II. [[1.4,|1. 4,]] [[Gobhila,|Gobhila.]] [[gr.|gṛ.]] I. [[6.2,|6. 2,]] [[Kausika-sutra|Kauśika-sūtra]] I. 31 employ the word upavasatha. [[Ap.|Āp.]] Dh. S. [[II.1.1.4-5.|II. 1. 1. 4-5.]] prescribe an upavāsa for husband and wife on parvan days and if they cannot totally abstain from food they should eat only once by day food proper for upavāsa. Aparārka p. 199, [[Smr.|Smṛ.]] C. ([[srāddha|śrāddha]] [[p.355)|p. 355)]] and [[Krtya ratnākara|Kṛtyaratnākara]] quote a verse from Vyāsa deriving the word [[upavāsa13a|upavāsa]]40. The [[Ap.|Āp.]] Dh. S. [[II, 5. 9-13,|II. 5. 9-13,]] Baud. Dh. [[II, 7.32,|II. 7. 32,]] Vas. [[6.21|6. 21]] and [[San.|Śāṅ.]] [[gr.|gṛ.]] II. [[16.5|16. 5]] have the same verse viz. ‘an āhitāgni, a draught ox and [[brahmacãrin,|brahmacārin,]] these three fulfill (their work) by eating; they cannot fulfill their duties by non-eating’. This is explained as not applying to prāyaścittas (Baud. Dh. [[S II, 7.34)|Ś II. 7. 34)]] and to fasts on Ekādaśī. The [[Santiparva|Śāntiparva]] [[323.17|323. 17]] states ‘Just as a dirty garment is afterwards cleansed by water, so to those who are heated in [[the fire of)|(the fire of)]] fasting inexhaustible happiness for a long time comes.’ Śānti 79. 18, however, says ‘merely [[emaciat ing|emaciating]] the body by fasting is not tapas, but [[ahimsā,|ahiṁsā,]] truthfulness, freedom from cruelty, restraint and compassion are tapas’.

[[P55]][[Tirthayātrā:|Tīrthayātrā:—]] Pilgrimage will be dealt with [[exhaus tively|exhaustively]] in a later section. It was believed that going on a pilgrimage and bathing in such holy rivers as the Ganges freed a man from sins. [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] Dh. S. [[(35.6)|(35. 6)]] lays down that those guilty of mahāpātakas may become pure by Aśvamedha sacrifice or by visiting all the sacred places on the earth. Devala says that a man removes the consequences of such grave sins as the murder of a [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] that he may have committed [[uninten tionally|unintentionally]] by the performance of great sacrifices or by going on a pilgrimage. Parāśara (XII. 58 ff) prescribes41 pilgrimage to see Rāma’s bridge to [[Lankā|Laṅkā]] for a murderer of a [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] who has studied the four Vedas. Devala states: ‘a man becomes freed from sin by approaching holy places and shrines, [[brāhma nas|brāhmaṇas]] that practise austerities, that all rivers that fall into the ocean, all great mountains, all shrines and all forests are holy.’ The Matsyapurāṇa [[(184.18)|(184. 18)]] says that a heap of sins as big as the mountain Meru or Mandara becomes destroyed when man reaches Avimukta (Benares). The [[Kurmapurana|Kūrmapurāṇa]]42 (pūrvārdha [[29.3)|29. 3)]] says: ‘I do not see any other expiation for destroying all sins in the Kaliyuga for all beings except Benares’. Even in the times of the Peshwas of Poona pilgrimage was prescribed for [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] murder (vide ‘Selections from Peshwa Records,’ vol. 43 p. 107) and the [[brāhmapas|brāhmaṇas]] were ordered that after the sinner performed the penance they were to dine with him and recognize that he had been purified. In an order dated [[sake|śake]] 1659 (1737 AD) the Peshwa Bajirao the first informs the Deshmukhas, Deshpandes and Mukadams that four brothers named Zende approached him with the story that their father was born when their paternal grandmother lived with one Pilaji Zende after she became a widow and that she intended to remarry that man but that had to be postponed owing to famine, that the caste meeting were prepared to take them into the [[P56]]caste and hold them pure if a Government order was issued. Then the Peshwa issued an order that they should go to Tulapur on the banks of the [[Bhimā|Bhīmā]] river and that they and their male progeny together with their wives should be restored back to the caste and complete association with them as to taking food and marriage be carried on with them from generation to [[genera tion,|generation.]]43 The Pr. Prakāśa quotes a verse from the [[Bhavisya purāṇa|Bhaviṣyapurāṇa]] about the penance for [[brāhmana|brāhmaṇa]] murder to the effect that for him who dwells to the north of the Vindhya mountain Parāśara prescribes the sight of Setubandha as a penance and then remarks that a man from the north of Vindhya has to travel 360 yojanas to reach Setubandha and that since 360 Prājāpatya penances are declared to be enough to remove the sin of brahmahatyā, it impliedly follows that each yojana towards a tīrtha is equal to one Prājāpatya. The [[Smrtyar thasāra|Smṛtyarthasāra]] (pp. [[149-150 )|149-150)]] quotes several verses from the [[Purānas|Purāṇas]] stating that gods like Brahmā, [[Visnu|Viṣṇu]] and [[Siva,|Śiva,]] great sages like [[Bhrgu,|Bhṛgu,]] [[Vasistha|Vasiṣṭha]] and Viśvāmitra, great kings like [[Hariscandra,|Hariścandra,]] Nala, Sagara obtained greatness by means of [[tirthas|tīrthas]] and that [[Pāndavas,|Pāṇḍavas,]] [[Sri|Śrī]] [[Krsna,|Kṛṣṇa,]] Nārada, Vyāsa and other sages engaged in pilgrimages for securing kingdoms and for removal of evil fortune. This subject of tīrthas will be dealt with at length in a separate section.
