By the time of smṛti, nibandha, dharma digests period, the notion of 7 pākayajñas, 7 haviryajñas and 7 somayajñas is well established. Restricting to pākayajñas, we can ask what were those 7 and whether they were always 7. 

Applying evolutionary principles, we can actually tease out the temporal layers. 

## 3 pākayajñas
I posit that āśvalāyana is the earliest layer with 3 types of pākayajñas - huta, prahuta and brahmaṇi huta. This is despite śānkhāyana claiming the total of pāka, havis and soma are 21. But it doesn't evenly divide them into 7 of each types.   

The commentator clarifies that they are  
(a) those offered in fire,  
(b) those offered as bali like sarpabali and  
(c) feeding Of brāhmaṇas. 

## 4 pAkayajnas
Coming back to śāṅkhāyana, we see the next stage in evolution - increase of the 3 kinds into 4 - huta, ahuta, prahuta, and prāśita.  
Those are "agnihotra homa" which we can take it mean as "offered in fire", as from the context, it is evident that shrauta is not meant;  
The ahuta here is the bali karma,  
prahuta is the pitṛyajña and  
the prāśita is the feeding of v1s. 

Neither of these RV sūtras clarify whether the sacrements/saṃskāras are covered in this schema or they are meant to be separate. This is layer 2 of the evolution. 

We can posit that pāraskara belongs to the same layer. pāraskara gives the identical enumeration as śāṅkhāyana (Side note- shukla YV and śāṅkhāyana go together in shrauta performance). The commentator harihara's explanation sort of tallies with śāṅkhāyana explanation.

The morning and evening homa mentioned by the commentator fits with "agnihotra" and the pratyavarohaṇa ceremony example can be thought of the counterpart of sarpabali (you come down after the fear of snakes is gone). +++(4)+++ 

## 7 pākayajñas
The next layer is bodhāyana.  
He has his own 3 - huta, prahuta, āhuta and adds śūlagava, baliharaṇa, pratyavarohaṇa, and aṣṭakā to make 7 pākayajñas. 

He brings in the saṃskāras in the first 3. Marriage and parting of the wife's hair ceremony is huta.  
Birth and shaving of Baby's hair forms prahuta.  
Sacred thread and graduation ceremony fall under āhuta.  
He understands bali as the regular vaiśvadeva.  
Spit ox sacrifice to rudra and aṣṭakā is a kind of śrāddha.  
Seasonal pratyavarohaṇa probably can be taken to include the sarpabali.  

The gist is that he sees the 7 which both sacraments and regular rituals. This can be thought of temporal layer 3. 

## gautama dharma
We get the most complicated schema in the next layer - gautama dharma. Gautama enumerates 40 saṃskāras. He includes sacraments like marriage, conception, rituals to pregnant wife, birth, sacred thread, graduation etc. separately from the 7 pākayajñas.  
In his scheme, the 7 are aṣṭakā, fortnightly śrāddha, sarpabali, offering of first fruits, āgrahāyaṇi (a full moon ritual in winter), redescending ritual, chaitri - another seasonal ritual. 

We can place Gopatha testimory between layers 3 and 4. It enumerates 7 pākayajñas with a slight twist. Its list consists of aupAsana, fortnightly homa with cooked food, offering of first fruits, bali, pitṛyajña and aṣṭakā.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, we can say that sūtras of different vedic schools had their own notion of classification of domestic rituals and this process more or less crystallized to 7 of each uber kind - pāka, havis and soma. Perhaps this is a gloss of Rig Veda mantra 10.52.4, where yajña is pancayāma, trivṛt, saptatantu - fivefold, 3 looped and 7 threaded. The symbolism of fivefold warrants another thread. /fin
