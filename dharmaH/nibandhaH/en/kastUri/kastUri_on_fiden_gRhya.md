## Intro
Thesis by Fiden called "The Codification and Mobilization of Domestic Religion from the R ̥gveda to the Gr ̥hyasūtras". 

It has lot of useful info and inferences that tally with my own independent study. The final conclusion it was building towards look like Whistler's mother painting from the movie Bean.

## Evolution
Evolution of gṛhya rituals is a topic of great interest to me. The subject material is vast as it stands today with numerous geographical variations. Applying evolutionary principles based on current surviving materials to infer precursors, relationship between shrauta and gṛhya, mutual interactions between various schools, and the position of atharvāngirasa in all these are great topics to investigate. Each one of these would take a lifetime. 

## Fiden errs
So, when the Fiden thesis landed on my way, I was hopeful that someone tackled a difficult subject. Alas, it looks like the final word on the topic isn't written. 

Fiden's RV:AV::Indra::Rudra is dubious to say the least.  
Atharvaveda being the "first textual expression of the gṛhya karmas" proposal is bass ackwards.  
Finally the charge of "brahmanas subverting by introducing Gṛhya rituals to expand client base and stay relevant in the face of urbanization and shramana encroachment" is fanciful if not libelous. 

## gRhya first
We should be able to draw a general picture. 

In evolution, one observes complex forms typically evolve from simpler ones (ignore barnacles for now. Ritual barnacles exist too). We should expect that the 3 fire shrauta system should have evolved from 1 fire prototype. Puranas exactly remember this.

We can only speculate about single fire rituals at remote past - perhaps by comparing zoroastrian rituals with vaidika and evaluate if there's a common precursor. Skipping over to the time period of gṛhya sūtra codification by various schools, we observe a variety of practices already. 

Every school has an idea of what gṛhya karmas are but they don't mutually agree on the specifics. They all say these are remembered in practice rather than directly described in the veda.

## sUtra topics
I see a threefold topical division of the gṛhyasūtras:

1. Samskāras - sacraments that admit you into the ārya community and the concomitant obligations and privileges. These span across conception, rites for protection of fetus, birth, naming ceremony, feeding first food, student hood, graduation, marriage, old age and death.
2. Upāsanā - The regular worship of Gods and Fathers. Daily, fortnightly, seasonal, yearly. There's an overlap with shrauta rituals here.
3. "Atharvanic": admittedly a misnomer and a label of convenience. Those rituals dealing with shānti, puṣṭi, omens and Portents, kāmyas like winning court case and in one instance (Ashvalayana) even royal rituals. These are the mainstay of the Atharvan. The topical overlap is noted by many indologists including Fiden.

## Classification variations
The way the the sūtras classify the topics itself varies from veda to veda.

RV sutras classify the rites into huta, ahuta, prahuta and brahmaņihuta. Ashvalāyana and śāṅkhāyana don't agree what falls under what category. There's a general agreement of 7 pākayajñas.  

Bodhāyana takes the first 3 and adds individual rites to arrive at 7.

## Fire origin
Where's the fire coming from?
Typically, it's the wedding fire. It's brought in a vessel along with the bride, installed in the northeast corner of the house and sthālīpāka is offered. This fire is preserved then onwards and morning/evening aupāsana, fortnightly sthālīpāka are regularly offered. 

Apastamba simply says if the fire is lost while the bride is coming to the husband's household, generate a new one by friction or get a fire from a śrotriya's house. This is the simplest form. 

Śāṅkhāyana and āśvalāyana mention different occasions to kindle the household fire apart from wedding. The fire in which the graduating student puts the last piece of wood, the division of inheritance, death of the eldest householder.

It's not at all clear what will a graduate do with gṛhya fire before marriage, as most of the rituals are ineligible for him. 

Hiranyakeshin explicitly mentions that the upanayana fire is preserved by the student, all the vedavratas are performed in it, preserved after graduation and used as the wedding fire. 

He also adds that "according to some", the sacraments of wife and children are done in this fire, though in his opinion, upanayana fire is kindled with laukika fire.

## sthAlIpAka
Āpastamba is cryptic that the fortnightly sthālīpāka is a stand-in for the śrauta darśapūrņamāsa and the oblations are to be inserted between the first one and sviṣṭakṛt. 

## AdhAna
So, if the fortnightly sthālīpāka is equivalent to shrauta iṣṭi, the setting up of the gṛhya fire would need it's ādhāna equivalent. You see the RV sūtras alluding to this - mention the handing over of araņis by the adhvaryu to the yajamāna, just like the shrauta equivalent. 

Gobhila procedure is a full on mimic of the shrauta equivalent- that includes the couple staying awake the night before regaling themselves, the priests ritually kindling the fire, upasthāna and oblations. 

Pāraskara bluntly calls the procedure "āvasathya installation" and it is as per chatush-prāshya procedure - all part of shrauta lore.

So, we see a "complexification" from using the wedding fire for regular upāsana to a solemn installation ritual to even start your upāsana. This is the next stage in evolution.

## Maintenance
How long is the fire kept? The sūtras are aware that not everyone sets up the 3 fires. Some important rituals like āgrayaņa need to be necessarily performed at least on single fire. 

## shrautification
Gṛhyasūtras gives a paśukalpa for śrāddha and shūlagava. So concessions are made to accommodate "some" shrauta rituals in single fire. 

It reaches a climax with bodhāyana - "śrautoktāni smārte kartavyāni" - whatever is mentioned in shrauta is to be performed in the smārta (by anāhitāgnis). His process for converting an iṣţi into a sthālīpāka matches descriptions in śāṅkhāyana and āśvalāyana.  

But the RV sūtra commentators seem to limit the applicability to kāmya rites.+++(5)+++ 

We should say this is an evolutionary dead end as there are no generic prayoga manuals prescribing shrauta obligatory performances as pākayajñas.

The concession should have arrived too late and most probably the lay dvijas switched to purānic mode of worship with purohitas performing samskaras by creating fire on-demand.

Fire as a medium of regular upāsanā unfortunately dwindled into a micro-minority.
