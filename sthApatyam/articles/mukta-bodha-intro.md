## Pratiṣṭhā
Apart from a short section in Varāhamihira’s Bṛhatsaṃhitā and several chapters in the Matsyapurāṇa, the oldest surviving discussions of the making of iconic and aniconic foci for worship, urban design and infrastructure, the design of temples, and the awakening and installation of images in a ritual setting are contained within the tantras. Unlike later works on Vāstu, texts such as the Devyāmata of the Niśvāsa, the Piṅgalāmata, the Mayasaṃgraha, the Bṛhatkālottara, and the Mohacūḍoṭṭara are frequently addressed not only to the ācārya himself, but also to the various categories of artisans whom he both trains and initiates. 

Key portions of these texts present the reader with modes of Śaiva and Śākta pedagogy and theology as they were presented to audiences not trained in classical Indian philosophy. Not only do these texts contain some of our most detailed accounts of pre-modern material culture, such as procedures for the making of liṅgas with and without faces, as well as mūrtis in a variety of media, they also present some of the earliest and most complete accounts of festival culture and non-purāṇic theorization of sacred landscapes.
